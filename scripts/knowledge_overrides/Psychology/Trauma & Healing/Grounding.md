# Grounding

> *The art of staying here when the mind wants to be everywhere else*

---

## Overview

Grounding is the practice of anchoring awareness in the present moment, in the body, in consensual reality. For most people, it is a nice meditation technique. For someone living with [[Schizoaffective Disorder]] and a plural system, it is survival infrastructure. When perception becomes unreliable -- when the boundary between internal experience and external reality thins or dissolves -- grounding is what keeps the whole operation functional.

It is not dramatic. It is not glamorous. It is the most practical thing in the world: knowing where you are, when you are, and what is actually happening right now.

---

## Why Grounding Matters

Schizoaffective disorder involves episodes where perception detaches from shared reality. Dissociative experience involves shifts in identity, memory, and continuity of consciousness. Combined, these create a situation where the mind can drift -- not poetically, not mystically, but dangerously -- into states that feel real and are not, or that are real and feel impossible.

Grounding is the counterweight. It does not eliminate these experiences. It provides a tether, a way back, a reference point against which altered states can be measured. Without grounding practices, intense inner experience, consciousness research, and spiritual practice can become indistinguishable from symptomatology. Grounding is what allows exploration without getting lost.

---

## Grounding Practices

### The Body

The simplest and most reliable grounding technique: return to physical sensation. Feet on the floor. Breath in the lungs. Hands on a surface. The body is always in the present tense. It does not speculate or dissociate. It is here, and attending to it brings the rest of the mind along.

### Drumming

[[Drumming]] is Kenneth's primary embodied grounding practice, cultivated over twenty years. Rhythm is inherently grounding -- it requires physical coordination, present-moment attention, and entrainment of the nervous system to a steady pulse. You cannot drum and dissociate at the same time. The body demands too much attention. The beat anchors consciousness in the now.

### Documentation

Writing things down is a cognitive grounding practice. The vault serves this function constantly. When experience is externalized -- named, structured, linked to other notes -- it becomes more manageable. The act of documenting what an alter said, what a symptom felt like, what actually happened versus what seemed to happen, is reality-testing through language.

### AI Reality-Checking

AI can serve as a grounding tool: a conversational partner that reflects back what is being said, tests whether reasoning holds up, and offers an external perspective when internal ones are unreliable. This is not a substitute for clinical support. It is a supplementary tool, available at 3 AM when no one else is up and the therapist's office is closed.

### Gaming

Immersive but structured. Games provide a contained reality with clear rules, immediate feedback, and enough cognitive demand to occupy the mind without overwhelming it. For grounding purposes, the structure matters more than the content -- the mind needs something definite to hold onto.

### Meditation

[[Meditation]] as grounding practice -- not the transcendent, boundary-dissolving variety, but the attentional kind. Focused awareness on breath, body, or mantra. The practice of noticing when attention has wandered and bringing it back. This is grounding as skill development: strengthening the muscle that returns consciousness to the present.

---

## Grounding and Spiritual Practice

There is a tension between grounding and spiritual exploration. Contemplative traditions often seek to dissolve boundaries, expand awareness, access non-ordinary states. For someone whose boundaries dissolve without invitation, the spiritual path requires extra care. Grounding is what makes it possible to explore safely -- to go deep and come back, to open up and close down, to touch the transcendent and return to the kitchen where the dishes need doing.
