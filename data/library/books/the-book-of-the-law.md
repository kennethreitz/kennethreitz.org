# The Book of the Law

> *Handle with care*

*Liber AL vel Legis* is the foundational scripture of Thelema, the spiritual system Aleister Crowley built around it. He claimed not to have written it but to have transcribed it over three days in Cairo in April 1904, dictated, by his account, by a praeternatural intelligence named Aiwass speaking through his wife Rose, who had begun making uncanny pronouncements about the god Horus. Whatever one makes of that origin story, Crowley spent the rest of his life treating the slim text as the cornerstone of a new aeon and forbidding anyone, including himself, from altering a single letter of it.

The book is divided into three chapters, each voiced by an Egyptian deity who governs it: Nuit, the infinite night sky and the source of all possibility; Hadit, the infinitely small point of consciousness at the center; and Ra-Hoor-Khuit, the crowned and conquering child whose violent, ecstatic energy announces the "Aeon of Horus." From this triad come the two famous formulae that compress the whole system: *"Do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law"* and its answering line, *"Love is the law, love under will."*

Provocative and dangerous in the way that unstable compounds are dangerous. "Do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law" is often misread as license. It's actually about discovering your true will, which turns out to be much harder and less fun than doing whatever you want.

Kenneth has mostly outgrown Crowley's framework, but this was an important waypoint. You don't get to where you're going without passing through where you've been.
