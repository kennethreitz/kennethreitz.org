# No Bad Parts

> *Healing trauma and restoring wholeness with the Internal Family Systems model*

The book that carried [Internal Family Systems](/knowledge/psychology/internal-family-systems) out of the clinic and into everyone else's hands. Schwartz's argument in full: the mono-mind is a myth, every psyche is a family of parts, and the parts doing the most damage are the ones carrying the most pain. No part is bad; some are burdened. Beneath them all sits the [Self](/knowledge/psychology/internal-family-systems/self-ifs), calm, curious, compassionate, unbroken by anything that happened, and the book is essentially a course in letting it lead, complete with exercises for meeting your own protectors and exiles directly.

For this vault the title is nearly redundant: [Plurality](/system-777/plurality) lived here, treated with respect, long before Schwartz's manifesto said the manyness of mind is normal and nobody inside needs to go away. But it matters that the most evidence-backed parts therapy in the field put that ethic in print for a general audience. The clinical world catching up to the lived one, forty years of sessions distilled into a sentence the plural community had already been saying.
