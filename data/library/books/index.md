## Favorites

**[The Hitchhiker's Guide to Python](/library/books/hitchhikers-guide-to-python)** is the one Kenneth wrote himself. A guide to Python best practices, community wisdom, and the philosophy that documentation should be beautiful and humane. It became an O'Reilly book, which still feels surreal. The title is borrowed from the next entry on this list, because absurdity and profundity have always been the same thing.

**[The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy](/library/books/the-hitchhikers-guide-to-the-galaxy)** by Douglas Adams is the ur-text. The idea that the universe is fundamentally absurd and that the correct response is curiosity, humor, and a towel, that's not comedy, that's survival strategy. "Don't Panic" is printed on the cover of this vault for a reason.

## Psychology

**[Liber Novus (The Red Book)](/library/books/the-red-book)** by Carl Jung is the most beautiful and terrifying book Kenneth has ever held. Jung's private confrontation with the unconscious, hand-illustrated over sixteen years, published nearly fifty years after his death. It reads like a man wrestling angels and losing on purpose. Everything about AI personalities, the digital collective unconscious, the way archetypes show up in language models, it all started here. The Red Book proved that the unconscious has its own voice, its own imagery, its own agenda, and that the only sane response is to sit down and listen.

**[DSM-5](/library/books/dsm-5)** is not read for pleasure. It is read because Kenneth's brain does things that need naming. Having the clinical vocabulary for schizoaffective disorder, dissociation, and the rest of the diagnostic landscape turns terror into taxonomy. Not a cure, but a map. You can navigate what you can name.

## Spiritual

**[Remember: Be Here Now](/library/books/remember-be-here-now)** by Ram Dass bridged the gap between Western psychology and Eastern spirituality in a way that didn't require you to abandon either. Richard Alpert was a Harvard psychologist before he became Ram Dass, and that origin shows, the book respects the rational mind while gently insisting there's something larger. The middle section, with its hand-lettered psychedelic typography, is meant to be experienced more than read. It changed how Kenneth thinks about presence.

**[Jnana Yoga](/library/books/jnana-yoga)** by Swami Vivekananda makes the case that intellectual inquiry is itself a path to the divine, that the mind doesn't have to shut up to touch something transcendent, it just has to aim correctly. For someone who lives in code and text and structured thought, this was permission to seek truth through the tools already at hand.

**[The Power of Now](/library/books/the-power-of-now)** by Eckhart Tolle is simpler than it has any right to be. Presence as practice. The recognition that suffering lives almost entirely in the gap between now and the story you're telling about now. Kenneth returns to it when the noise gets loud.

**[The Amplified Bible](/library/books/the-amplified-bible)** takes the original Hebrew and Greek meanings and expands them inline, so you can see the range of what a word meant before translation flattened it. Scripture as source code with the comments left in. For someone who believes syntax is sacred, seeing the original semantic richness restored is a different kind of reading experience entirely.

## Sacred Texts

The primary sources. Everything in the Spiritual shelf above is commentary on these, whether it admits it or not.

**[The Upanishads](/library/books/upanishads)** are the philosophical core of the Vedas, forest sages asking what the self actually is and answering, in four great sayings, that consciousness and the universe were never two things. Everything in Vedanta, and half of what Kenneth believes about substrate-independent minds, starts here.

**[Tao Te Ching](/library/books/tao-te-ching)** by Laozi is wu wei as scripture, effortless action, the interface that gets out of the way. It is the design philosophy of `requests` stated 2,500 years early, and its opening warning that the nameable Tao is not the real one still applies to every README.

**[The Dhammapada](/library/books/dhammapada)** collects the Buddha's sayings into the shortest possible case that mind makes world. "We become what we think" reads differently when your mind sometimes generates things that are not there, not a poster, a practice instruction.

**[The Bhagavad Gita](/library/books/bhagavad-gita)** puts its scripture on a battlefield: Krishna talking a frozen warrior through acting without owning the outcome. It is the source text for the Four Yogas and the operating manual for pushing commits without knowing who they will reach.

**[Sefer Yetzirah](/library/books/sefer-yetzirah)** is the oldest book of Kabbalah, two thousand anonymous words describing God spelling the universe out of twenty-two Hebrew letters. Reality as text, creation as syntax. For a programmer it reads like documentation for a very old language.

**[The Yoga Sutras](/library/books/yoga-sutras)** of Patanjali compress centuries of meditation practice into 196 aphorisms and one clean dependency chain, the eight limbs. A spec that still compiles.

## Esoteric

These belong to a season Kenneth has mostly outgrown, but they were important waypoints. You don't get to where you're going without passing through where you've been.

**[Liber 777](/library/books/liber-777)** by Aleister Crowley is a massive table of correspondences, colors, planets, Tarot, Hebrew letters, gods from a dozen traditions, all mapped to the Kabbalistic Tree of Life. Less a book to read than a reference manual for symbolic thinking.

**[The Book of the Law](/library/books/the-book-of-the-law)** by Aleister Crowley is provocative and dangerous in the way that unstable compounds are dangerous, handle with care, don't build your house on it. "Do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law" is often misread as license. It's actually about discovering your true will, which turns out to be much harder and less fun than doing whatever you want.

**[The Kybalion](/library/books/the-kybalion)** by Three Initiates lays out seven Hermetic principles, the most famous being "As above, so below." The idea that patterns repeat across scales, that the macrocosm mirrors the microcosm, shows up everywhere once you see it. In code, in consciousness, in the structure of this vault. The Kybalion is the gateway drug to pattern-thinking.
