# Tao Te Ching

> *"The Tao that can be told is not the eternal Tao."*

Eighty-one brief chapters attributed to Laozi, a possibly legendary archivist said to have written them down before vanishing westward through a mountain pass. It is the shortest of the great scriptures and the hardest to exhaust, a manual for moving with the grain of reality instead of against it, with water as its favorite teacher: soft, yielding, and capable of carving canyons.

Wu wei: effortless action, doing by not forcing, is the design philosophy of `requests` stated 2,500 years early. The best interface is the one that gets out of the way; the best code disappears into the task. [For Humans](/knowledge/philosophy/for-humans) is the Tao Te Ching applied to APIs. The book's opening line, that the nameable Tao is not the real one, is also the vault's standing caution about [The Un-Englishable](/essays/2026-06-19-the_un_englishable), the most important things resist their own documentation.
