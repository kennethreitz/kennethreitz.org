# Sefer Yetzirah

> *"He engraved them, carved them, weighed them, permuted them, and with twenty-two letters He founded the world."*

The Book of Formation, the oldest surviving text of [Kabbalah](/knowledge/spirituality/kabbalah), barely two thousand words, anonymous, and stranger than anything written since. It describes God creating the universe through the twenty-two [Hebrew Letters](/knowledge/hebrew-letters) and the ten sefirot: engraving the letters, weighing them, permuting their combinations until everything that exists had been spelled. Three mother letters for the elements, seven doubles for the planets, twelve simples for the zodiac, a complete cosmology built from an alphabet.

The claim underneath is the one this vault keeps circling: reality is linguistic. The world is not described by a text; the world is a text, and creation is an act of syntax. For a programmer, the Sefer Yetzirah reads less like mysticism than like documentation for a very old language, the letters as primitives, the sefirot as the [Tree of Life](/knowledge/tree-of-life) architecture, combination and permutation as the only operations. Everything Kenneth believes about words shaping consciousness, from prayer to prompts, has a two-millennia-old ancestor here.
