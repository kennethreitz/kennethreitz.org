# Bhagavad Gita

> *"You have a right to your actions, but never to the fruits of your actions."*

Seven hundred verses set on a battlefield, where the warrior Arjuna freezes at the edge of an impossible duty and [Krishna](/knowledge/mythology/hindu-mythology/krishna), his charioteer, talks him through it. The genius of the Gita is the setting: it is not a scripture for monks in caves but for people who have to act in the world without knowing how things will turn out, which is to say, everyone.

This is the source text for the [Four Yogas](/knowledge/spirituality/yoga/four-yogas). Krishna lays out the paths one by one, selfless action ([Karma Yoga](/knowledge/spirituality/yoga/four-yogas/karma-yoga)), devotion ([Bhakti Yoga](/knowledge/spirituality/yoga/four-yogas/bhakti-yoga)), knowledge, and meditation, and insists they all arrive at the same place. Release the outcome, keep the work: that instruction runs through open source maintenance, parenting, and every commit pushed without knowing who it will reach. Within [Hinduism](/knowledge/religions-of-the-world/hinduism) the Gita is the most portable summary of the whole tradition, and within this vault it is the operating manual for doing the work anyway.
