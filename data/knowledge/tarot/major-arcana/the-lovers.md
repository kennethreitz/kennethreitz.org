# The Lovers  ·  VI

> *To choose one thing is to marry it and bury all the others.*

---

## Imagery

A naked man and woman stand in a green landscape beneath a great winged angel who spreads his arms in blessing, radiant against a golden sun. Behind the woman rises the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, a serpent coiling its trunk; behind the man burns the tree of flames, twelve tongues of fire. The woman looks up toward the angel, the man looks toward the woman — a chain of gazes rising from earth to heaven. A mountain stands between them, distant and singular. The scene is Eden before or after the choice: union, temptation, and the threshold of conscious decision all at once.

## Upright

The Lovers is union and the choice that makes union real. On its surface it is romance and partnership; beneath, it is the deeper alchemy of joining opposites into a whole, and the free, values-driven decision that seals such a joining. Upright, the card counsels alignment — acting from what you truly love rather than what you merely want. Where [The Hierophant](/knowledge/tarot/major-arcana/the-hierophant) offered the inherited answer, the Lovers demands a personal one. To draw it is to stand at a choice that will define you.

## Reversed

Reversed, union fractures or choice is evaded. The card can mean discord, misalignment of values, temptation followed against one’s better knowing, or the refusal to commit to anything for fear of losing everything else. Inverted, it may reveal a relationship built on projection rather than sight, or a decision made from outside pressure rather than inner truth — the self divided against itself.

## Archetype

The Lovers stages the meeting of the ego with its contrasexual soul-image — the Anima or Animus — and the beginning of their conscious relationship. Jung saw romantic love as one of the great vehicles of projection, in which we first meet our own unlived depths in the face of another. The card also marks the emergence of genuine moral choice: the moment the psyche stops merely obeying and begins to author itself. The sword of Zayin is discrimination, the cut that turns fusion into relationship.

## Mythological Echoes

The card is Eden — Adam and Eve beneath the tree, the serpent, the angel with the flaming sword who guards the gate of return. It is the sacred marriage of every mythology, the hieros gamos in which sky weds earth. It echoes Eros and Psyche, whose love survives betrayal and trial; and the myth of the divided beings in Plato’s Symposium, each half seeking the other across the whole of a life.

## In Practice

When the Lovers appears, the reading turns toward relationship and, beneath it, toward a choice of values. Journalling prompt: what are you actually choosing here, and does the choice come from love or from fear? What must you set down in order to say a wholehearted yes?
