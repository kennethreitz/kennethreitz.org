# The Moon  ·  XVIII

> *Not everything in the dark is a threat, but everything in the dark asks to be walked through.*

---

## Imagery

A full moon hangs in a troubled sky, its face both radiant and sorrowful, dripping fifteen Yod-shaped drops of dew. Below, a path runs straight between two grey towers into distant hills. A dog and a wolf lift their heads and howl at the light — the tamed and the wild aspects of the animal mind. From a still pool in the foreground a crayfish crawls up onto the land, a primitive creature emerging from the deep. There are no human figures. Everything is uncertain, half-lit, dreamlike. The card is the landscape of the unconscious at night, where the path is real but the shapes beside it cannot be trusted.

## Upright

The Moon is illusion, the unconscious, and the necessary passage through fear and confusion. Where [The Star](/knowledge/tarot/major-arcana/the-star) gave clear serene light, the Moon gives reflected, shifting light in which nothing is quite what it seems. Upright, the card counsels courage in uncertainty — the willingness to walk the path even when dreams, anxieties, and half-truths crowd its edges. It speaks of intuition and the deep material rising, and of not mistaking every shadow for a monster. To draw it is to be told the way forward runs through the dark, not around it.

## Reversed

Reversed, the mist begins to clear, or it thickens into delusion. The card can mean the lifting of confusion, a fear seen through and released, a truth emerging from the murk. Inverted, it may also mean the opposite — self-deception deepening, anxiety spiralling, intuition drowned by fantasy. It marks the point where one either learns to read the moonlit landscape or gets lost in it entirely.

## Archetype

The Moon is the deep Collective Unconscious itself, met at night — the realm of instinct, dream, and the archaic images that rise when the sun of consciousness sets. The crayfish crawling from the pool is the primordial content emerging into awareness; the dog and wolf are the domesticated and feral faces of the psyche howling at what they cannot name. In [Individuation](/knowledge/psychology/jungian/individuation) this is the dark night of the soul, the disorienting passage through one’s own depths that cannot be reasoned away, only traversed.

## Mythological Echoes

She is Hecate at the crossroads, Selene and Artemis of the night; the lunar goddess whose light rules tides and madness alike. She echoes every night-sea journey — the hero swallowed by the deep, travelling through the belly of the dark toward a dawn on the far side. The two towers are the gates of the underworld, the threshold between the known day-world and the uncharted country of dream through which the soul must pass.

## In Practice

When the Moon appears, the reading turns toward the unconscious, uncertainty, and the courage to keep walking through the dark. Journalling prompt: what fear are you treating as fact? What is your intuition telling you beneath the anxiety, and can you tell the two apart? Where is the path asking you to trust it even though you cannot see the end?
