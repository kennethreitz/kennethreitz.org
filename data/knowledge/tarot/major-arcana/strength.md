# Strength  ·  VIII

> *The lion is not defeated; it is befriended.*

---

## Imagery

A woman in a white gown, crowned with flowers and girdled with roses, bends gently over a great lion and closes its jaws with her bare hands — not by force, but with a tenderness the beast accepts. Above her head floats the lemniscate, the same infinity that crowned the Magician, marking her power as spiritual rather than muscular. The landscape is soft and golden, the mood serene. There is no strain in her face. The card’s whole meaning lives in that absence of strain: the mastery of raw instinct through love and patience rather than domination.

## Upright

Strength is fortitude of the quiet kind — courage, patience, and the gentle command of one’s own animal nature. Where [The Chariot](/knowledge/tarot/major-arcana/the-chariot) mastered opposing forces by force of will, Strength masters them by acceptance and warmth. Upright, the card counsels the soft power that outlasts the hard: composure under pressure, compassion toward one’s own wildness, the confidence that does not need to prove itself. To draw it is to be reminded that the lion is tamed by the hand that does not fear it.

## Reversed

Reversed, the inner beast slips its handler. The card can mean self-doubt, raw emotion overwhelming reason, or the brittle overcompensation of forced toughness. Inverted, it may reveal a person at war with their own instincts — repressing the lion rather than befriending it — or one whose fear has hardened into aggression. The gentleness is lost, and with it the true strength.

## Archetype

Strength depicts the conscious self in loving relationship with the instinctual, animal layers of the psyche — a reconciliation with The Shadow achieved not by suppression but by integration. Jung insisted that the goal was never to conquer the shadow but to bring it into conscious partnership; the lion is the disowned vitality that becomes an ally once it is met without fear. The woman is the ego matured into the capacity to hold its own darkness with tenderness.

## Mythological Echoes

She echoes the tamers of beasts across the world — the Mistress of Animals of the ancient Aegean, the potnia theron flanked by lions; Cyrene wrestling the lion bare-handed; the saints who calmed wild things by their peace alone. The card inverts the Herculean labour: where the hero strangled the Nemean lion by main force, here the lion is gentled, and the deeper strength is shown to be the one that need not kill.

## In Practice

When Strength appears, the reading turns toward patience, courage, and the gentle handling of one’s own intensity. Journalling prompt: what wild or shameful part of you have you been trying to cage, and what would it mean to place a calm hand on its jaw instead? Where is your real strength quieter than you thought?
