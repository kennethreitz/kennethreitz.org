# Seven of Cups

> *Seven bright doors, and behind most of them, only more fog.*

---

## Imagery

A dark silhouette stands before seven cups that hover in a cloud, each brimming with a different vision: a face, a shrouded and glowing figure, a serpent, a castle, jewels, a wreath of victory, and a dragon. The figure raises hands in wonder or bewilderment, unable to tell which offering is real and which is phantom. The whole scene floats in unreality, a dreaming intoxication where desire projects itself into the air and multiplies. Nothing here has weight yet; everything is possibility, and therefore nothing is chosen.

## Upright

The Seven of Cups is illusion, fantasy, the seductive paralysis of too many choices. It marks wishful thinking, the fog of desire that mistakes glittering options for real ones, and the temptation to live among daydreams rather than commit to a single path. Some cups hold genuine treasure; others hold serpents and shrouded snares. The card asks for discernment — the difficult work of telling vision from delusion, of grounding the imagination before it dissipates entirely into smoke.

## Reversed

Inverted, the fog begins to lift. This can be clarity arriving at last, the return of focus and the decision to pursue one real cup rather than seven imagined ones. Alternatively it deepens the disorder — escapism hardening into addiction, the willful preference for fantasy over a reality one refuses to face. The reversal turns on whether you are waking from the dream or burrowing further into it, whether the cups are being sorted or simply multiplied.

## Archetype

This is the realm of projection, where the unconscious throws its contents onto the screen of the world and the ego mistakes them for objective fact. Jung warned that unexamined fantasy governs us precisely because we do not recognize it as ours. The seven cups are complexes, desires, and fears made luminous and confusing. Discernment — withdrawing projections, learning which longing is soul and which is glamour — is the alchemical labor the card demands on the road to [Individuation](/knowledge/psychology/jungian/individuation).

## Mythological Echoes

The floating visions recall the enchantments of Circe and the lotus-eaters, whose sweetness dissolved the will to go home. They echo maya, the Vedic veil of appearance that mistakes the shimmering many for the real One — a theme central to [Vedanta](/knowledge/spirituality/vedanta). The card summons every myth of the sorcerer’s illusion, the fairy gold that turns to leaves at dawn, the tempting mirage in the desert that recedes as one approaches.

## In Practice

When this card appears, name your options honestly and ask which are real. Journal on a fantasy you have been feeding — and what single, grounded step would test whether it holds water.
