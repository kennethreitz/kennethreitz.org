# Two of Cups

> *Two vessels lifted, and between them a thing neither could hold alone.*

---

## Imagery

A man and a woman stand facing each other, each raising a cup, exchanging a pledge across the small distance between them. Above and between their gazes rises the caduceus of Hermes, its twin serpents coiled around a winged staff, crowned by the red lion’s head of Leo — a symbol of desire raised to something higher. The ground beneath them is green and level, a meeting of equals. Everything in the image bends toward that central axis where two become a shared field.

## Upright

This is the card of union — love, partnership, mutual recognition, the alchemy of two natures meeting as peers. Where the [Ace of Cups](/knowledge/tarot/minor-arcana/ace-of-cups) is love as pure source, the Two is love made relational, feeling that has found its answering other. It speaks of attraction and reconciliation, of contracts sealed with the heart rather than the pen, of the moment when connection becomes covenant. It carries the promise of the [The Lovers](/knowledge/tarot/major-arcana/the-lovers) at an intimate, human scale.

## Reversed

Inverted, the exchange falters. This is imbalance, a partnership where one gives and the other only takes, or the rupture of a bond once mutual. It can mark miscommunication, broken trust, or the disharmony that creeps in when two people stop truly seeing each other. Sometimes it points inward — a failure of self-love that no other person can supply, the cups tilting until the water runs out between them.

## Archetype

Jung placed the coniunctio — the sacred marriage of opposites — at the heart of the psyche’s work. The Two of Cups is its human prelude: the meeting of masculine and feminine, conscious and unconscious, self and other, as a mutual and voluntary exchange. It is projection made honest, the anima and Animus recognized across the space between two people. In relationship we glimpse the inner union that [Individuation](/knowledge/psychology/jungian/individuation) finally asks us to make within.

## Mythological Echoes

The caduceus names Hermes, messenger and reconciler, the god who moves between worlds to make exchange possible. The image echoes every marriage of gods — the hieros gamos of Greek ritual, the wedding of heaven and earth. It recalls Eros and Psyche, love that must be recognized face to face to survive, and the countless myths in which a shared cup binds two fates into one.

## In Practice

When this card appears, attend to a specific bond. Is the exchange mutual? Journal on where you meet another as a true equal — and where you are still waiting to be seen.
