# Queen of Wands

> *She keeps a black cat and a sunflower on the same throne, and is entirely unafraid of either.*

---

## Imagery

A queen sits on a stone throne carved with lions and sunflowers, facing forward with open, confident ease. She holds a leafing wand in one hand and a large sunflower in the other, both blooming. Before her, at her feet, sits a black cat gazing directly out of the card. Behind her rise more sunflowers against a warm yellow sky, and the lions on her throne echo the fire sign of Leo. Her posture is relaxed but commanding, her legs slightly apart, entirely at home in her power. The feeling is radiant self-possession: warmth and magnetism married to an unspoken depth.

## Upright

The Queen of Wands is fire made warm, mature, and magnetic — confidence without insecurity, charisma rooted in genuine self-knowledge. She is passionate, courageous, and generous with her warmth, drawing people toward her light without needing to chase them. The sunflower is her openness; the black cat is her familiarity with the shadow, the instinctual and hidden. She does not fear her own depths, and that fearlessness is the source of her allure. This card speaks of vibrant creativity, natural leadership, and sexual and social vitality. Its invitation is to inhabit your fire fully, to be warm without apology.

## Reversed

Reversed, the warmth turns brittle or cold. Confidence curdles into insecurity that overcompensates — the demand for attention, jealousy, a temper that scorches. Or the fire withdraws entirely, the radiant self dimming into self-doubt and the fear of taking up space. The shadow is the queen who has lost touch with her own light and now either grasps at others' regard or hides her flame altogether, the black cat of the instincts turned feral or exiled.

## Archetype

This is the mature feminine in her fiery aspect — the anima who has integrated her instinct and her shadow, symbolised perfectly by the black cat she keeps close rather than banishes. Jung would see in her a figure well along the path of [Individuation](/knowledge/psychology/jungian/individuation), power and depth reconciled, the The Shadow befriended rather than feared. She embodies a warmth that can only come from wholeness, kin to the generative radiance of [The Empress](/knowledge/tarot/major-arcana/the-empress) and the confident vitality of [The Sun](/knowledge/tarot/major-arcana/the-sun).

## Mythological Echoes

The card carries the great solar and lioness goddesses — Sekhmet with her fierce protective heat, Bast with her cats, the queenly sun-figures whose warmth gives life and whose wrath can wither. It echoes every sovereign woman whose charisma was inseparable from her fearlessness, at ease with both the sunflower and the cat, the light and the dark familiar.

## In Practice

When this card appears, it invites you to stand in your own warmth without shrinking. Ask where you dim yourself for others' comfort. The Queen keeps her shadow close and shines anyway. Journal on what it would mean to be fully, unapologetically radiant.
