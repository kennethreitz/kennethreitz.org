# Proserpina

**Domain**: The underworld, spring, grain, the seed and its return
**Symbol**: Pomegranate, sheaf of grain, torch, narcissus
**Greek Equivalent**: Persephone

---

## Overview

Proserpina is the Roman queen of the underworld and, in the same breath, the goddess of spring — the two facts held together in a single life. Daughter of [Ceres](/knowledge/mythology/roman-mythology/ceres), she was gathering flowers when [Pluto](/knowledge/mythology/roman-mythology/pluto) opened the earth and carried her below to reign beside him. Her mother’s grief laid the world barren, and only a bargain restored her: because Proserpina had eaten seeds of the pomegranate in the underworld, she must return there for part of each year. Her descent is winter; her rising is spring. She is the seed that must be buried before it can grow.

She is therefore a threshold goddess, belonging wholly to neither world. Above, she is the tender green of the returning year; below, she is a dread queen, sovereign over the dead beside her husband. To know her is to understand that the same power governs the grave and the garden — that what goes down does not go down forever.

## Differences from Persephone

The myth follows the Greek Persephone closely, and the Roman telling — most fully in Ovid and in Claudian’s later epic — keeps its shape while giving her mother [Ceres](/knowledge/mythology/roman-mythology/ceres) the emotional center as the grieving searcher whose sorrow explains the seasons. The core meaning is unchanged: an abduction, a bargain sealed by the pomegranate, and a cycle of descent and return written into the turning year.
