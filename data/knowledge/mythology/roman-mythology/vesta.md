# Vesta

**Domain**: Hearth, home, family, domestic life
**Symbol**: Sacred fire, donkey
**Greek Equivalent**: Hestia

---

## Overview

Vesta is the virgin goddess of the hearth, home, and family. Her sacred fire burned continuously in her temple in the Roman Forum, tended by the Vestal Virgins -- priestesses chosen as children who served for thirty years, enjoying great honor and privilege but facing severe punishment for breaking their vows.

The sacred flame was never allowed to go out, serving as a symbol of Rome's eternal nature. It was rekindled only on March 1st each year, the start of the old Roman calendar. Her sacred animal is the donkey, whose braying — by one tradition — once woke the goddess in time to thwart an attempted assault by the fertility god Priapus, the reason donkeys were garlanded at her festival.

Almost uniquely among the major deities, Vesta was rarely depicted in human form. She *was* the fire, and the living flame on the hearth served as her image — a goddess present in every Roman home, not only in temples. The hearth was the symbolic center of the household, and its flame the center of the state; the two scaled into one another, the family writ large as the city. Her great festival, the Vestalia, ran from June 7th to 15th, when the inner sanctum of her temple was opened to married women and the storehouses ritually cleaned.

The six Vestal Virgins formed one of the most respected priesthoods in Rome. Sworn to thirty years of chastity — a decade learning, a decade serving, a decade teaching — they enjoyed privileges no other Roman women held: they could own property, make wills, and move about the city freely. The flame's care was sacred duty. Letting it go out was read as an omen of disaster and met with scourging, while a Vestal who broke her vow of chastity faced the terrible penalty of being entombed alive, since her sacred blood could not be spilled.
