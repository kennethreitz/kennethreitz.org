# Faunus

**Domain**: The wild, forests, fields, herds, fertility, prophecy
**Symbol**: Horns, goat-legs, the woodland, the pipe
**Greek Equivalent**: [Pan](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/pan)

---

## Overview

Faunus is the old Roman god of the untamed countryside — the forests and pastures, the herds that graze them, and the fertility that keeps both flock and field alive. He is a rustic power, half-wild, sometimes pictured with horns and the legs of a goat, and he belongs to the woods more than to any city temple. Farmers and shepherds knew him as a friend to the herd and a guardian of grazing land, but also as a presence that could unsettle a lonely traveller who wandered too far into the trees.

His nature drew him early into the orbit of the Greek [Pan](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/pan), and the two were largely identified, so that Faunus took on Pan's goat-form and his association with panic and wild music. Yet Faunus kept a distinctly Roman office the Greek god never had: he was a prophetic voice of the woodland. Sleepers who lay down in his sacred groves received oracles in their dreams, and his warnings were said to sound out from the trees themselves — a disembodied voice in the forest, the *fauni voces*.

## The Prophetic Voice

Where other gods spoke through priests and formal oracles, Faunus spoke through the land. Roman writers record his voice issuing from the depths of a wood to warn of coming disaster, and his oracles came to those who slept on the fleece of a sacrificed sheep in his grove. This gift of woodland prophecy made him more than a mere shepherd's god: he was the countryside made articulate, the wild returning an answer to those who listened.

## The Lupercalia

The wild, fertile character of Faunus is bound up with the Lupercalia, the archaic February festival celebrated at the Lupercal cave below the Palatine. There the *Luperci*, young men clad in the skins of sacrificed goats, ran through the streets striking bystanders — especially women — with strips of hide to confer fertility. Under his cult-title Faunus Lupercus he presides over this rite of purification and increase, the god of the fecund wild loosed for a day into the heart of the city.
