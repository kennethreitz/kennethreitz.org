# Saturn

**Domain**: Time, harvest, agriculture, wealth, the lost Golden Age
**Symbol**: Sickle, scythe, sheaf of grain
**Greek Equivalent**: [Cronus](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/cronus)

---

## Overview

Saturn is the Roman god of time, harvest, and agriculture — the deity of the sown field and the turning year. In myth he is the father dethroned by [Jupiter](/knowledge/mythology/roman-mythology/jupiter), cast out of heaven; but Rome told a gentler sequel than Greece did. Fleeing to Italy, Saturn was received by [Janus](/knowledge/mythology/roman-mythology/janus) and ruled Latium in the Golden Age, a lost time of plenty and peace when the earth gave freely and no one knew slavery or want. To honor a god of time is to remember that such an age existed and to grieve that it passed.

His great festival, the Saturnalia, opened in mid-December and inverted the social order — masters served slaves, gifts changed hands, and license replaced restraint, a brief return of the Golden Age within the calendar. His temple at the foot of the Capitoline housed the state treasury, marking him as a keeper of wealth as well as of grain and years.

## Differences from Cronus

Greek [Cronus](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/cronus) is chiefly the devouring titan — the father who swallows his children to forestall his own overthrow, an image of time consuming all it makes. Rome kept that shadow but added a redemptive one: the exiled king who founded a paradise. Saturn is therefore double, both the reaper who cuts everything down and the gentle sovereign of the age we long to recover.

His name endures in the planet [Saturn](/knowledge/astrology/planets/saturn-astrology), which carries the same weight — limitation, discipline, time, and the slow accounting of what endures.
