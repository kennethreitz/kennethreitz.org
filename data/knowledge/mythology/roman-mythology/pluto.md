# Pluto

**Domain**: The underworld, death, hidden wealth, the buried dead
**Symbol**: Cypress, key, cornucopia, the bident
**Greek Equivalent**: [Hades](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/hades)

---

## Overview

Pluto is the Roman lord of the underworld and ruler of the dead — but his name, drawn from the Greek Plouton, carries a meaning [Hades](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/hades) often lacked: “the wealthy one.” To the Romans, the god of the underworld was also the god of the earth’s hidden riches, for everything that grows and everything of value — grain, metal, the seed itself — rises from below. Death and abundance were, in his person, the same fact seen from two directions.

He rules with his queen [Proserpina](/knowledge/mythology/roman-mythology/proserpina), whom he carried into the earth, and his realm is the destination of every mortal life. Where the Greeks feared even to speak the name of Hades, the Romans could greet Pluto as Dis Pater — “the rich father” — acknowledging that the ground which receives the dead is the same ground that feeds the living.

## Differences from Hades

The Roman conception leans harder into the wealth aspect. Hades is chiefly the grim custodian of shades; Pluto is that custodian understood as a giver — the source of buried treasure and agricultural plenty. The dread remains, but it is tempered by the recognition that the underworld is not merely an end. It is where things are stored, and from which they return.

His name also passed to the astrological body [Pluto](/knowledge/astrology/planets/pluto-astrology), keeper of the same themes on a psychological scale: death, transformation, and what lies buried in the depths.
