# Durga

**Domain**: Warrior mother, protection, righteous power, slayer of demons
**Symbol**: The lion or tiger mount, many arms bearing weapons, the trident
**Consort**: [Shiva](/knowledge/mythology/hindu-mythology/shiva) (as the great goddess Devi)

---

## Overview

Durga is the warrior mother — the goddess who takes up arms when the gods themselves cannot prevail. She rides a lion into battle, her many arms each wielding a weapon gifted by the gods, and she meets the demons that threaten creation with a serenity that never breaks even at the height of war. She is Shakti concentrated into martial power, the fierce protector who is nonetheless, always, a mother to her devotees.

Her name means “the impassable” or “the invincible fortress.” She is the boundary that evil cannot cross. Where Parvati is gentleness and Kali is annihilation, Durga is the balanced middle — ferocity in the service of order, power wielded with composure. Her face in battle is calm, even beautiful, for she fights without hatred, destroying what must be destroyed as a matter of cosmic necessity.

---

## Birth from the Gods

Durga is born of crisis. The buffalo demon Mahishasura had won a boon that no man and no god could kill him, and so he conquered the heavens and drove the gods from their thrones. Powerless individually, the gods combined their energies — a great blaze of light poured from each of them — and from that fused radiance Durga took form, a woman more powerful than any of her makers.

Each god armed her: [Shiva](/knowledge/mythology/hindu-mythology/shiva) gave his trident, [Vishnu](/knowledge/mythology/hindu-mythology/vishnu) his discus, Indra his thunderbolt, and so on, until she bore a weapon in every hand. The Himalaya gave her a lion to ride. She was made from all the gods precisely because no single one could do what she was born to do.

---

## The Slaying of Mahishasura

The battle with the buffalo demon is her defining myth. Mahishasura, who could shift his shape, hurled army after army against her, and she cut them all down without losing her calm. When he charged as a buffalo, she caught him; when he slipped into other forms, she pursued him through each. At last she pinned him beneath her foot and drove her trident through him, slaying the unslayable.

For this she is called Mahishasuramardini — the slayer of Mahishasura — and the festival of Durga Puja, and the nine nights of Navaratri, celebrate this victory of divine order over the demonic. The image of the goddess triumphant over the demon, lion at her side, is among the most beloved in all of Hindu art.

---

## The One and the Many

Durga is understood as the great goddess Devi in her active form — the source from which other goddesses emerge. When her wrath overflows in battle, Kali springs from her brow. She contains Lakshmi and Saraswati as her own aspects of fortune and wisdom. She is, in the theology of Shaktism, the ultimate reality itself, the power without which even the great gods are inert.

This is her deepest teaching: that power and protection are feminine, that the ground of being is a mother, and that she is invincible precisely because she is everything.

---

## Archetypal Significance

Durga is the archetype of righteous power held in perfect composure — force without cruelty, courage without fear. She is [Strength](/knowledge/tarot/major-arcana/strength) in the tarot: not brute domination but the gentle, unshakable mastery of the lion, the soul that tames ferocity through inner steadiness. Psychologically she is the integrated warrior, the capacity to confront and defeat the demonic without being consumed by it, the protective power that guards what is sacred.
