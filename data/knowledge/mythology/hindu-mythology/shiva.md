# Shiva

**Domain**: Destroyer, transformer, ascetic, cosmic dancer
**Symbol**: Trident, third eye, crescent moon, cobra, lingam
**Consort**: Parvati/Shakti (divine feminine energy)

---

## Overview

Shiva is the destroyer/transformer god in the Hindu Trimurti. He destroys to transform, ending cycles so new ones can begin. He is the supreme ascetic and the cosmic dancer. Paradoxical and complex, he embodies opposites: destruction and creation, ascetic and householder, gentle and fierce.

His third eye destroys all when opened, yet also represents spiritual vision. The crescent moon on his head controls time. Matted hair contains the river Ganges. His blue throat (Nilakantha) comes from drinking cosmic poison to save the world. Ash-smeared body signifies renunciation; tiger skin represents conquered ego. The trident (Trishula) represents the three aspects of existence; the cobra around his neck signals mastery of death and fear; the damaru (drum) sounds the rhythm of creation.

The lingam -- his most common worship form -- represents the formless within form, creative power as cosmic pillar.

---

## Forms and Aspects

**Nataraja (Lord of Dance)**: The cosmic dancer, dancing in a ring of fire with four arms in symbolic positions. Upper right hand holds a drum (creation), upper left holds flame (destruction), lower right offers blessing (fearlessness), lower left points to raised foot (liberation). He stands on the dwarf demon of ignorance. Creation and destruction through dance, the cosmic rhythm made visible.

**Shiva the Ascetic**: The Great Yogi, meditating on Mount Kailash in the Himalayas. Supreme renunciate, source of yoga, who destroys through withdrawal.

**Rudra**: The fierce, howling aspect -- destroyer of evil, storm god, pre-Vedic in origin.

**Bhairava**: The terrifying guardian form, associated with time and annihilation, Tantric practices.

---

## Shiva and Parvati

Shiva's first wife Sati self-immolated at her father's insult to Shiva, nearly unleashing cosmic destruction through his grief and rage. Parvati, Sati's reincarnation, pursued Shiva despite his deep meditation, winning him through devotion.

Their relationship embodies perfect balance: Shiva is consciousness, Parvati/Shakti is energy. Neither is complete without the other. This finds its most striking expression in Ardhanarishvara, the half-male, half-female form representing ultimate non-duality.

Their children: [Ganesha](/knowledge/mythology/hindu-mythology/ganesha) (the elephant-headed remover of obstacles, created by Parvati, unknowingly beheaded by Shiva, restored with an elephant head) and Kartikeya/Murugan (god of war, born to defeat a demon, popular in South India).

## Major Myths

**Drinking the Poison**: When the churning of the cosmic ocean produces a deadly poison (halahala) threatening all existence, Shiva drinks it. Parvati grabs his throat to stop it from spreading, holding it there -- turning his throat blue.

**Ganges Descent**: The Ganges falls from heaven with a force that would destroy earth. Shiva catches the river in his matted hair and releases it gently, controlling the cosmic flow.

**Destruction of Kama**: The god of desire shoots an arrow at meditating Shiva to make him notice Parvati. Shiva opens his third eye and burns Kama to ash. Later restores him.

**Tandava Dance**: The cosmic dance of destruction at the end of each age, destroying to renew, the rhythm of the universe itself.

---

## Shaivism

The largest Shaivite tradition holds Shiva as ultimate reality (Para-Brahman), with all other gods as aspects of him. Kashmir Shaivism, a major philosophical school, treats Shiva as consciousness itself, emphasizing recognition (pratyabhijna) and non-dual reality with Tantric influence.

Core practices include lingam worship, chanting "Om Namah Shivaya," the Maha Shivaratri festival, meditation and yoga, and Tantric practices.

---

## Philosophical Aspects

**Destruction as Transformation**: Not malicious but necessary -- the end of illusion (maya), the death of ego, cosmic recycling.

**Union of Opposites**: Shiva contains creation (lingam, Nataraja) and destruction (third eye, Rudra), the ascetic and the householder, the gentle (with Parvati) and the fierce (as Rudra), form (Nataraja) and formless (lingam), the transcendent and the immanent.

**Shiva-Shakti Unity**: Shiva is passive consciousness, Shakti is active energy. Neither exists without the other. The ultimate non-duality.

---

## Sacred Sites

**Mount Kailash** in the Himalayas (Tibet) is Shiva's abode -- an unclimbed sacred mountain and major pilgrimage site. **Varanasi (Kashi)**, the holiest city to Shiva on the Ganges, grants liberation at death. The **Twelve Jyotirlingas** are the most powerful lingam shrines across India.

## Archetypal Significance

Destroyer of ego, death and rebirth, integration of opposites, the transcendent function. As shadow archetype: the necessary darkness, wildness and chaos, what must be destroyed for wholeness. Psychologically: transformation through dissolution, meditation and withdrawal, dancing with life and death.
