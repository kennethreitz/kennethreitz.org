# Hera

**Domain**: Queen of the gods, marriage, women, childbirth
**Symbol**: Peacock, cow, pomegranate
**Roman Equivalent**: [Juno](/knowledge/mythology/roman-mythology/juno)

---

## Overview

Hera is the queen of the Olympian gods, wife and sister of [Zeus](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/zeus). As goddess of marriage, women, and childbirth, she fiercely protects the sanctity of the marital bond. Her jealousy toward Zeus's many lovers and their offspring is a central thread running through Greek mythology.

Her sacred animals are the peacock and the cow. She wears a diadem and carries a lotus staff, symbols of her royal authority. Despite her often vengeful portrayal, she embodies the archetype of committed partnership and the power of feminine sovereignty — a queen in her own right, not merely a consort, whose wrath is the shadow side of her devotion to the sanctity of the bond.

---

## Key Myths

**The persecution of Heracles.** Heracles, son of Zeus by the mortal Alcmene, was hated by Hera from birth. She sent serpents to his cradle, drove him to the madness in which he killed his own children, and engineered the Twelve Labors as his penance. Yet the hero's very name — "glory of Hera" — binds his fame to the goddess who tormented him.

**The wrath against Leto.** When Leto was pregnant by Zeus with the twins [Apollo](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/apollo) and [Artemis](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/artemis), Hera forbade any land under the sun to give her shelter. Only the floating island of Delos, anchored to nothing, dared receive her, and there the twins were born.

**The Judgment of Paris.** When Eris threw the golden apple "for the fairest," Hera, [Athena](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/athena), and Aphrodite each sought it. Paris awarded it to Aphrodite, who had promised him Helen — and Hera's slighted pride made her an implacable enemy of Troy for the whole of the Trojan War.
