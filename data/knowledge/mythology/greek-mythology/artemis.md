# Artemis

**Domain**: Hunt, wilderness, moon, childbirth, virginity
**Symbol**: Bow and arrow, deer, crescent moon
**Roman Equivalent**: [Diana](/knowledge/mythology/roman-mythology/diana)

---

## Overview

Artemis is the goddess of the hunt, wilderness, wild animals, the moon, and childbirth. A virgin goddess and twin sister of [Apollo](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/apollo), she roams the forests with her band of nymphs, fiercely protecting young women and wildlife alike.

Her bow and arrow are her defining attributes, alongside the deer and the crescent moon. The cypress tree is sacred to her. She is swift to punish those who violate her sanctity or harm those under her protection, making her one of the most formidable Olympians despite standing apart from the politics of the other gods. As a member of the Triple Goddess — maiden alongside Selene and Hecate — she governs the waxing moon and the wild, unmarried freedom of young women.

---

## Key Myths

**Actaeon.** The hunter Actaeon stumbled upon Artemis bathing naked in a forest pool. For seeing what no mortal man was permitted to see, she transformed him into a stag, and his own hounds tore him apart — the archetypal lesson in the cost of violating the goddess's sanctity.

**The birth at Delos.** Born just before her twin [Apollo](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/apollo) to the wandering Leto, Artemis is said in some traditions to have midwifed her own brother's birth, becoming thereafter a protector of women in childbirth despite her own sworn virginity.

**Niobe's children.** When Niobe boasted that her fourteen children made her superior to Leto, who had only two, Artemis and Apollo answered the insult with arrows — Apollo killing the sons, Artemis the daughters — a stark display of the twins' shared and merciless defense of their mother's honor.
