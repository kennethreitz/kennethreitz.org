# Hades

**Domain**: The underworld, the dead, hidden wealth
**Symbol**: Cerberus, the bident, the Helm of Darkness, cypress, narcissus
**Roman Equivalent**: Pluto (Dis Pater)

---

## Overview

Hades is the god of the underworld and the dead — eldest son of Cronus, brother to [Zeus](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/zeus) and [Poseidon](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/poseidon). When the three brothers drew lots to divide the cosmos after overthrowing the Titans, Zeus took the sky, Poseidon the sea, and Hades the realm below. He rules the dead but is not death itself (that is Thanatos); he is the grim, even-handed steward of the place all souls eventually reach. The Greeks feared his name enough to call him *Plouton* — "the wealthy one" — partly as flattery, partly because the riches of the earth (seed, metal, the buried) belong to the ground he governs.

He is not evil, only inexorable. His kingdom takes everyone and releases almost no one.

---

## Key Myths

**The abduction of Persephone.** Hades' one great act of longing: he carried Persephone, daughter of [Demeter](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/demeter), into the underworld to be his queen, with [Zeus](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/zeus)'s quiet consent. The pomegranate seeds she ate there bound her to return below for part of each year — the myth of the seasons, and the one story that gives the cold god a consort.

**The Helm of Darkness.** Forged by the Cyclopes alongside Zeus's thunderbolt and Poseidon's trident, Hades' helm grants invisibility. He lends it rarely — to Perseus for the slaying of Medusa, and to gods who need to move unseen.

**Few escapes.** Heracles dragged Cerberus into the daylight as his final labor and returned; Orpheus nearly led Eurydice back with his song before losing her at the threshold; Sisyphus cheated his way out twice before his eternal stone. The lesson is always the same: the door swings one way.
