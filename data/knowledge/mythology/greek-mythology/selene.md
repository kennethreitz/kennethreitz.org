# Selene

**Domain**: The moon
**Symbol**: The crescent and full disc, the silver chariot, the torch, the veil
**Roman Equivalent**: Luna

---

## Overview

Selene is the Titan goddess of the moon itself — not a symbol of it but the thing in the sky. Daughter of the Titans Hyperion and Theia, she is sister to [Helios](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/helios) the sun and to Eos the dawn, the three of them the great lights of the day and night. Each evening she rises as her brother sets, driving a silver chariot drawn by pale horses across the dark, a crescent crown on her brow and a trailing veil behind her. Where later Greeks folded lunar associations into [Artemis](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/artemis), Selene remained the older, purer thing: the moon as a face, a body, a light that waxes and wanes.

She is gentle and luminous, the softest of the sky-powers — but constant, pulling the tides of night after her without fail.

---

## Key Myths

**Endymion.** Selene's great love was Endymion, a mortal shepherd of extraordinary beauty. Unwilling to lose him to age or death, she arranged that he sleep forever, ageless and unchanging in a cave, so she could descend to him each night. By him she was said to bear fifty daughters — the fifty lunar months of an Olympiad. It is the tenderest of the moon myths: a goddess who chose eternal, quiet devotion over a love that would fade.

**The lights of the sky.** Selene, Helios, and Eos divide the whole of time between them — dawn, day, and night — the children of Hyperion carrying the three great lights in turn. When Selene rides, the world sleeps beneath her; her waxing and waning became the measure of the month itself.

**The lunar triad.** In later worship Selene took her place as the shining, heavenly face of the moon alongside [Artemis](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/artemis) on earth and [Hecate](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/hecate) in the underworld — one power seen from three vantages, full and bright in the sky above.
