# Aphrodite

**Domain**: Love, beauty, desire, pleasure
**Symbol**: Dove, rose, swan, myrtle
**Roman Equivalent**: [Venus](/knowledge/mythology/roman-mythology/venus)

---

## Overview

Aphrodite is the goddess of love, beauty, pleasure, and procreation — one of the oldest and most powerful forces in the Greek cosmos, governing a domain no god or mortal can resist. Born from the sea foam (*aphros*) where the severed flesh of the primordial sky-god Uranus met the waves, she predates the Olympians yet takes her place among them. She possesses the cestus, an embroidered girdle that makes anyone fall in love with its wearer; even [Hera](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/hera) borrows it to seduce Zeus.

Her sacred animals are the dove and the swan; the rose and the myrtle are her plants. The scallop shell recalls her ocean birth. Married to [Hephaestus](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/hephaestus) the lame craftsman-god but lover of [Ares](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/ares) the war-god, she is central to some of mythology's most consequential stories. Beautiful and capricious, she rewards those who honor desire and punishes those who deny it — and her gifts, freely given, often carry ruinous consequences.

---

## Key Myths

**The Judgement of Paris.** When Eris threw down a golden apple inscribed "to the fairest," Hera, [Athena](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/athena), and Aphrodite each claimed it. Zeus appointed the Trojan prince Paris to judge. Each goddess offered a bribe — Hera power, Athena wisdom and victory, Aphrodite the most beautiful woman in the world. Paris chose Aphrodite, and she gave him Helen of Sparta. The abduction that followed sparked the Trojan War.

**The affair with Ares.** Aphrodite's love for the war-god Ares, conducted behind the back of her husband Hephaestus, ended in scandal when the smith trapped the lovers in an unbreakable net of fine chains and exposed them to the laughter of the gods. The union produced Harmonia and, in many tellings, Eros.

**Adonis.** Aphrodite fell in love with the beautiful mortal youth Adonis and quarreled with Persephone over him; Zeus ruled that he divide the year between them. When Adonis was killed by a wild boar while hunting, the goddess's grief stained the anemone flowers red — an ancient myth of love, death, and seasonal return.

**Pygmalion.** Moved by the sculptor Pygmalion's devotion to the ivory woman he had carved and fallen in love with, Aphrodite brought the statue to life — proof that the goddess of love can answer sincere longing with transformation.
