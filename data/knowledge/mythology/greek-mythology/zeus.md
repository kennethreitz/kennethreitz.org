# Zeus

**Domain**: King of the gods, sky, thunder, lightning
**Symbol**: Thunderbolt, eagle, oak tree
**Roman Equivalent**: [Jupiter](/knowledge/mythology/roman-mythology/jupiter)

---

## Overview

Zeus is the king of the Olympian gods and ruler of Mount Olympus. God of the sky, thunder, and lightning, he maintains order among gods and mortals alike, wielding the thunderbolt as both weapon and symbol of supreme authority.

His sacred animals are the eagle and the bull. The oak tree is his sacred plant, and the aegis serves as his divine shield. He overthrew his father Cronus and the Titans to establish Olympian rule, then divided dominion of the world with his brothers [Poseidon](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/poseidon) (sea) and Hades (underworld), keeping the sky and earth for himself. As ruler he is at once the guarantor of cosmic order — protector of guests, suppliants, and oaths — and its most flagrant transgressor, his endless affairs setting the plot of half of Greek myth in motion.

---

## Key Myths

**The overthrow of Cronus.** Cronus, warned that one of his children would depose him, swallowed each at birth. Rhea hid the infant Zeus on Crete and gave Cronus a swaddled stone instead. Grown in secret, Zeus forced his father to disgorge his siblings and led them in the ten-year Titanomachy, casting the defeated Titans into Tartarus and dividing the cosmos with his brothers.

**The birth of [Athena](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/athena).** Warned that the child of his first wife Metis would surpass him, Zeus swallowed her while she was pregnant. In time a splitting headache seized him; Hephaestus cleaved his skull, and Athena sprang forth fully grown and armored — wisdom born directly from the king's own head.

**The seductions.** Zeus pursued mortals and goddesses in a famous array of disguises — a swan to Leda, a shower of gold to Danaë, a white bull to Europa, an eagle for Ganymede — fathering heroes and gods while incurring the relentless jealousy of [Hera](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/hera). These unions seed the lineages of Heracles, Perseus, Helen, and many others.
