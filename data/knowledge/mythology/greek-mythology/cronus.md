# Cronus

**Domain**: Time, the harvest, the ruling Titan
**Symbol**: The sickle (or scythe), the grain
**Roman Equivalent**: Saturn

---

## Overview

Cronus is the youngest and boldest of the Titans, the son of [Gaia](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/gaia) and [Uranus](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/uranus) who overthrew his father and ruled the cosmos in the age before the Olympians — a span the Greeks remembered, paradoxically, as a Golden Age. He carries the sickle with which he unseated Uranus, and in later tradition he blurs into *Chronos*, Time itself: the power that harvests everything it brings to ripeness.

He is the hinge of the cosmogony — the son who ends his father's reign and then, in trying to keep his own, becomes the father who must be ended.

---

## Key Myths

**Devouring his children.** Warned by [Gaia](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/gaia) and [Uranus](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/uranus) that one of his own children would overthrow him, Cronus swallowed each as [Rhea](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/rhea) bore them — [Hera](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/hera), [Demeter](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/demeter), [Hestia](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/hestia), [Hades](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/hades), and [Poseidon](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/poseidon) all consumed at birth. It is the starkest image in the cosmogony of a power that would rather destroy the future than be replaced by it.

**The stone and the fall.** Rhea hid the infant [Zeus](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/zeus) and gave Cronus a swaddled stone to swallow instead. Grown in secret, Zeus forced his father to disgorge the others, and in the ten-year Titanomachy the Olympians cast Cronus and the Titans down into Tartarus — the prophecy fulfilled exactly as Cronus had fulfilled it against Uranus.
