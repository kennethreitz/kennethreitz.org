# Iris

**Domain**: The rainbow, and the messages of the gods
**Symbol**: The rainbow, golden wings, the herald's staff (caduceus), a pitcher of water
**Roman Equivalent**: Arcus (rarely personified)

---

## Overview

Iris is the Greek goddess of the rainbow and, before [Hermes](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/hermes) took the role for Olympus at large, the gods' own messenger — the one who carries word between heaven, earth, and the sea. Daughter of the sea-god Thaumas and the cloud-nymph Electra, she is sister to the Harpies, storm-spirits of the snatching wind; she herself is the gentler weather, the arc of colour that appears when the storm breaks and light returns to the rain. Where Hermes serves the gods generally, Iris is especially the handmaid of [Hera](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/hera), queen of Olympus, running her errands across the world.

The rainbow is her road. The Greeks saw in that bridge of colour between cloud and ground a literal path a goddess might travel, and so Iris is always in motion — golden-winged, swift, descending a shaft of light with a message and ascending it again. She is a threshold power, belonging fully to neither sky nor earth nor sea but moving between all three, and so she was also called on to carry water from the [underworld](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/hades) river Styx up to Olympus whenever a god needed to swear an unbreakable oath.

---

## Key Myths

**The messenger of the gods.** In Homer's *Iliad* it is Iris, not Hermes, who flies down with the will of Zeus and Hera — rousing armies, summoning the winds, calling the gods to war or restraint. She is the divine word in transit, valued precisely because she delivers faithfully and does not linger. Her speed is proverbial: she goes and returns before the message has cooled.

**The oath of the gods.** When a god swore a solemn oath, Iris fetched a pitcher of water from the Styx for them to pour; to break such an oath was to suffer a year of breathless coma and nine more years of exile from the divine feasts. The rainbow thus links not only sky to earth but the living heaven to the deathly river below — Iris the one power trusted to cross that dreadful boundary and come back.

**Handmaid of Hera.** Iris attends the queen of Olympus, dressing her, bearing her commands, and standing ready at the throne. Where [Hermes](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/hermes) is quicksilver and sometimes sly, Iris is loyal and plain — the reliable herald, the calm after the storm rather than the trickster at the crossroads.
