# Helios

**Domain**: The sun, sight, oaths
**Symbol**: The radiate crown, the golden chariot, the four fiery horses
**Roman Equivalent**: Sol

---

## Overview

Helios is the Titan god of the sun — the sun as a person driving it. Son of the Titans Hyperion and Theia, brother to [Selene](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/selene) the moon and Eos the dawn, he rises each morning from the eastern ocean in a golden chariot pulled by four fire-breathing horses and crosses the sky from end to end, sinking into the western sea at dusk to be carried back east by night in a great golden cup. From that height he sees everything that happens on earth and under the sky, which made him the god the other gods called on when they needed a witness that could not be deceived.

He is brilliant and impartial — the all-seeing eye of the daylight world. In later thought his role was increasingly merged with [Apollo](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/apollo), but the older Helios is the literal sun, blazing and tireless, keeping his circuit whether men bless him or curse him.

---

## Key Myths

**The witness who sees all.** Because nothing on earth escapes him, Helios is the great informer of myth. It was he who saw [Hades](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/hades) seize Persephone and told the grieving [Demeter](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/demeter) where her daughter had gone; and it was he who caught [Aphrodite](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/aphrodite) and [Ares](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/ares) together and reported it to [Hephaestus](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/hephaestus). To swear by Helios was to swear by the one who could not be lied to.

**Phaethon and the chariot.** Helios's mortal son Phaethon begged to drive the sun-chariot for a single day. Bound by an oath, his father let him — and the boy could not hold the horses. The chariot scorched the earth, burning Africa to desert, until [Zeus](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/zeus) struck Phaethon down with a thunderbolt to save the world from fire. It is the definitive Greek parable of a power too great for the hand that reaches for it.

**The cattle of the sun.** On the island of Thrinacia, Helios kept sacred herds that were never to be harmed. When Odysseus's starving men slaughtered them, the god demanded justice from [Zeus](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/zeus), who wrecked their ship and drowned every man but Odysseus — a reminder that even the wanderer's cunning bows to the god who sees.
