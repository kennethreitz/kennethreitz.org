# Ma’at

**Domain**: Truth, justice, cosmic order, balance
**Symbol**: The ostrich feather, the scales, outstretched wings

---

## Overview

Ma’at is less a goddess than the principle that holds the universe together, given a face. Her name is the Egyptian word for truth, justice, order, and cosmic balance all at once — the right ordering of things, from the swing of the stars to the honesty of a merchant’s scale. She is depicted as a slender woman crowned with a single ostrich feather, and that feather is the measure against which all of reality is weighed. Where there is Ma’at, the cosmos runs true; where she fails, there is *isfet* — chaos, falsehood, the disorder that creation must forever hold at bay.

Every pharaoh ruled in her name, pledged to “uphold Ma’at,” because the throne’s whole justification was the maintenance of order against chaos. She is the daughter of [Ra](/knowledge/mythology/egyptian-mythology/ra) and stood at the heart of Egyptian ethics: not a list of commandments but a felt sense of balance, of living in truth, of not tipping the scales. To act with Ma’at was to align yourself with the deep grain of the world.

---

## Key Myths

**The weighing of the heart.** In the hall of judgment, the heart of the dead was placed on a scale against Ma’at’s feather. If the heart — heavy with sin and falsehood — outweighed the feather, it was devoured by the monster Ammit and the soul destroyed. If it balanced, the deceased passed into the afterlife justified. [Anubis](/knowledge/mythology/egyptian-mythology/anubis) worked the scales and [Thoth](/knowledge/mythology/egyptian-mythology/thoth) recorded the verdict, but it was Ma’at’s feather that set the standard. A whole moral universe rested on that single plume.

**The daughter of Ra.** Ma’at was said to be the daughter of the sun god, and she rode in his barque as he crossed the sky each day — order itself accompanying the sun on its journey, keeping chaos from swallowing the light. Some texts say Ra “lives on Ma’at,” as though truth were the food that sustains even the creator.

**The pharaoh’s duty.** Kings were depicted offering a small figure of Ma’at to the gods, symbolizing their pledge to sustain justice and order in the land. Ruling was not conquest but maintenance — the endless work of keeping the world in balance.
