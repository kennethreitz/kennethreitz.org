# Baskerville

> *An amateur's perfectionism, in black ink on white wove paper.*

---

## The Face

Baskerville is the type designed by [John Baskerville](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/john-baskerville)[^sn-john-baskerville] of Birmingham and first used in his edition of Virgil in 1757. It is the classic English [Transitional](/knowledge/typography/classification/transitional) face: wider and rounder than [Caslon](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/caslon), with a more vertical axis, crisper [serifs](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/serif), and greater [Stroke Contrast](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/stroke-contrast).

## The Whole Book

Baskerville treated the book as a single designed object. He developed a blacker, glossier ink, used smooth wove paper and pressed his printed sheets to give them a satin finish, and set his pages with wide margins and generous spacing. Contemporaries found the result dazzling, and some complained it hurt the eyes. His types influenced [Giambattista Bodoni](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/giambattista-bodoni) and the [Didone](/knowledge/typography/classification/didone) style that followed.

## Revival and Descendants

Monotype's 1923 revival brought Baskerville back into book production, and it has been a mainstay ever since. Zuzana Licko's Mrs Eaves (1996), named after Sarah Eaves, Baskerville's housekeeper and later his wife, is a looser, low [X-Height](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/x-height) reinterpretation.
