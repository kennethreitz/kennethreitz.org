# Gill Sans

> *An English sans serif with an inscription carver's hand.*

---

## The Face

Gill Sans was designed by [Eric Gill](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/eric-gill) and released by Monotype in 1928, with [Stanley Morison](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/stanley-morison)[^sn-stanley-morison] as its champion. It developed the lettering Gill's teacher [Edward Johnston](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/edward-johnston) had drawn for the London Underground in 1916, giving it the proportions of classical Roman capitals and the forms of a [Humanist Sans](/knowledge/typography/classification/humanist-sans).

## Character

Gill Sans has the variety of widths of an old roman: a wide, round **O** and a narrow **E**, a two-storey **g** with a spectacle-like lower bowl, and a gently calligraphic feel despite its plain strokes. Its many weights are not entirely consistent, which gives the family character and some awkwardness.

## In Britain

Adopted by the London and North Eastern Railway for its posters, timetables, and signage, by Penguin Books for its classic covers, and later by the BBC, Gill Sans came to stand for a certain mid-century Britishness the way [Futura](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/futura) stood for German modernism.

## Its Maker

Gill's achievements as a letter cutter and type designer sit beside the sexual abuse documented in his own diaries, discussed in [Eric Gill](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/eric-gill). Many designers now weigh that history when choosing the face.
