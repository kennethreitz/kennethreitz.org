# Akzidenz-Grotesk

> *The anonymous sans serif that became the voice of Swiss modernism.*

---

## The Face

Akzidenz-Grotesk was released by the Berthold foundry of Berlin in 1898. *Akzidenz* means jobbing or commercial printing, the forms, posters, and advertisements that were the bread and butter of a print shop. Its origins are murky; it was assembled and extended over years from existing grotesques, and no single designer is reliably credited.

## Character

A classic [Grotesque](/knowledge/typography/classification/grotesque): plain, sturdy, with slight irregularities between weights that give it more life than its successors. Its [apertures](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/aperture)[^sn-aperture] are fairly closed and its [terminals](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/terminal) mostly horizontal.

## Swiss Favorite

In the 1950s the designers of the [Swiss International Style](/knowledge/typography/history/swiss-international-style), including [Josef Müller-Brockmann](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/josef-muller-brockmann), preferred Akzidenz-Grotesk for its neutrality, setting it on rigorous [grids](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/grid-systems). Its popularity directly prompted the creation of [Helvetica](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/helvetica) (by the Haas foundry) and [Univers](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/univers), both designed to compete with it. Berthold extended the family through the twentieth century under the art direction of Günter Gerhard Lange.
