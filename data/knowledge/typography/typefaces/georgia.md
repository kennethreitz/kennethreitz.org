# Georgia

> *A serif drawn for the pixel grid, not the page.*

---

## The Face

Georgia was designed by [Matthew Carter](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/matthew-carter) for Microsoft, with hinting by Tom Rickner, and was released in 1996 as part of Microsoft's free core fonts for the web, alongside its sans serif sibling [Verdana](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/verdana). Its name came from a tabloid headline about alien heads found in Georgia.

## Designed for Screens

Carter designed the bitmaps, the pixel patterns at each common screen size, first and the outlines to fit them, rather than the reverse. The result is a serif with a large [X-Height](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/x-height), sturdy [serifs](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/serif), open [counters](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/counter), and moderate [Stroke Contrast](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/stroke-contrast)[^sn-stroke-contrast] that stays clear on low-resolution displays. Its [Figures](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/figures) are old-style by default, rising and falling like lowercase letters, which is unusual for a system font.

## Legacy

For a decade Georgia was the best serif reliably available on the web, which is why so many blogs and publications of the 2000s were set in it. It remains an excellent fallback in web font stacks (see font-family recommendations. Chris Morgan).
