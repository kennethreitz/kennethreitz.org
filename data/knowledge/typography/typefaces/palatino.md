# Palatino

> *A Renaissance hand, redrawn by a modern calligrapher.*

---

## The Face

Palatino was designed by [Hermann Zapf](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/hermann-zapf)[^sn-hermann-zapf] and released by the Stempel foundry of Frankfurt around 1949 to 1950. It is named after Giambattista Palatino, a sixteenth-century Italian writing master. Zapf drew on the inscriptional and calligraphic letters of the Italian Renaissance, and the face shows a calligrapher's hand in its broad, open forms and slightly flared strokes.

## Character

Palatino has wide letters, generous [counters](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/counter), a moderately large [X-Height](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/x-height), and calligraphic [serifs](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/serif). It was originally intended as a display and jobbing face, but its sturdiness and openness made it one of the most popular text faces of the century, and it reads well at small sizes and on screen.

## Copies and Revisions

Palatino was widely imitated, and close look-alikes such as Book Antiqua shipped with office software. Zapf objected strongly to unauthorized copies of his work. He revised the family himself, with Akira Kobayashi, as Palatino nova (2005), and it ships with macOS and Windows in authorized versions.
