# Optima

> *A sans serif that still swells at its ends, like carved stone.*

---

## The Face

Optima was designed by [Hermann Zapf](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/hermann-zapf)[^sn-hermann-zapf] and released by Stempel in 1958. Zapf conceived it in 1950 while visiting the church of Santa Croce in Florence, where he studied the lettering of the floor tombs and sketched letters on what he had to hand, famously a banknote.

## Character

Optima has no serifs, but its strokes are not monoline: they swell slightly toward their ends, as in carved letters, a style sometimes called *flared* or *glyphic*. It has the proportions of a classical roman and the open forms of a [Humanist Sans](/knowledge/typography/classification/humanist-sans). It sits deliberately between serif and sans, which makes it both warm and clean.

## Uses

Optima's dignity has made it a choice for memorials, most famously the names on the Vietnam Veterans Memorial in Washington, as well as for cosmetics and luxury brands. Zapf revised it with Akira Kobayashi as Optima nova (2002).
