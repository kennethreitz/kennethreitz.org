# Caslon

> *When in doubt, use Caslon.*

---

## The Face

Caslon is the type of [William Caslon](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/william-caslon), the London founder whose romans and italics, shown in his famous specimen sheet of 1734, freed English printers from their dependence on Dutch type. It is the last great [Old-Style](/knowledge/typography/classification/old-style) face: sturdy, unfussy, and slightly irregular, drawing on Dutch models of the seventeenth century.

## In the Colonies

Caslon type was shipped widely across the British Empire, including the American colonies. Benjamin Franklin knew and used it, and it is traditionally said that the first printed broadsides of the Declaration of Independence were set in Caslon. "When in doubt, use Caslon" became a printer's proverb.

## Character

Moderate [Stroke Contrast](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/stroke-contrast), bracketed [serifs](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/serif), a sturdy color, and a lively, uneven texture that gives the page warmth rather than polish. It fell out of favor when the [Transitional](/knowledge/typography/classification/transitional) and [Didone](/knowledge/typography/classification/didone) faces arrived, and was revived in the 1840s by the Chiswick Press, one of the first deliberate historical revivals in printing. Modern versions include Adobe Caslon (Carol Twombly, 1990) and Big Caslon by Matthew Carter.[^sn-matthew-carter]
