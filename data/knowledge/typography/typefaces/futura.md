# Futura

> *The typeface of today, and of the Moon.*

---

## The Face

Futura was designed by [Paul Renner](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/paul-renner) and released by the Bauer Type Foundry of Frankfurt in 1927. Its name means "the future," and it was the commercial fulfillment of the ideas of the [New Typography](/knowledge/typography/history/bauhaus-and-the-new-typography), though Renner was never a Bauhaus teacher. Bauer advertised it as "the typeface of our time."

## Character

The definitive [Geometric Sans](/knowledge/typography/classification/geometric-sans):[^sn-geometric-sans] a circular **o**, a single-storey **a**, sharply pointed **A**, **M**, and **N**, near uniform stroke weight, and tall [ascenders](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/ascender) that rise above the [Cap Height](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/cap-height). Renner's early drawings included far more radical experimental letters, which were dropped before release. The finished design makes many subtle optical corrections so that the geometry looks right rather than measures right.

## Everywhere

Futura spread quickly across Europe and America. The plaque left on the Moon by Apollo 11 in 1969 is set in Futura capitals, and the face has been a favorite of advertising, film titles, and brands for nearly a century. Its relatives and descendants include Avenir and [Gotham](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/gotham).
