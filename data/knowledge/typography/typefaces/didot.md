# Didot

> *The French modern: contrast as couture.*

---

## The Face

Didot names the types cut in Paris by the [Didot](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/firmin-didot) family, a dynasty of printers, publishers, and typefounders, above all those cut by Firmin Didot[^sn-firmin-didot] from the 1780s. With [Bodoni](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/bodoni), they define the [Didone](/knowledge/typography/classification/didone) style that bears their joint name.

## Character

Didot is lighter and more delicate than Bodoni, with fine hairline [serifs](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/serif) and thins, extreme [Stroke Contrast](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/stroke-contrast), vertical stress, and ball [terminals](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/terminal). It became the national type of Napoleonic France.

## Revivals

The Didot style is now synonymous with fashion. Jonathan Hoefler's HTF Didot (1991), drawn for Harper's Bazaar in multiple optical sizes so that the hairlines stay fine but intact at every size, is the best known modern version (see [Jonathan Hoefler](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/jonathan-hoefler)). A system Didot ships with macOS. The family also gave its name to the Didot point (see [Points and Picas](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/points-and-picas)), the European unit of type measurement, devised by François-Ambroise Didot, Firmin's father.
