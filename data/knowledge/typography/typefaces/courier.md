# Courier

> *The typewriter's voice, still heard in every screenplay.*

---

## The Face

Courier was designed by Howard "Bud" Kettler for IBM's typewriters in 1955. IBM did not secure exclusive rights to the design, and it became a standard across the typewriter industry and later on computers, where Courier New became the fixed-width fallback on Windows.

## Character

A [Monospace](/knowledge/typography/classification/monospace) [Slab Serif](/knowledge/typography/classification/slab-serif)[^sn-slab-serif] with low contrast and even strokes, designed to survive the impact of a typebar and the variability of ribbons and paper. Its slab serifs fill out narrow letters like **i** and **l** in their wide cells.

## Afterlife

Courier became the look of the typed document: government cables, manuscripts, and above all screenplays, where 12 point Courier is still the industry standard, partly because a page of it reliably approximates a minute of screen time. On screen, the thin digital Courier New is widely disliked (the clipping font-family recommendations. Chris Morgan calls it an abomination), and modern coding fonts like [IBM Plex](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/ibm-plex) Mono, [SF Mono](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/sf-mono), and [Iosevka](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/iosevka) have replaced it.
