# Frutiger

> *A typeface designed to be read while walking quickly toward a gate.*

---

## The Face

Frutiger began as the signage alphabet for the new Charles de Gaulle Airport at Roissy outside Paris, which opened in 1974. [Adrian Frutiger](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/adrian-frutiger)[^sn-adrian-frutiger] wanted letters that stayed legible from a distance, at an angle, and in motion. Stempel and Linotype released an expanded version as a text typeface in 1976 under its designer's name.

## Character

Frutiger is the archetypal [Humanist Sans](/knowledge/typography/classification/humanist-sans): open [apertures](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/aperture), angled [terminals](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/terminal), slight [Stroke Contrast](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/stroke-contrast), and letters with distinct, varied widths, all of which help readers identify characters quickly in difficult conditions (see [Readability and Legibility](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/readability-and-legibility)).

## Influence

It became one of the most widely used signage faces in the world and the model for a generation of humanist sans serifs in wayfinding, healthcare, and corporate identity. Frutiger Next (around 2000) and later Neue Frutiger extended the family, and its influence is plain in screen faces like [Verdana](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/verdana) and in the more open interface faces, such as [SF Pro](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/sf-pro), that succeeded [Helvetica](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/helvetica).
