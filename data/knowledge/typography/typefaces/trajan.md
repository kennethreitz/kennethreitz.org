# Trajan

> *Two thousand years of capitals, from a column in Rome to a movie poster.*

---

## The Face

Trajan is a capitals-only typeface designed by Carol Twombly for Adobe in 1989, based on the inscription at the base of Trajan's Column in Rome, dedicated in 113 AD. That inscription has long been regarded as the finest surviving example of Roman imperial capitals, and it is the starting point for most discussions of where the [Serif](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/serif) came from.

## The Inscription

The Roman capitals have no lowercase; the lowercase letters we use descend from medieval scribal hands, joined to these capitals by the Renaissance humanists (see [Humanist Serif](/knowledge/typography/classification/humanist-serif)). The inscription's proportions, stressed strokes, and bracketed serifs were studied by Renaissance letterers, by [Edward Johnston](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/edward-johnston)[^sn-edward-johnston] and [Eric Gill](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/eric-gill) in the twentieth century, and by Edward Catich, whose study of it argued for the brush origin of serifs.

## Fame

Trajan the typeface became, for a long stretch, the cliché of film posters, especially for historical epics and prestige dramas, because it lends any title instant gravity. Its real home, though, is the stone.
