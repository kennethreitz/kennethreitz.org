# Latin Modern

> *The academic paper's face, carried into the age of OpenType.*

---

## The Face

Latin Modern is the extension of [Computer Modern](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/computer-modern)[^sn-computer-modern] made by Bogusław Jackowski and Janusz M. Nowacki for GUST, the Polish TeX Users Group, in the 2000s. It converts Knuth's METAFONT designs to modern outline formats and adds a vast range of accented letters for European languages, while keeping the look of the original exactly.

## Optical Sizes

Like Computer Modern, Latin Modern comes in multiple optical sizes (for example LMRoman 7, 10, and 12), each drawn for its size rather than scaled, so small text is a little wider and sturdier. Modern LaTeX engines like XeLaTeX and LuaLaTeX use it in place of Computer Modern by default.

## In This Vault

Latin Modern is the reading face of Kenneth's Obsidian theme. When he wanted the theme to match LaTeX exactly, a real LaTeX PDF was measured, its embedded faces turned out to be LMRoman10 and 12 and LMMono10, and those genuine fonts were bundled into the theme across three optical sizes, with Latin Modern Mono for code in reading view (see [Draft to Document Why I Made My Notes Look Like LaTeX Papers](/essays/2026-11-04-draft_to_document)). The writing side of the same theme is set in [IBM Plex](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/ibm-plex) Mono.
