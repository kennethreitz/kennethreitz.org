# Bembo

> *The roman that taught the rest of Europe what a book page could be.*

---

## The Face

Bembo is the twentieth-century revival of the roman type cut by [Francesco Griffo](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/francesco-griffo)[^sn-francesco-griffo] for [Aldus Manutius](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/aldus-manutius) and first used in *De Aetna*, a short account of a climb up Mount Etna by the young humanist Pietro Bembo, printed in Venice in 1495 to 1496. That same book is also remembered as the first to print the modern semicolon. The type was the model for the French [Old-Style](/knowledge/typography/classification/old-style) romans of [Claude Garamond](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/claude-garamond) and his contemporaries, and so for most of the book faces that followed.

## The Revival

In 1929 the Monotype Corporation, under the typographic direction of [Stanley Morison](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/stanley-morison), issued a revival named Bembo after the author. Its italic was based not on Griffo's italic but on the writing of the Renaissance calligraphers. Bembo became one of the great book faces of the twentieth century.

## Descendants

[Edward Tufte](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/edward-tufte)'s [ET Book](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/et-book), used in his books and in the Tufte CSS that styles KJVstudy.org, is a Bembo-derived face, which means a line of type designed in Venice in the 1490s is quietly at work in Kenneth's own projects.

## Character

Small [X-Height](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/x-height), long extenders, light color, and calm proportions. Like [Garamond](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/garamond) it rewards generous [Leading](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/leading) and print or high-resolution screens.
