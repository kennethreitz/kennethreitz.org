# Garamond

> *Half the Garamonds in the world are not by Garamond, and they are lovely anyway.*

---

## The Face

Garamond names a family of [Old-Style](/knowledge/typography/classification/old-style) romans descended from the types cut by the Parisian punchcutter [Claude Garamond](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/claude-garamond) in the middle of the sixteenth century. Building on the roman cut by [Francesco Griffo](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/francesco-griffo)[^sn-francesco-griffo] for [Aldus Manutius](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/aldus-manutius) (see [Bembo](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/bembo)), Garamond produced types of such balance and quality that they set the pattern for European book printing for two hundred years. He also cut the *Grecs du roi*, Greek types commissioned for the French crown.

## The Jannon Confusion

Many twentieth-century "Garamonds" are not based on Garamond at all. In the 1920s [Beatrice Warde](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/beatrice-warde),[^sn-beatrice-warde] writing under the pseudonym Paul Beaujon, showed that several revivals, including American Type Founders' Garamond (1917) and Monotype's (1922), were actually modeled on types cut around 1615 by Jean Jannon, a Protestant printer working at Sedan, which had been misattributed to Garamond. Faces based on the authentic Garamond types include Robert Slimbach's Adobe Garamond (1989) and Garamond Premier, and the open-source EB Garamond by Georg Duffner.

## Character

Garamond has a small [X-Height](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/x-height), long [ascenders](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/ascender), a diagonal axis, moderate [Stroke Contrast](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/stroke-contrast), and graceful, small [counters](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/counter) that give the page an even, light texture. It is economical with space, which made it a printer's favorite, and it remains a standard for literary books.

## In Practice

Garamond prefers print and generous sizes; at small sizes on screens its fine features can look faint (see [Screen Typography and Hinting](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/screen-typography-and-hinting)). It pairs well with old-style [Figures](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/figures) and true [Small Caps](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/small-caps), which good digital versions supply.
