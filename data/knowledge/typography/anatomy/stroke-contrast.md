# Stroke Contrast

> *Contrast is the hand that held the pen, still visible centuries later.*

---

## Thick and Thin

Stroke contrast is the variation between the thickest and thinnest strokes in a letter. In an **o**, it is the difference between the heavy sides and the thin top and bottom. Closely tied to it is the *axis* or *stress*: the angle of the imaginary line drawn through the thinnest parts of a round letter.

## A Map of History

Contrast and axis tell you, almost at a glance, where a typeface sits in history (see [Classification](/knowledge/typography/classification)).

| Family | Contrast | Axis |
|---|---|---|
| [Humanist Serif](/knowledge/typography/classification/humanist-serif) | Low to moderate | Strongly diagonal, from a broad-nib pen held at an angle |
| [Old-Style](/knowledge/typography/classification/old-style) | Moderate | Diagonal |
| [Transitional](/knowledge/typography/classification/transitional) | Higher | Nearly vertical |
| [Didone](/knowledge/typography/classification/didone) | Extreme, with hairline thins | Vertical, rational |
| [Slab Serif](/knowledge/typography/classification/slab-serif) | Low | Vertical |
| [Geometric Sans](/knowledge/typography/classification/geometric-sans) | Near zero (monoline) | None |
| [Humanist Sans](/knowledge/typography/classification/humanist-sans) | Slight | Often a faint diagonal |

The diagonal axis of the early romans comes straight from the broad-edged pen of the humanist scribes. Over the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, punchcutters such as [John Baskerville](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/john-baskerville) drew letters that answered more to engraving and the pointed pen than to the broad nib, and the axis turned upright. By the time of [Bodoni](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/giambattista-bodoni)[^sn-giambattista-bodoni] and [Didot](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/firmin-didot), contrast had become a deliberate, dazzling effect made possible by smoother paper and better presses.

## In Practice

High-contrast faces like [Didot](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/didot)[^sn-didot] and [Bodoni](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/bodoni) sparkle at display sizes but their hairlines thin out or vanish at small sizes and on screens, which is why good revivals come in optical sizes. Low-contrast faces hold up in rough conditions, from newspaper presses to low-resolution displays (see [Screen Typography and Hinting](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/screen-typography-and-hinting)).
