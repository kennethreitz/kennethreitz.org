# Bowl

> *The bowl holds the counter the way a cupped hand holds water.*

---

## The Curve

A bowl is the curved stroke that encloses a [Counter](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/counter),[^sn-counter] as in **b**, **d**, **o**, **p**, **q**, and the upper and lower parts of **B**. A bowl may be closed, meeting the stem completely, or open, as in some forms of **P** where the curve stops short of the stem.

## Two-Storey Letters

The bowls of **a** and **g** are some of the most characteristic shapes in a typeface. Most serif faces and grotesques use the *two-storey* **a**, with a small bowl beneath an arched top, and many serif faces use the two-storey **g**, whose lower bowl is called the loop and is joined to the upper by the link. [Geometric Sans](/knowledge/typography/classification/geometric-sans) faces like [Futura](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/futura) prefer the single-storey **a**, a circle and a stem, which is purer in geometry but closer to children's handwriting and easier to confuse with **o** at small sizes.

## Shape and Stress

How a bowl swells and thins shows its [Stroke Contrast](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/stroke-contrast)[^sn-stroke-contrast] and axis. In [Garamond](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/garamond) the bowl of **o** thickens on a diagonal; in [Bodoni](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/bodoni) it thickens straight up and down; in [Futura](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/futura) it is a near perfect circle of even weight. Designers adjust bowls optically, letting them overshoot the [Baseline](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/baseline) and [X-Height](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/x-height) slightly so they look the same size as the flat letters.
