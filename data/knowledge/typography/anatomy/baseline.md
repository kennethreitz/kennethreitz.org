# Baseline

> *Every line of type is a row of figures standing on the same floor.*

---

## The Line

The baseline is the imaginary line on which the letters of a line of type sit. The flat feet of **H**, **x**, and **n** rest exactly on it; the round letters like **o** and **c** dip very slightly below it (a correction called overshoot, since a circle that only touched the line would look like it was floating); and the [descenders](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/descender) of **g**, **p**, and **y** hang down past it. Every other vertical measurement in a typeface, the [X-Height](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/x-height), the [Cap Height](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/cap-height),[^sn-cap-height] the reach of the [ascenders](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/ascender), is counted up or down from this line.

## Why It Matters

Because the baseline is shared, it is what makes a line of mixed sizes, weights, or even typefaces read as one line. Set an italic word or a run of [Small Caps](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/small-caps) inside a sentence and the eye accepts it because the feet still land in the same place. Across a page, the distance from one baseline to the next is the [Leading](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/leading), and keeping that distance consistent through headings, images, and footnotes is the whole discipline of [Vertical Rhythm](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/vertical-rhythm),[^sn-vertical-rhythm] often enforced with a baseline grid inside a larger [grid system](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/grid-systems).

In metal type the baseline was fixed by the foundry's casting, and type from different foundries often refused to line up until the American and British foundries standardized "point line" alignment around the turn of the twentieth century. On screens, the baseline is part of the font's internal metrics, and differences between fonts' metrics are one of the quiet reasons a fallback font makes a web page jump while it loads (see [Web Fonts](/knowledge/typography/history/web-fonts)).

## In Practice

Align text in adjacent columns to a common baseline rather than to the tops of their boxes. When pairing faces (see [Pairing Typefaces](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/pairing-typefaces)), check that their baselines and x-heights sit comfortably together at the sizes you intend. And when hanging superscripts and [Figures](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/figures) off the baseline, remember that the reader feels the floor before they notice anything else.
