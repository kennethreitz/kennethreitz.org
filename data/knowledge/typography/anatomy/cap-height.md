# Cap Height

> *Capitals are the architecture; the lowercase is the people living inside.*

---

## The Measure

Cap height is the distance from the [Baseline](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/baseline)[^sn-baseline] to the top of a flat capital letter such as **H** or **E**. Round and pointed capitals (**O**, **A**) overshoot it slightly, just as round lowercase letters overshoot the [X-Height](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/x-height).

In many [Old-Style](/knowledge/typography/classification/old-style)[^sn-old-style] and [Humanist Serif](/knowledge/typography/classification/humanist-serif) faces, the capitals are noticeably shorter than the lowercase [ascenders](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/ascender), so a capital at the start of a sentence does not shout over the words that follow. Many modern sans serifs, especially the [Neo-Grotesque](/knowledge/typography/classification/neo-grotesque) faces like [Helvetica](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/helvetica), set cap height and ascender height nearly level, which gives text a tidier, more uniform top edge.

## Capitals in Running Text

Because the capitals derive from Roman inscriptional letters (see [Trajan](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/trajan) and [Serif](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/serif)), they were designed to be read one at a time, carved large. Set in long runs they become a wall: every letter is the same height and word shapes disappear. This is why acronyms and runs of capitals in good typography are usually given a little extra [Tracking](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/tracking) or set in [Small Caps](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/small-caps), whose height sits near the x-height and blends with the lowercase.

## In Practice

Cap height also governs alignment in interface design: icons and buttons look centered on a line of text when they are centered on its cap height or x-height, not on the full height of the type's bounding box. Typographers aligning a large display capital (a drop cap) to a paragraph match its cap height to a whole number of lines of [Leading](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/leading).
