# Anatomy

> *Learn the names of the parts, and you start to see the letters.*

---

Every letter is built from a small set of parts, and every line of type sits inside a small set of measurements. Learning the vocabulary is the first step to seeing type clearly: once you can name the [X-Height](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/x-height), the [Aperture](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/aperture), or the [Stroke Contrast](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/stroke-contrast) of a face, you can say *why* it looks the way it does, and why it suits one job and not another. These notes cover the vertical metrics of a line, the parts of letters, the spacing that turns letters into text, and the units typographers measure with.

## Reading the Parts Together

The vertical metrics, [Baseline](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/baseline), [X-Height](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/x-height), [Cap Height](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/cap-height), [Ascender](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/ascender), and [Descender](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/descender), set how big a face looks and how much [Leading](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/leading) it needs. The letter parts, [Serif](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/serif), [Stroke Contrast](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/stroke-contrast), [Bowl](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/bowl), [Counter](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/counter), [Aperture](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/aperture), and [Terminal](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/terminal), are the features that [Classification](/knowledge/typography/classification) sorts typefaces by. The spacing terms, [Kerning](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/kerning), [Tracking](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/tracking), [Leading](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/leading), and [Measure](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/measure), are where anatomy becomes [practice](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles). And the units, [Em and En](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/em-and-en) and [Points and Picas](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/points-and-picas), plus the [Glyph](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/glyph) and the [Ligature](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/ligature), are the raw material of digital type.
