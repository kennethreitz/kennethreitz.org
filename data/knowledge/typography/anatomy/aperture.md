# Aperture

> *An open door lets the letter breathe; a closed one keeps the eye guessing.*

---

## The Opening

Aperture is the size of the opening into a partly enclosed [Counter](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/counter),[^sn-counter] as in **c**, **e**, **s**, **a**, and **C**. In an open-aperture face the strokes of **c** and **e** curl back only a little and the opening is wide; in a closed-aperture face the [terminals](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/terminal) curl round until they nearly meet.

## Open and Closed

Aperture is one of the clearest differences between kinds of sans serif (see [Classification](/knowledge/typography/classification)). The [Neo-Grotesque](/knowledge/typography/classification/neo-grotesque) faces, [Helvetica](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/helvetica) and [Univers](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/univers), have relatively closed apertures with horizontal terminals, which gives them their compact, self-contained look. The [Humanist Sans](/knowledge/typography/classification/humanist-sans) faces, [Gill Sans](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/gill-sans), [Frutiger](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/frutiger), [Verdana](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/verdana), open their apertures in the manner of the written hand.

Open apertures help legibility in hard conditions: at small sizes, at a distance, at an angle, or through glare. This is why [Adrian Frutiger](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/adrian-frutiger)[^sn-adrian-frutiger] designed the face that became [Frutiger](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/frutiger) for the signs of Charles de Gaulle airport with wide openings, and why many modern interface faces such as [Inter](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/inter) and [SF Pro](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/sf-pro) have moved toward more open forms than Helvetica's.

## In Practice

When text has to be read quickly or in poor conditions (signage, small interface labels, low-contrast screens), prefer open apertures. When a face will only be seen large and close, closed apertures can lend a calm uniformity. The research summarized in [Readability and Legibility](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/readability-and-legibility) keeps returning to aperture as one of the features that actually matters.
