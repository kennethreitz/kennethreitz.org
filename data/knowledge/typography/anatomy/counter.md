# Counter

> *Type is as much the shaping of white as the drawing of black.*

---

## The Inner Space

A counter is the white space enclosed, fully or partly, by the strokes of a letter: the hole in **o**, the two spaces in **B**, the eye of **e**, the open space inside **n** or **c**. Closed counters are surrounded entirely by a [Bowl](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/bowl)[^sn-bowl] or strokes; open counters connect to the outside, and the width of that opening is the [Aperture](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/aperture).

The name comes from metal type, where the counter was struck into the matrix by a *counterpunch*, a separate punch used to shape the interior before the outline of the letter was cut. Punchcutters like [Claude Garamond](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/claude-garamond)[^sn-claude-garamond] and [William Caslon](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/william-caslon) thought about the inside of a letter as carefully as the outside.

## Why It Matters

Counters carry legibility. Generous counters stay open when ink spreads on cheap paper or when pixels blur at small sizes; tight counters fill in. Faces for difficult conditions, like Matthew Carter's Bell Centennial for telephone directories, use *ink traps*, small notches cut into joints so that the spreading ink does not clog the counters. At the other end, the large counters of [Verdana](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/verdana) and [Georgia](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/georgia) were part of their design for coarse screens (see [Screen Typography and Hinting](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/screen-typography-and-hinting)).

Counters also set the rhythm of a line. Good spacing balances the white *inside* letters with the white *between* them; this is the foundation of [Kerning](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/kerning) and [Tracking](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/tracking), and one reason Bringhurst insists that spacing is part of the typeface, not something added afterward (see [The Elements of Typographic Style](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/the-elements-of-typographic-style)).
