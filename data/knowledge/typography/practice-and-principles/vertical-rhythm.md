# Vertical Rhythm

> *A page keeps time the way a drummer does: everything lands on the grid.*

---

## The Beat

Vertical rhythm is the practice of building every vertical measurement on a page from a single unit, usually the line height of the body text (see [Leading](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/leading)). Headings, paragraph spacing, images, and block quotes take up whole multiples (or simple fractions) of that unit, so that lines of text stay on a consistent baseline grid down the page and across columns.

## Why It Works

When lines in adjacent columns align and the spacing between elements is proportional, the page feels calm and ordered even if the reader cannot say why. Bringhurst borrows the language of music for exactly this: the page has a scale, and its intervals should relate (see [The Elements of Typographic Style](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/the-elements-of-typographic-style)[^sn-the-elements-of-typographic-style] and [Page Canons](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/page-canons)). It is the visual equivalent of [Cadence](/knowledge/key-concepts/cadence), the rhythm underneath sentences, music, and drumming alike, and of the steady grid of a groove in [Music](/knowledge/music).

## In Practice

In print, layout programs offer a baseline grid to snap text to. On the web, strict baseline grids are harder because of how CSS distributes line height around text, but the principle survives: choose a base line height, express margins and padding in that unit, and size headings so their line heights stay on the rhythm. The newer CSS `text-box-trim` properties make true baseline alignment easier. Strict rhythm can be relaxed for display type and images, as long as the body text returns to the beat.
