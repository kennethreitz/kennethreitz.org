# Readability and Legibility

> *Legibility is whether you can read it; readability is whether you want to keep going.*

---

## Two Words

Typographers distinguish *legibility*, how easily individual characters can be recognized, from *readability*, how comfortably a whole text can be read at length. Legibility belongs mostly to the typeface; readability belongs mostly to the typesetting.

## Legibility

Legible faces make letters distinct from one another. The features that matter most are open [apertures](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/aperture), generous [counters](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/counter), a sensible [X-Height](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/x-height), clear differences between similar characters (**I**, **l**, **1**; **O** and **0**), and moderate [Stroke Contrast](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/stroke-contrast)[^sn-stroke-contrast] that survives the conditions of use. This is why [Humanist Sans](/knowledge/typography/classification/humanist-sans) faces like [Frutiger](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/frutiger) and [Verdana](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/verdana) were designed for signs and screens.

## Readability

Readability depends on size, [Measure](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/measure), [Leading](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/leading),[^sn-leading] spacing, contrast with the background, and the rhythm of the text block. A highly legible face can still be unreadable when set too small, too wide, or too tight.

## Myths and Evidence

Many claims, that serif faces are always more readable in print, or sans serifs on screens, have weak evidence; familiarity, rendering quality, and good typesetting matter more than any single feature (see [Serif](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/serif)). What reliably helps is the boring stuff: adequate size, a comfortable measure, enough leading, and good contrast.
