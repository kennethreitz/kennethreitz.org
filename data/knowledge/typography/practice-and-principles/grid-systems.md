# Grid Systems

> *The grid is invisible scaffolding: the reader sees only the order it leaves behind.*

---

## The Structure

A grid divides a page or screen into columns, rows, gutters, and margins, giving every element a place to align to. Grids long predate modernism: medieval scribes ruled their pages, and the proportions of early printed books follow careful constructions (see [Page Canons](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/page-canons)). But the grid as a flexible, modular system for whole publications is the achievement of the [Swiss International Style](/knowledge/typography/history/swiss-international-style).

## The Swiss Grid

[Josef Müller-Brockmann](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/josef-muller-brockmann)'s [Grid Systems in Graphic Design](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/grid-systems-in-graphic-design) (1981) is the classic statement. A modular grid divides the area into fields separated by consistent intervals, usually built from the [Leading](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/leading) of the body text so that the grid and the [Vertical Rhythm](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/vertical-rhythm)[^sn-vertical-rhythm] coincide. Text, images, and white space then occupy one or more fields, producing variety within a consistent order.

## Grids on Screens

Web and interface design adopted the grid wholesale: twelve-column layout frameworks, CSS Grid, and the 4 or 8 point spacing systems of modern design systems are direct descendants. On screens the grid must be fluid, adapting to many widths, which is why modern grids are defined in proportions and constraints rather than fixed measurements.

## In Practice

Let the [Measure](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/measure)[^sn-measure] of the text set the column width, not the other way round; derive gutters and margins from the type size; and break the grid deliberately when something deserves emphasis. The constraint is the point, in the sense of [Constraints as Liberation](/knowledge/key-concepts/constraints-as-liberation).
