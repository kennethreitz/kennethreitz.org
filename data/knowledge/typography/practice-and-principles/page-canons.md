# Page Canons

> *The old books were built like cathedrals: by proportion, not by measurement.*

---

## The Problem

Where should the text block sit on a page? Medieval scribes and early printers answered with geometric constructions that relate the text area to the page through simple proportions, so that the margins feel inevitable rather than arbitrary.

## The Van de Graaf Canon

The best known construction, reconstructed by J. A. van de Graaf in the 1940s and popularized by [Jan Tschichold](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/jan-tschichold)[^sn-jan-tschichold] (see [The Form of the Book](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/the-form-of-the-book)), works by drawing diagonals across a two-page spread and across each page. Where they intersect, the text block is fixed. On a page of proportion 2:3, the result places a text block with the same proportions as the page, inner margin one ninth of the page width, top margin one ninth of the height, and outer and bottom margins twice those, so that the margins run in the ratio 2:3:4:6 and the text block's height equals the page's width. Tschichold found similar proportions in medieval manuscripts and early printed books.

## Proportion and Harmony

Tschichold and Bringhurst both connect page proportions to wider harmonic systems. Bringhurst (see [The Elements of Typographic Style](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/the-elements-of-typographic-style))[^sn-the-elements-of-typographic-style] relates page shapes to musical intervals, the 2:3 page to the fifth and 3:4 to the fourth, and gives special attention to the golden section, the ratio of about 1:1.618 at the heart of [the golden ratio](/knowledge/sacred-geometry/golden-ratio) and its approximation in the [Fibonacci Sequence](/knowledge/sacred-geometry/fibonacci-sequence), studied in [Sacred Geometry](/knowledge/sacred-geometry). Whether or not a reader knows any of this, they feel when a page is balanced.

## Today

Screens make fixed page proportions rare, but the lessons translate: generous margins, a text block in proportion to its container, and a [Measure](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/measure) set by reading comfort.
