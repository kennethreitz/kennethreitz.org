# Small Caps

> *Capitals that know how to speak quietly.*

---

## True Small Caps

Small capitals are capital letterforms drawn at roughly the [X-Height](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/x-height)[^sn-x-height] of the lowercase, with stroke weights matched to the surrounding text. They let capitals sit inside a line without shouting. True small caps are separately designed [glyphs](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/glyph); *fake* small caps, made by shrinking full capitals, come out too thin and too light, which is why typographers avoid them.

## Uses

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Acronyms and initialisms | NASA, HTML, set in small caps with a little [Tracking](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/tracking) |
| Lead-ins | The first words of a chapter or section |
| Names and references | Author names in bibliographies, speaker names in plays |
| Era and time abbreviations | AD, BC, AM, PM |

Bringhurst recommends small caps for acronyms in running text, since full capitals punch holes in the texture of a paragraph (see [The Elements of Typographic Style](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/the-elements-of-typographic-style)).

## In Digital Type

OpenType provides small caps through the `smcp` feature (lowercase to small caps) and `c2sc` (capitals to small caps), both available in CSS through `font-variant-caps` (see [OpenType Features](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/opentype-features)). Good text families such as [Garamond](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/garamond) and [Latin Modern](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/latin-modern) include them; many system fonts do not.
