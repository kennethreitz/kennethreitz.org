# Typographic Hierarchy

> *The reader should know what matters before reading a word.*

---

## Structure Made Visible

Typographic hierarchy is the ordering of text by importance, made visible. Titles, headings, body, captions, and notes each need a distinct voice, so that a reader can see the shape of a document at a glance and navigate it without effort. Done well, hierarchy disappears into comprehension; done badly, everything shouts or everything whispers.

## The Tools

| Tool | Effect |
|---|---|
| Size | The strongest signal; use a consistent scale rather than arbitrary sizes |
| Weight | Bold and light distinguish without changing size |
| Style | Italic, [Small Caps](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/small-caps), or capitals with [Tracking](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/tracking) |
| Space | White space above a heading binds it to what follows |
| Position | Indentation, alignment, and placement on the [grid](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/grid-systems) |
| Color | Tone and contrast, used sparingly |

Classical book typography, as described in [The Elements of Typographic Style](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/the-elements-of-typographic-style),[^sn-the-elements-of-typographic-style] uses few levels and subtle distinctions, often changing only one property at a time. Modernist design (see [Swiss International Style](/knowledge/typography/history/swiss-international-style) and [Die neue Typographie](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/die-neue-typographie)) makes hierarchy bolder and more spatial. Both agree that a small number of clearly distinct levels beats many fuzzy ones.

## In This Vault

Kenneth's essay [Visual Hierarchy and the Shape of Attention](/essays/2025-09-19-visual-hierarchy-and-the-shape-of-attention) treats hierarchy as the shape of the reader's attention, and the Reitz LaTeX theme follows LaTeX's measured heading ratios so that structure, not decoration, does the work (see [Draft to Document Why I Made My Notes Look Like LaTeX Papers](/essays/2026-11-04-draft_to_document)). [The Interface Is the Subconscious](/essays/2026-03-20-the_interface_is_the_subconscious) makes the same point about interfaces: hierarchy is felt before it is noticed.
