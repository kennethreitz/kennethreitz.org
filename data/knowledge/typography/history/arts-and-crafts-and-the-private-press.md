# Arts and Crafts and the Private Press

> *When industry cheapened the book, a few people decided to make beautiful ones by hand.*

---

## The Reaction

By the late nineteenth century commercial printing had become cheap and fast but often poor: thin types, bad paper, gray ink, crowded pages. The Arts and Crafts movement, led by [William Morris](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/william-morris), rejected industrial shoddiness in favor of honest handwork, and in printing it produced the *private press movement*.

## The Presses

| Press | Founded | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Kelmscott Press | 1891 | Morris's press; the Golden Type after [Nicolas Jenson](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/nicolas-jenson); the Kelmscott Chaucer (1896) |
| Doves Press | 1900 | T. J. Cobden-Sanderson and Emery Walker; the austere Doves Type and the Doves Bible |
| Ashendene Press | 1895 | C. H. St John Hornby; fine editions of Dante and others |

The Doves Type has a famous coda: after a bitter dispute with Walker, Cobden-Sanderson threw the type into the River Thames around 1916 to 1917 rather than let his partner have it. Pieces were recovered by divers in 2015 and the face has been digitally revived.

## Legacy

The private presses revived interest in the history of type and the quality of the whole book: paper, ink, margins (see [Page Canons](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/page-canons)). Their influence passed to [Edward Johnston](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/edward-johnston)'s calligraphy revival, to the scholarly revivals of [Stanley Morison](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/stanley-morison),[^sn-stanley-morison] and in reaction, to the modernists of the [New Typography](/knowledge/typography/history/bauhaus-and-the-new-typography), who rejected the medievalism while keeping the seriousness.
