# The Aldine Press

> *The small book in your pocket was invented in Venice.*

---

## The House

The Aldine Press was the printing and publishing house founded in Venice in the mid 1490s by the humanist [Aldus Manutius](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/aldus-manutius),[^sn-aldus-manutius] continued after his death in 1515 by his family, including his son Paolo and grandson Aldus the Younger, until the end of the sixteenth century.

## What It Did

The press's mission was scholarly: to print accurate editions of the Greek and Latin classics. Its achievements reshaped the book:

| Innovation | Detail |
|---|---|
| The Aldine roman | Cut by [Francesco Griffo](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/francesco-griffo) for *De Aetna* (1495 to 1496), the ancestor of [Old-Style](/knowledge/typography/classification/old-style) type and of [Bembo](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/bembo) |
| The semicolon | An early printed use of the modern form appears in *De Aetna* |
| Italic type | Cut by Griffo from humanist cursive, first used for Virgil (1501) |
| The octavo | Small, portable editions of the classics for private reading |
| Greek type | Editions of Aristotle and many other Greek authors |

## Legacy

The *Hypnerotomachia Poliphili* (1499) set a standard for the integration of text and image. The press's dolphin and anchor device is still widely imitated, and the [Old-Style](/knowledge/typography/classification/old-style) romans of [Claude Garamond](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/claude-garamond) carried the Aldine letter across Europe.
