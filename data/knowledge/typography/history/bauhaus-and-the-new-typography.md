# Bauhaus and the New Typography

> *Typography for the machine age: asymmetry, sans serif, and no apologies to the past.*

---

## The School

The Bauhaus was founded by Walter Gropius in Weimar in 1919 and moved to Dessau in 1925, then briefly to Berlin before closing under Nazi pressure in 1933. It aimed to unite art, craft, and industry. In typography, László Moholy-Nagy and Herbert Bayer pushed toward a functional, photographic, machine-age graphic language. Bayer's *universal* alphabet of 1925 went so far as to abolish capital letters.

## The New Typography

The movement's most systematic voice was [Jan Tschichold](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/jan-tschichold), who had seen the 1923 Bauhaus exhibition. His special issue *elementare typographie* (1925) and book [Die neue Typographie](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/die-neue-typographie) (1928) laid out the program: asymmetric layouts in place of the centered axis, sans serif type (especially the new [Geometric Sans](/knowledge/typography/classification/geometric-sans) faces), clear [Typographic Hierarchy](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/typographic-hierarchy),[^sn-typographic-hierarchy] standardized paper sizes, and photography in place of illustration. [Paul Renner](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/paul-renner)'s [Futura](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/futura) (1927) was the movement's commercial typeface.

## Aftermath

The Nazi regime suppressed the movement; its leaders emigrated, and Tschichold himself later turned against its dogmatism. But its ideas flowed into the [Swiss International Style](/knowledge/typography/history/swiss-international-style) after the war and from there into modern corporate, editorial, and interface design.
