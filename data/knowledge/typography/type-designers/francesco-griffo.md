# Francesco Griffo

> *The hand behind the Aldine books, and the first to slant the letter.*

---

## The Punchcutter

Francesco Griffo of Bologna was the punchcutter who gave [Aldus Manutius](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/aldus-manutius) his types. He cut the roman first used in *De Aetna* (1495 to 1496), the ancestor of the [Old-Style](/knowledge/typography/classification/old-style) romans of [Claude Garamond](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/claude-garamond)[^sn-claude-garamond] and the basis of the twentieth-century [Bembo](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/bembo); Greek types for the Aldine editions of the classics; and, around 1500 to 1501, the first italic type, based on the humanist cursive handwriting of the day (see [Script](/knowledge/typography/classification/script)).

## Quarrel and Disappearance

Griffo resented that Aldus held the privileges to the italic and took credit for it, and he left Venice after a dispute. He later printed small books in his own italic at Fano and Bologna. Around 1518, according to the surviving records, he was charged with killing his son-in-law, and he disappears from history after that.

## Legacy

Griffo's roman defined what a book page looked like for centuries, and his italic began a separate tradition that took decades to be paired with the roman as a single family.
