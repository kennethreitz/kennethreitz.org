# Beatrice Warde

> *Printing should be invisible.*

---

## The Scholar

Beatrice Warde was an American typographic scholar who spent most of her career in England as publicity manager for the Monotype Corporation and editor of *The Monotype Recorder*. In 1926, writing under the pseudonym Paul Beaujon in *The Fleuron*, she showed that types widely attributed to [Claude Garamond](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/claude-garamond)[^sn-claude-garamond] were actually the work of Jean Jannon, a discovery that reshaped the history of the [Garamond](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/garamond) revivals.

## The Crystal Goblet

Her most famous work is a lecture delivered in London in 1930 and later published as [The Crystal Goblet](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/the-crystal-goblet), which argues that typography should be like a clear crystal glass, revealing the wine rather than drawing attention to itself. It remains the classic statement of the view that type serves the text.

## This Is a Printing Office

In 1932 she wrote a short text celebrating printing, beginning "This is a printing office, crossroads of civilization," set as a broadside in Perpetua capitals. It has been cast in bronze and posted in print shops ever since. She worked closely with [Stanley Morison](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/stanley-morison) throughout her career.
