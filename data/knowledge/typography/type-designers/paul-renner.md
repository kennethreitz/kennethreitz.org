# Paul Renner

> *The modernist who would not bend to the regime that loved his typeface.*

---

## The Designer

Paul Renner was a German painter and book designer who became one of the central figures of modern German typography. He was never part of the Bauhaus, but he shared its goal of a type for the machine age, and in [Futura](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/futura)[^sn-futura] (1927) he produced its most successful realization (see [Geometric Sans](/knowledge/typography/classification/geometric-sans) and [Bauhaus and the New Typography](/knowledge/typography/history/bauhaus-and-the-new-typography)).

## Teacher and Writer

From 1926 Renner led the printing trades school in Munich, where [Jan Tschichold](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/jan-tschichold) taught under him. He wrote extensively on typography, including *Typografie als Kunst* (1922) and *Die Kunst der Typographie* (1939), arguing for a modernism grounded in craft and tradition rather than slogans.

## Against the Regime

Renner publicly criticized Nazi cultural policy in a 1932 pamphlet, and in 1933 he was arrested and removed from his post. Ironically, Futura remained widely used in Germany, and the regime later turned against [Blackletter](/knowledge/typography/classification/blackletter), the very letter its propaganda had promoted.
