# Johannes Gutenberg

> *A goldsmith in Mainz built a machine for multiplying words, and the world reorganized itself around it.*

---

## The Inventor

Johannes Gutenberg was a goldsmith of Mainz who, around the 1440s and early 1450s, developed a complete system for printing with movable metal type (see [Movable Type](/knowledge/typography/history/movable-type)). Movable type had existed in East Asia for centuries, but Gutenberg's combination of inventions made it fast, precise, and scalable: an adjustable hand mould for casting identical letters quickly, a metal alloy that cast cleanly, an oil-based ink that would hold to metal, and a screw press adapted to print evenly.

## The Bible

His great work is the 42-line Bible, completed around 1455, printed in a [Blackletter](/knowledge/typography/classification/blackletter)[^sn-blackletter] Textura type designed to rival the finest manuscripts. To match the even texture of a scribe's page, his type included hundreds of [sorts](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/glyph), with many [ligatures](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/ligature) and abbreviations, and his lines are fully justified, a feat of [Justification and Hyphenation](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/justification-and-hyphenation) by hand. Around 180 copies were printed, on paper and vellum.

## Business and Legacy

Gutenberg financed the work with loans from the merchant Johann Fust, who sued him in 1455 and took over much of the printing operation with Gutenberg's skilled assistant Peter Schöffer. Late in life he was granted a pension by the Archbishop of Mainz. Within fifty years printing had spread across Europe (see [Incunabula](/knowledge/typography/history/incunabula)), and the speed with which ideas could now travel made possible, among much else, [The Reformation](/knowledge/spirituality/protestantism/the-reformation).[^sn-the-reformation]
