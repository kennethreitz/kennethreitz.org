# John Baskerville

> *A perfectionist with money, which is a rare and productive combination.*

---

## The Printer

John Baskerville was a Birmingham writing master who made a fortune in japanning (lacquerware) and then spent it on printing. His first book, an edition of Virgil, appeared in 1757. He was appointed printer to Cambridge University and produced a celebrated folio Bible in 1763.

## Method

Baskerville rethought the whole craft. He improved the press, developed a darker and glossier ink, used smooth wove paper, and pressed his printed sheets to give them a satin finish. His type, [Baskerville](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/baskerville), is the classic English [Transitional](/knowledge/typography/classification/transitional) face, with a more vertical axis and higher [Stroke Contrast](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/stroke-contrast) than the [Caslon](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/caslon) it displaced at the high end of the trade.

## Influence

Baskerville was admired abroad more than at home. After his death his widow sold his punches and matrices to France, where they were used for an edition of Voltaire; they eventually returned to England and were given to Cambridge University Press in 1953. His work inspired [Giambattista Bodoni](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/giambattista-bodoni)[^sn-giambattista-bodoni] and the [Didone](/knowledge/typography/classification/didone) style.
