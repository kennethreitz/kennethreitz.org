# Eric Gill

> *The letters are clean. The man was not.*

---

## The Letterer

Eric Gill trained as a letter cutter under the influence of [Edward Johnston](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/edward-johnston) and became one of the most celebrated sculptors and stone carvers in Britain, with works including the sculpture over the entrance of the BBC's Broadcasting House. Encouraged by [Stanley Morison](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/stanley-morison) at Monotype, he designed typefaces: [Gill Sans](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/gill-sans) (1928), the roman Perpetua, and Joanna, which he used to set his own *An Essay on Typography* (1931).

## The Work

Gill's types carry the discipline of the chisel: classical proportions, crisp terminals, and a carver's sense of the letter as an object. Gill Sans[^sn-gill-sans] made the [Humanist Sans](/knowledge/typography/classification/humanist-sans) a mainstream face and remains one of the defining British typefaces.

## The Diaries

Fiona MacCarthy's 1989 biography, drawing on Gill's own diaries, revealed that he had sexually abused two of his daughters, among other abuses. The revelation changed how his work is discussed. Institutions holding his art and designers using his faces now have to decide how to hold the beauty of the work alongside the harm of the man, and many choose to name the history openly rather than look away from either.
