# Jan Tschichold

> *He wrote the manifesto of modern typography, then spent his life arguing with it.*

---

## The Modernist

Jan Tschichold was born in Leipzig, the son of a sign painter, and trained in calligraphy and lettering. After seeing the Bauhaus exhibition of 1923 he became the most articulate advocate of the new, asymmetric typography, publishing a famous special issue, *elementare typographie*, in 1925, and the book [Die neue Typographie](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/die-neue-typographie) in 1928 (see [Bauhaus and the New Typography](/knowledge/typography/history/bauhaus-and-the-new-typography)). He argued for asymmetric layouts, sans serif type, standardized paper sizes, and typography built for function.

## Exile and Reversal

In 1933 the Nazis arrested him as a "cultural Bolshevik," and after his release he emigrated to Basel. In exile he came to see the dogmatic modernism he had championed as having something in common with authoritarianism, and returned to classical, centered book typography, which drew sharp criticism from younger Swiss modernists (see [Swiss International Style](/knowledge/typography/history/swiss-international-style)).

## Penguin and After

From 1947 to 1949 he redesigned Penguin Books in London, writing the *Penguin Composition Rules* and standardizing hundreds of books. His later essays, collected in English as [The Form of the Book](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/the-form-of-the-book), include his research on [Page Canons](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/page-canons). His typeface Sabon (1967) was designed to work identically on hand, Monotype, and Linotype composition, an [Old-Style](/knowledge/typography/classification/old-style)[^sn-old-style] revival in the manner of [Garamond](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/garamond).
