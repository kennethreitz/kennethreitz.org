# Edward Johnston

> *He picked up a broad-edged pen and gave the twentieth century its letters back.*

---

## The Calligrapher

Edward Johnston, born in Uruguay and raised in Britain, is the father of modern calligraphy. Studying medieval manuscripts at the British Museum, he rediscovered how the broad-edged pen, cut and held at a consistent angle, produced the letterforms of the historic scripts. His book *Writing & Illuminating, & Lettering* (1906) became the foundational manual of the revival, and he taught at the Central School of Arts and Crafts in London, where [Eric Gill](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/eric-gill) was among his students.

## The Underground

In 1916, commissioned by Frank Pick of the London Underground, Johnston designed a sans serif alphabet based on the proportions of classical Roman capitals. It was the first great [Humanist Sans](/knowledge/typography/classification/humanist-sans), and a version of it still signs London's transport network today. [Gill Sans](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/gill-sans) grew directly from it.

## Influence

Johnston's students and readers spread his ideas across Europe, and his understanding of the pen's logic shaped later thinking about [Stroke Contrast](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/stroke-contrast)[^sn-stroke-contrast] and letter structure, including the work of [Hermann Zapf](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/hermann-zapf) and [Jan Tschichold](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/jan-tschichold).
