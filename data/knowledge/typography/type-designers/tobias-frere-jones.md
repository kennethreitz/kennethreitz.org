# Tobias Frere-Jones

> *He reads the street for letters nobody thought were type.*

---

## The Designer

Tobias Frere-Jones studied at the Rhode Island School of Design and worked at the Font Bureau in Boston in the 1990s, where he designed Interstate (1993), based on the lettering of American highway signs, and a range of other faces.

## Hoefler & Frere-Jones

From 1999 he was a partner with [Jonathan Hoefler](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/jonathan-hoefler)[^sn-jonathan-hoefler] in Hoefler & Frere-Jones, where he designed [Gotham](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/gotham) (2000), drawn from the vernacular lettering of New York buildings, as well as Whitney and Retina, a face for tiny financial tables in The Wall Street Journal. The partnership ended in a 2014 dispute.

## Frere-Jones Type

He founded his own studio, Frere-Jones Type, in 2015, and has written thoughtfully about type history and vernacular lettering. His work is a sustained argument that good type can come from engineering and the street as much as from the scriptorium.
