# Hermann Zapf

> *A calligrapher who saw the computer coming and walked out to meet it.*

---

## The Calligrapher

Hermann Zapf was born in Nuremberg and taught himself calligraphy from the books of [Edward Johnston](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/edward-johnston)[^sn-edward-johnston] and Rudolf Koch. Working for the Stempel foundry after the Second World War, he designed some of the most widely used typefaces of the century, including [Palatino](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/palatino) (1949), Melior (1952), and [Optima](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/optima) (1958), and later Zapf Dingbats and Zapf Chancery for ITC and the calligraphic Zapfino (1998), a showcase of what digital [Script](/knowledge/typography/classification/script) fonts could do. His wife, Gudrun Zapf-von Hesse, was a distinguished type designer in her own right.

## Toward the Computer

Zapf was one of the first type designers to take computers seriously, lecturing on computer typesetting from the 1960s. He collaborated with [Donald Knuth](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/donald-knuth) at Stanford, designing the AMS Euler math alphabet for the American Mathematical Society (see [Typesetting Math with TeX](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/typesetting-math-with-tex)). With the firm URW he developed the hz-program, a system for [Microtypography](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/microtypography) that adjusted letter widths and spacing for better justified text, ideas that later influenced Adobe InDesign's paragraph composer.

## Copies

His popularity made his faces among the most copied, and he fought for years against unauthorized versions, especially of Palatino.
