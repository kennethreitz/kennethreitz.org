# William Caslon

> *England stopped importing its letters.*

---

## The Founder

William Caslon began as an engraver of gun locks and barrels in London and turned to cutting type in the 1720s, encouraged by printers who admired his lettering. His foundry, established in London, produced romans, italics, and exotic types including Arabic and Hebrew. His specimen sheet of 1734 displayed a complete range of sizes and established him as the leading founder in Britain.

## The Type

[Caslon](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/caslon) is a sturdy [Old-Style](/knowledge/typography/classification/old-style)[^sn-old-style] face built on Dutch models of the seventeenth century, practical rather than refined, and enormously popular across the British Empire and the American colonies. For the first time English printers did not need to buy their type from Holland.

## The Dynasty

The Caslon foundry passed through his descendants and continued under the family name into the twentieth century. His types were revived in the 1840s by the Chiswick Press, one of the first deliberate historical revivals in printing, and remain in use in many digital versions.
