# Firmin Didot

> *A family business that set the point, and the style, for a continent.*

---

## The Family

The Didots were a Parisian dynasty of printers, publishers, papermakers, and typefounders. François-Ambroise Didot (1730 to 1804) reformed the system of type measurement into what became the European *Didot point* (see [Points and Picas](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/points-and-picas)). His son Firmin Didot cut the types that define the French [Didone](/knowledge/typography/classification/didone).[^sn-didone]

## The Type

Firmin Didot's romans, from the 1780s onward, have extreme [Stroke Contrast](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/stroke-contrast), hairline unbracketed [serifs](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/serif), and a strictly vertical axis, a crisper and lighter counterpart to the work of [Giambattista Bodoni](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/giambattista-bodoni) (see [Didot](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/didot)). They became the standard look of French printing in the Napoleonic era. Firmin Didot also worked on stereotyping, the casting of whole pages as plates for reprinting.

## Legacy

The Didot name lives on in the point system and in the fashion world's love of high-contrast type, revived for Harper's Bazaar in HTF Didot by [Jonathan Hoefler](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/jonathan-hoefler).
