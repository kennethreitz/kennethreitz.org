# Aldus Manutius

> *Make haste slowly: the dolphin and the anchor.*

---

## The Publisher

Aldus Manutius was a humanist scholar and teacher who founded a press in Venice in the mid 1490s with the aim of putting the Greek classics into print (see [The Aldine Press](/knowledge/typography/history/the-aldine-press)). He published first editions of Aristotle and many other Greek authors, set by the finest punchcutter of the age, [Francesco Griffo](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/francesco-griffo).[^sn-francesco-griffo]

## Innovations

The Aldine Press introduced the small *octavo* format for classical texts, books meant to be carried and read anywhere rather than consulted at a lectern. To fit more text on these smaller pages, Griffo cut the first italic type, based on a humanist cursive hand, used for an edition of Virgil in 1501. Aldus's printing of Pietro Bembo's *De Aetna* (1495 to 1496) introduced the roman type revived in the twentieth century as [Bembo](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/bembo)[^sn-bembo] and is remembered for an early form of the modern semicolon. He also published the *Hypnerotomachia Poliphili* (1499), often called the most beautiful book of the Renaissance.

## The Device

The Aldine printer's mark, a dolphin wrapped around an anchor with the motto *festina lente* ("make haste slowly"), is one of the most famous in printing history and is still borrowed by publishers today. The name *Garalde*, used for [Old-Style](/knowledge/typography/classification/old-style) type, honors Aldus alongside [Claude Garamond](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/claude-garamond).
