# Nicolas Jenson

> *The first great roman was cut by a Frenchman in Venice.*

---

## The Printer

Nicolas Jenson was a French engraver who, according to a later tradition, had been sent to Mainz to learn the new art of printing. By 1470 he was printing in Venice, then the busiest printing city in Europe, and that year he issued a book set in a roman type of extraordinary clarity and evenness.

## The Roman

Jenson's roman is the defining [Humanist Serif](/knowledge/typography/classification/humanist-serif): it pairs capitals modeled on Roman inscriptions with a lowercase based on humanist handwriting, and sets them into a dark, even, harmonious texture. It is often called the first fully mature roman type, and it was the model for revivals four centuries later, above all [William Morris](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/william-morris)'s Golden Type and Bruce Rogers' Centaur (see [Arts and Crafts and the Private Press](/knowledge/typography/history/arts-and-crafts-and-the-private-press)).

## Legacy

Jenson also cut gothic types for the legal and religious books that still demanded them, and his business prospered. Within a generation, [Aldus Manutius](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/aldus-manutius) and [Francesco Griffo](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/francesco-griffo)[^sn-francesco-griffo] would develop the roman further into the [Old-Style](/knowledge/typography/classification/old-style) tradition.
