# William Morris

> *Have nothing in your houses that you do not know to be useful, or believe to be beautiful.*

---

## The Designer

William Morris was a designer of wallpapers, textiles, and furniture, a poet, a translator of the sagas, and a socialist, and the leading figure of the Arts and Crafts movement, which rejected shoddy industrial production in favor of honest handcraft (see [Arts and Crafts and the Private Press](/knowledge/typography/history/arts-and-crafts-and-the-private-press)).

## The Kelmscott Press

Inspired by a lecture on early printing by his friend Emery Walker in 1888, Morris founded the Kelmscott Press in 1891. He designed three types: the Golden Type, a heavy roman based on [Nicolas Jenson](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/nicolas-jenson)'s, and two [Blackletter](/knowledge/typography/classification/blackletter) faces, Troy and Chaucer. The press's masterpiece, the Kelmscott Chaucer of 1896, with illustrations by Edward Burne-Jones, is dense, decorated, and medieval in spirit.

## Influence

Morris's books were deliberately backward-looking, and later modernists found them overwrought. But he reawakened interest in the history and quality of printing, launched the private press movement, and inspired the revival of historic type that [Stanley Morison](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/stanley-morison)[^sn-stanley-morison] and others carried out in the twentieth century. The idea that everyday objects deserve care is close kin to this vault's [For Humans](/knowledge/philosophy/for-humans).
