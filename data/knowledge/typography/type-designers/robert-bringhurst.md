# Robert Bringhurst

> *Typography exists to honor content.*

---

## The Poet

Robert Bringhurst is a Canadian poet, translator, and typographer. Born in Los Angeles and raised in the American and Canadian West, he studied architecture, linguistics, and physics before turning to poetry, and he has translated classical Haida oral literature into English.

## The Book

His [The Elements of Typographic Style](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/the-elements-of-typographic-style)[^sn-the-elements-of-typographic-style] (1992, revised through version 4.0 in 2012) is widely regarded as the finest modern guide to the craft, admired as much for its prose as for its rules. It treats typography as a living tradition stretching back through the Renaissance to the scribes, and every rule in it is tied to the reader's experience: the comfortable [Measure](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/measure), the proper use of [Small Caps](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/small-caps) and [Figures](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/figures), [Hanging Punctuation](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/hanging-punctuation), and the scale of the page drawn from music and geometry (see [Page Canons](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/page-canons)).

## Influence

Bringhurst's book became the touchstone for a generation of designers, including those who carried classical typography onto the web; Matthew Butterick's [Practical Typography](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/practical-typography) and many web typography guides acknowledge it.
