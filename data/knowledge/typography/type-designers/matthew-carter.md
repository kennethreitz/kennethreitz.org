# Matthew Carter

> *A designer who cut metal punches by hand and then drew the fonts of the early web.*

---

## The Designer

Matthew Carter is the son of the typographic historian Harry Carter. He learned punchcutting at the Enschedé foundry in the Netherlands in the 1950s, one of the last people to learn the craft before it disappeared, and went on to work through every major type technology since: hot metal, [Phototypesetting](/knowledge/typography/history/phototypesetting),[^sn-phototypesetting] and digital.

## The Work

His typefaces include Snell Roundhand (1965, see [Script](/knowledge/typography/classification/script)), Bell Centennial (designed for AT&T's telephone directories, with ink traps to keep its [counters](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/counter) open on cheap paper), Galliard, Charter, Big Caslon, and the screen fonts he drew for Microsoft in the 1990s, [Verdana](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/verdana), [Georgia](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/georgia), and Tahoma, which were designed pixel first and became the core fonts of the early web (see [Web Fonts](/knowledge/typography/history/web-fonts) and [Screen Typography and Hinting](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/screen-typography-and-hinting)).

## Bitstream and After

In 1981 he co-founded Bitstream, one of the first independent digital type foundries, and later Carter & Cone. In 2010 he received a MacArthur Fellowship, a rare honor for a type designer.
