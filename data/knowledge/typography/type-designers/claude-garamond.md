# Claude Garamond

> *A punchcutter's name became a genre, and then a misunderstanding.*

---

## The Punchcutter

Claude Garamond was a Parisian punchcutter, one of the first to work as an independent type designer rather than as a printer who cut his own type. Building on the Aldine roman of [Francesco Griffo](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/francesco-griffo),[^sn-francesco-griffo] he cut romans of such balance and refinement that they set the standard for European book typography for two centuries (see [Old-Style](/knowledge/typography/classification/old-style)). He also cut the *Grecs du roi*, Greek types commissioned by King Francis I, for the scholar-printer Robert Estienne.

## After His Death

Garamond died in 1561 without great wealth. His punches and matrices were sold off, and many ended up with Christophe Plantin in Antwerp, whose printing house, now the Plantin-Moretus Museum, still preserves them. Through Plantin and the Dutch founders his designs spread across Europe.

## The Name

Later revivalists used the name [Garamond](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/garamond) for faces that turned out to be based on the 1615 types of Jean Jannon, a confusion exposed by [Beatrice Warde](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/beatrice-warde) in the 1920s. The Vox-ATypI word *Garalde* honors him alongside [Aldus Manutius](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/aldus-manutius).
