# Adrian Frutiger

> *He designed for metal, for film, and for screens, and the letters stayed good in all three.*

---

## The Designer

Adrian Frutiger was born in Unterseen, Switzerland, trained as a compositor, and moved to Paris in 1952 to work for the foundry Deberny & Peignot. There he designed [Univers](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/univers)[^sn-univers] (1957), a systematically planned [Neo-Grotesque](/knowledge/typography/classification/neo-grotesque) family whose numbered weight system reorganized how type families are named.

## Across Technologies

Frutiger's career spanned three technologies. He adapted classic faces for the Lumitype photosetting machine (see [Phototypesetting](/knowledge/typography/history/phototypesetting)), designed OCR-B (1968), a machine-readable alphabet that remains a standard, and drew faces that made the transition to digital. His signage alphabet for Charles de Gaulle Airport became the typeface [Frutiger](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/frutiger) (1976), the model [Humanist Sans](/knowledge/typography/classification/humanist-sans) for wayfinding, and his Avenir (1988) gave the [Geometric Sans](/knowledge/typography/classification/geometric-sans) a more humane form.

## Legacy

Frutiger thought of type as something felt more than seen: a good letter, he argued, should be so natural that the reader forgets it, a view close to [Beatrice Warde](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/beatrice-warde)'s [The Crystal Goblet](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/the-crystal-goblet).
