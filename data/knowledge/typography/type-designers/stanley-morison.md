# Stanley Morison

> *The scholar who decided what the twentieth century would read.*

---

## The Adviser

Stanley Morison was largely self-taught, a typographic historian and adviser whose influence on twentieth-century printing was immense. As typographic adviser to the Monotype Corporation from 1923, and to Cambridge University Press, he directed a program of revivals that put the great historic faces onto the machines of [Hot Metal Typesetting](/knowledge/typography/history/hot-metal-typesetting): among them [Bembo](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/bembo),[^sn-bembo] [Baskerville](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/baskerville), Fournier, and Poliphilus, alongside new faces like [Eric Gill](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/eric-gill)'s [Gill Sans](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/gill-sans) and Perpetua.

## The Times

In 1929 Morison criticized the typography of The Times, and the paper responded by hiring him to fix it. The result was [Times New Roman](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/times-new-roman), drawn by Victor Lardent under his direction, first used in 1932.

## Principles

His essay *First Principles of Typography* (1930) argued that typography is essentially utilitarian, the efficient means to an end, and that the reader's convenience should rule, a position close to [Beatrice Warde](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/beatrice-warde)'s [The Crystal Goblet](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/the-crystal-goblet). He also edited the influential journal *The Fleuron* in the 1920s.
