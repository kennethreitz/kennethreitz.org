# The Form of the Book

> *The essays of a modernist who came home to the classical page.*

---

## The Book

*The Form of the Book: Essays on the Morality of Good Design* collects [Jan Tschichold](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/jan-tschichold)'s later writings on book design, written after he had turned away from the doctrines of [Die neue Typographie](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/die-neue-typographie). The English edition of 1991 was edited by [Robert Bringhurst](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/robert-bringhurst).

## Contents

The essays argue for classical, centered book typography as the most serviceable form for long reading, and address the details: the proportions of the page and the text block (see [Page Canons](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/page-canons), including Tschichold's account of the construction recovered by Van de Graaf), margins, title pages, indents instead of blank lines between paragraphs, the proper use of italics and [Small Caps](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/small-caps),[^sn-small-caps] and his work at Penguin Books.

## Why It Matters

The book is a counterweight to the modernist manifesto Tschichold wrote in his twenties, and a reminder that a designer's best work may come from revising his own certainties. Its influence runs directly into [The Elements of Typographic Style](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/the-elements-of-typographic-style).
