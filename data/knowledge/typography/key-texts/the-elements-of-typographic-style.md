# The Elements of Typographic Style

> *Typography exists to honor content.*

---

## The Book

Robert Bringhurst's *The Elements of Typographic Style* is the most widely admired modern guide to typography. First published in 1992 and revised several times through version 4.0 in 2012, it combines practical rules with a deep sense of history and an unusually beautiful prose style. [Hermann Zapf](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/hermann-zapf)[^sn-hermann-zapf] said he wished to see it become the typographer's Bible, and in many studios it is.

## What It Covers

The book moves from first principles to fine detail: rhythm and proportion (the [Measure](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/measure), [Leading](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/leading), word spacing, [Justification and Hyphenation](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/justification-and-hyphenation)), harmony and counterpoint ([Typographic Hierarchy](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/typographic-hierarchy),[^sn-typographic-hierarchy] [Small Caps](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/small-caps), [Figures](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/figures), [Pairing Typefaces](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/pairing-typefaces)), structural forms such as headings, notes, and tables, the "analphabetic" symbols like dashes and quotation marks (see [Quotes and Dashes](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/quotes-and-dashes)), and the shape of the page, where Bringhurst relates page proportions to musical intervals and the golden section (see [Page Canons](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/page-canons)). A long survey of typefaces and a historical synopsis tie it to the tradition traced in [Classification](/knowledge/typography/classification).

## Key Ideas

Bringhurst's central claim is that typography serves the text: "typography exists to honor content." Rules exist to be understood and then, occasionally, broken with intent. His recommendation of a 45 to 75 character line, with 66 as ideal, is one of the most quoted numbers in the field.
