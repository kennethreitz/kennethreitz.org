# The Crystal Goblet

> *Printing should be invisible.*

---

## The Essay

*The Crystal Goblet, or Printing Should Be Invisible* began as a lecture [Beatrice Warde](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/beatrice-warde) gave to the British Typographers' Guild in London in 1930. It was published soon afterward and later gave its name to a collection of her essays, *The Crystal Goblet: Sixteen Essays on Typography* (1955).

## The Argument

Warde imagines a choice between two wine glasses: one of solid gold, exquisitely worked, and one of clear crystal, thin as a bubble. The connoisseur of wine chooses the crystal, because everything about it is meant to reveal the wine rather than hide it. Typography, she argues, should be like that glass: its job is to convey the author's meaning, and it succeeds when the reader forgets it is there.

## Influence

The essay is the classic statement of typography as service, shared by [Stanley Morison](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/stanley-morison)'s *First Principles of Typography* and echoed by designers from [Adrian Frutiger](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/adrian-frutiger) to [Matthew Butterick](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/matthew-butterick).[^sn-matthew-butterick] It has also been argued with: expressive and postmodern designers point out that no typography is truly invisible, and that form always shapes meaning. Kenneth's own essay on the Reitz LaTeX theme takes the opposite side deliberately, arguing with McLuhan that how a thing looks changes what it is (see [Draft to Document Why I Made My Notes Look Like LaTeX Papers](/essays/2026-11-04-draft_to_document)). Both views live comfortably in this vault.
