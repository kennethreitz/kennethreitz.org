# Practical Typography

> *Typography is for everyone who writes.*

---

## The Book

*Butterick's Practical Typography* is a free web book by [Matthew Butterick](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/matthew-butterick), grown out of his earlier *Typography for Lawyers* (2010). It is aimed not at designers but at anyone who writes documents: lawyers, students, programmers, writers. Butterick asks readers who find it useful to pay for it voluntarily or buy his fonts.

## The Approach

The book begins with a short list of rules that fix most bad documents: choose the point size, the [Measure](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/measure),[^sn-measure] the line spacing (see [Leading](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/leading), about 120 to 145 percent of the point size), and better fonts than system defaults like [Times New Roman](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/times-new-roman); use curly quotes and proper dashes (see [Quotes and Dashes](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/quotes-and-dashes)); use one space between sentences; avoid underlining and all caps in body text. Later chapters cover [Typographic Hierarchy](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/typographic-hierarchy), tables, [Kerning](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/kerning), [Small Caps](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/small-caps), and the reasoning behind each recommendation.

## Why It Matters

Practical Typography brought the standards of [The Elements of Typographic Style](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/the-elements-of-typographic-style) to the web and to everyday writing, in a voice that is brisk, opinionated, and often funny. Its premise, that good typography is an act of respect for the reader, is a direct expression of [For Humans](/knowledge/philosophy/for-humans).
