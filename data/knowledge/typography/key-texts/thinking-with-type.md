# Thinking with Type

> *Typography is what language looks like.*

---

## The Book

*Thinking with Type*, by the designer, writer, and curator Ellen Lupton, is the most widely used introductory textbook of typography in design schools. First published in 2004 and revised in later editions, it is organized in three parts: Letter, Text, and Grid.

## What It Covers

The *Letter* section covers anatomy and [Classification](/knowledge/typography/classification), with a historical survey from the Renaissance to digital type. *Text* covers spacing, alignment, [Typographic Hierarchy](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/typographic-hierarchy),[^sn-typographic-hierarchy] and the history of the text block. *Grid* covers columns, modules, and the layout tradition of [Grid Systems](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/grid-systems) from [Müller-Brockmann](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/grid-systems-in-graphic-design) to screens.

## Approach

Where [The Elements of Typographic Style](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/the-elements-of-typographic-style) is a classical treatise, Thinking with Type is visual and contemporary, mixing modernist and experimental work with clear explanations of the basics. It treats typography as a way of thinking as well as a craft, and it gives as much space to the web and screens as to print.
