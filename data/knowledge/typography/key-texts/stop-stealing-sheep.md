# Stop Stealing Sheep

> *Type is a language everyone already reads and almost no one has been taught to speak.*

---

## The Book

*Stop Stealing Sheep & Find Out How Type Works*, by [Erik Spiekermann](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/erik-spiekermann) and E. M. Ginger, was written for the flood of new typesetters created by [Desktop Publishing](/knowledge/typography/history/desktop-publishing): office workers, students, and designers who suddenly had fonts on their computers and no training in using them.

## The Title

The title comes from a remark usually credited to Frederic Goudy, that anyone who would letterspace lowercase would steal sheep (see [Tracking](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/tracking)).

## Approach

The book explains type through everyday analogies, comparing typefaces to clothing and to tones of voice, and walks through how type works in practice: choosing faces for a purpose, [Pairing Typefaces](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/pairing-typefaces), spacing, [Readability and Legibility](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/readability-and-legibility), and how type expresses identity. It is short, visual, and friendly, the opposite of a treatise.

## Legacy

It became one of the standard first books on typography and remains in print in updated editions, alongside [Thinking with Type](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/thinking-with-type) and [Practical Typography](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/practical-typography).[^sn-practical-typography]
