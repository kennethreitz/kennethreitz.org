# Die neue Typographie

> *A manifesto for a typography that belonged to its own time.*

---

## The Book

*Die neue Typographie* (The New Typography), published in Berlin in 1928, is [Jan Tschichold](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/jan-tschichold)'s handbook of modernist typography and the definitive statement of the movement described in [Bauhaus and the New Typography](/knowledge/typography/history/bauhaus-and-the-new-typography). An English translation by Ruari McLean appeared in 1995.

## The Argument

Tschichold argued that traditional, centered book typography was a relic, and that modern life demanded a new approach grounded in function:

| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Asymmetry | Layouts built on dynamic, off-center balance rather than a central axis |
| Sans serif type | The simplest letters, especially [Grotesque](/knowledge/typography/classification/grotesque) and [Geometric Sans](/knowledge/typography/classification/geometric-sans) faces, as the type of the age |
| Hierarchy | Clear [Typographic Hierarchy](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/typographic-hierarchy) through size, weight, and position, not ornament |
| Standardization | DIN paper sizes and standard formats for letterheads, envelopes, and forms |
| Photography | Images made by machine, in place of drawn illustration |

## Afterward

The book became a foundation of modern graphic design and of the [Swiss International Style](/knowledge/typography/history/swiss-international-style). Tschichold himself soon came to regard its absolutism as dangerously close to the authoritarian politics he had fled, and returned to classical typography, writing the essays later gathered in [The Form of the Book](/knowledge/typography/key-texts/the-form-of-the-book). Reading the two together is an education in how a designer can grow by disagreeing with himself.
