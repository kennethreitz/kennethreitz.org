# Grid Systems in Graphic Design

> *The grid system is an aid, not a guarantee.*

---

## The Book

*Grid Systems in Graphic Design* (1981), by [Josef Müller-Brockmann](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/josef-muller-brockmann), is the classic manual of the modular grid, published in parallel German and English. It distills decades of [Swiss International Style](/knowledge/typography/history/swiss-international-style) practice into a step-by-step method.

## The Method

The book shows how to build a grid from the type itself: choose the type size and [Leading](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/leading),[^sn-leading] derive the column widths and the depth of fields from them, and divide the page into modules separated by consistent intervals. It then works through grids of increasing complexity (two, three, four columns; eight, twenty, and more fields) and shows how text, photographs, and illustrations occupy them in books, catalogs, posters, and exhibition design (see [Grid Systems](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/grid-systems) and [Vertical Rhythm](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/vertical-rhythm)).

## Philosophy

Müller-Brockmann presents the grid as an expression of a professional ethic: objective, rational, and accountable, ordering information for the reader's benefit. The book's opening insists that the grid is an aid to the designer, not a guarantee of good work.

## Legacy

It remains in print and in constant use, and the column systems of web design frameworks and modern design systems are its descendants.
