# The Visual Display of Quantitative Information

> *Graphical excellence is that which gives to the viewer the greatest number of ideas in the shortest time with the least ink in the smallest space.*

---

## The Book

[Edward Tufte](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/edward-tufte)'s first book on information design, self-published in 1983 through his own Graphics Press so that he could control its production, became one of the most influential design books of the century. It is about charts and graphs, but its standards of clarity, density, and integrity apply to any page.

## Key Ideas

| Idea | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Data-ink ratio | Maximize the share of ink that carries information |
| Chartjunk | Decoration that adds nothing and distracts |
| Lie factor | The distortion between the size of an effect and its graphic |
| Small multiples | A series of similar small graphics for comparison |
| Graphical integrity | Honest scales and context |

## Typography

The book, set in a [Bembo](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/bembo)-based face later released as [ET Book](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/et-book),[^sn-et-book] integrates text, images, and notes on the same page, often with notes in wide margins: the origin of the Tufte style of sidenotes. That style lives on in Tufte CSS and in this vault's own `sn-` and `mn-` sidenote conventions, and it shapes KJVstudy.org (see [Building for Contemplation When Spiritual Practice Becomes Code](/essays/2025-11-23-building_for_contemplation_when_spiritual_practice_becomes_code)).

## In This Vault

Tufte's insistence that design serve understanding is close to the core of [Design](/knowledge/technology/design) and [For Humans](/knowledge/philosophy/for-humans), and his principles are the visual counterpart of [Typographic Hierarchy](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/typographic-hierarchy).
