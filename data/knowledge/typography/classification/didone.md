# Didone

> *Pure contrast: black like velvet, thin like a thread of light.*

---

## The Moderns

The Didones, also called *Moderns*, are named for the [Didot](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/firmin-didot) family of Paris and [Giambattista Bodoni](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/giambattista-bodoni)[^sn-giambattista-bodoni] of Parma, whose types around the turn of the nineteenth century carried the [Transitional](/knowledge/typography/classification/transitional) trend to its logical end. Justus Erich Walbaum in Germany cut the third great face of the style.

## Recognizing Them

Extreme [Stroke Contrast](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/stroke-contrast) with hairline thins, a perfectly vertical axis, flat unbracketed hairline [serifs](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/serif), ball [terminals](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/terminal), and a strong vertical rhythm. They are typefaces of display and spectacle, made possible by very smooth wove paper and precise presses.

## After the Moderns

Didones dominated European printing in the early nineteenth century and then provoked a reaction: the [Slab Serif](/knowledge/typography/classification/slab-serif) and the [Grotesque](/knowledge/typography/classification/grotesque) were in part answers to their fragility in commercial work. The "Modern" faces of the later Victorian period, including the Monotype Modern on which [Donald Knuth](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/donald-knuth) based [Computer Modern](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/computer-modern), soften the style for text. Today Didones are the face of fashion: [Didot](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/didot) and its relatives set the mastheads of fashion magazines.
