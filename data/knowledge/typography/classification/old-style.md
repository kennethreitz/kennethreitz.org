# Old-Style

> *Calm, warm, and patient, the default voice of the printed book for three centuries.*

---

## The Garaldes

Old-style faces, called *Garaldes* in the Vox-ATypI system (a blend of Garamond and Aldus), descend from the roman cut by [Francesco Griffo](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/francesco-griffo)[^sn-francesco-griffo] for [Aldus Manutius](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/aldus-manutius) in 1495, the type known today through its revival as [Bembo](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/bembo). French punchcutters of the sixteenth century, above all [Claude Garamond](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/claude-garamond), refined it into the dominant book type of Europe. Dutch and English founders carried it through the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, ending with [William Caslon](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/william-caslon).

## Recognizing Them

Compared with the [Humanist Serif](/knowledge/typography/classification/humanist-serif) faces, old-styles are lighter and more refined. The axis is still diagonal, [Stroke Contrast](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/stroke-contrast) is moderate, the [serifs](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/serif) are bracketed and the head serifs angled, the **e** has a level crossbar, and the [ascenders](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/ascender) are tall. Many old-style families pair naturally with old-style [Figures](/knowledge/typography/practice-and-principles/figures), numerals that rise and fall like lowercase letters.

## Today

Old-style faces remain the classic choice for long reading: [Garamond](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/garamond), [Bembo](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/bembo), [Caslon](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/caslon), [Palatino](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/palatino) (a twentieth-century face in the tradition), and [ET Book](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/et-book). Their relatively small [X-Height](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/x-height) makes them elegant in print but sometimes small on screen.
