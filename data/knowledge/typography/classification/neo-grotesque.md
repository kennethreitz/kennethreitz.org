# Neo-Grotesque

> *Neutrality is a design decision too.*

---

## The Refinement

In 1957 two Swiss typefaces appeared that redefined the sans serif: [Helvetica](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/helvetica)[^sn-helvetica] (first released as Neue Haas Grotesk) and [Univers](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/univers). Both took the [Grotesque](/knowledge/typography/classification/grotesque) tradition, especially [Akzidenz-Grotesk](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/akzidenz-grotesk), and smoothed it into something more uniform, rational, and systematic, perfectly suited to the grid-based design of the [Swiss International Style](/knowledge/typography/history/swiss-international-style).

## Recognizing Them

Low [Stroke Contrast](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/stroke-contrast), horizontal [terminals](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/terminal), relatively closed [apertures](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/aperture), a large [X-Height](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/x-height), cap height and ascenders nearly level, and tight, even spacing. The result is a calm, almost anonymous texture, which made these faces the voice of corporate identity, public signage, and modernist graphic design.

## Descendants

Arial (1982) was drawn to share Helvetica's widths so it could replace it without reflowing documents. On screens, the neo-grotesque became the default interface voice: Apple's [SF Pro](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/sf-pro), Rasmus Andersson's [Inter](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/inter), and many system fonts belong here, though most open their apertures slightly toward the [Humanist Sans](/knowledge/typography/classification/humanist-sans) for legibility at small sizes.
