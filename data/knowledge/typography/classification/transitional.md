# Transitional

> *The Enlightenment, cast in metal: the pen gives way to reason.*

---

## Between Two Worlds

Transitional faces sit between the [Old-Style](/knowledge/typography/classification/old-style) and the [Didone](/knowledge/typography/classification/didone). The movement began in France with the *Romain du Roi*, a type commissioned for the royal printing office in the 1690s and designed by committee on a fine grid, an early attempt to define letters rationally rather than by hand. Pierre-Simon Fournier's types followed, and in England [John Baskerville](/knowledge/typography/type-designers/john-baskerville) produced the most celebrated transitional face in the 1750s.

## Recognizing Them

The axis turns nearly vertical, [Stroke Contrast](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/stroke-contrast) increases, [serifs](/knowledge/typography/anatomy/serif) become flatter and more sharply bracketed, and the letters grow wider and more regular. The texture is brighter and crisper than old-style, made possible by smoother paper and blacker ink, both areas where Baskerville[^sn-baskerville] experimented.

## Descendants

Many of the twentieth century's workhorse serifs are transitional at heart. [Times New Roman](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/times-new-roman) mixes old-style roots with transitional sharpness. Matthew Carter's [Georgia](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/georgia) is usually grouped here too, and the Scotch Romans of the nineteenth century, a direct ancestor of [Computer Modern](/knowledge/typography/typefaces/computer-modern), bridge transitional and [Didone](/knowledge/typography/classification/didone).
