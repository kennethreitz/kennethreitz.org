# Fire

> *The first light was a decision. Everything since has been its consequence.*

---

## The Element

Fire is the element of will — the outward thrust of a being that has decided to exist. Where the other elements describe how the world holds together, Fire describes why anything moves at all. It is hot and dry: hot because it radiates, spends itself outward, gives without hoarding; dry because it does not yield or absorb, it consumes and transforms. To touch Fire is to be changed. Nothing that passes through it returns unaltered.

As a mode of being, Fire is passion, energy, and spirit — the spark that leaps ahead of thought. It is intuition rather than deliberation, the flash that knows before it can explain. This is the element of the initiate, the one who acts, the courage that precedes certainty. Its shadow is the same as its gift: unattended, it burns what it was meant to warm. Fire asks a single question of anyone who carries it — what are you willing to spend yourself on?

## Across the Traditions

In tarot, Fire rules the suit of Wands — the batons of creativity, enterprise, and ambition, cards that crackle with beginnings. In [Astrology](/knowledge/astrology) it forms the fiery triplicity of [Aries](/knowledge/astrology/signs/aries), [Leo](/knowledge/astrology/signs/leo), and [Sagittarius](/knowledge/astrology/signs/sagittarius): the cardinal spark that starts, the fixed flame that sustains, the mutable blaze that seeks the horizon. Plato gave Fire the [Tetrahedron](/knowledge/sacred-geometry/tetrahedron), the sharpest and simplest of the [Platonic Solids](/knowledge/sacred-geometry/platonic-solids), its keen points the very reason flame feels the way it does against the skin. In [Alchemy](/knowledge/alchemy) Fire is the agent of every transformation, the calcinating heat that reduces matter to its essence, drawn as the upward triangle — aspiration made geometry. Among the [Chakras](/knowledge/chakras) its heat gathers at [Manipura](/knowledge/chakras/manipura), the solar plexus, seat of power, drive, and the sovereign sense of self. Everywhere it appears, Fire is the same: the principle that refuses to remain merely potential.
