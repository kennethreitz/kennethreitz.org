# Balam

> *Three heads upon a bear, riding fierce, granting the gift of the unseen.*

---

## The Spirit

Balam — his name echoing the biblical seer Balaam — is the fifty-first spirit of the Ars Goetia, a terrible and powerful King. He is described with three heads: the first that of a bull, the second of a man, and the third of a ram. He has the tail of a serpent and eyes that flame with fire, and he rides upon a fierce bear while carrying a hawk upon his fist. His many-headed monstrosity places him among the most vividly composite figures of the grimoire, a spirit whose form gathers several natures into one.

The Goetia notes that he speaks with a hoarse voice, and that beneath his fearsome exterior he is a giver of true answers, prized for candour once he has been properly commanded.

## Office

According to the Goetia, Balam gives true answers concerning things past, present, and to come. His signature gift is invisibility: at the operator’s command he can make a person go unseen. He also grants men wit and cunning, sharpening the intellect of those who summon him. His office thus joins prophecy, concealment, and cleverness — knowledge of time, the power to vanish, and the quickness of mind to use both.
