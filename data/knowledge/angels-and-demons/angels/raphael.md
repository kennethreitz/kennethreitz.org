# Raphael

> *“God heals” — and the road home is itself the medicine.*

---

## The Archangel

Raphael is the physician of heaven, the angel whose very name — *Rapha-El*, “God heals” — is a prescription. In the Book of Tobit he walks the dusty roads disguised as a travelling companion named Azarias, guiding young Tobias to a cure, binding a demon, restoring an old man’s sight, and revealing himself only at the journey’s end as “one of the seven who stand before the glory of the Lord.” From this tale he takes his double office: he is at once the healer who mends the body and the guide who accompanies the pilgrim, and in Raphael these are not two roles but one, for the deepest healing is a journey and every true journey heals. He carries the physician’s staff and the pilgrim’s gourd; he is the patron of travellers, of seekers, of all who are on the way from sickness toward wholeness.

His planet is Mercury and his element air — the quicksilver intelligence that moves between worlds, the breath that carries both speech and life. Air is the medium of exchange, of commerce between distant things, and Raphael governs all such crossings: the movement of the healer’s hand, the passage of the traveller, the bridge of communication between the sick and the whole. Enthroned at the heart of the great scheme, at the very centre of harmony and beauty, he mediates between the higher and the lower, drawing down the solar grace and distributing it as remedy. He is robed in yellow and green, the colours of dawn air and living leaf, and where he passes the wounded are made new and the lost are set again upon their road.

## Scripture and Tradition

Raphael’s great scripture is the Book of Tobit, one of the tenderest narratives in the whole angelic literature. There he is sent by God in answer to two prayers uttered on the same day and in the same hour — the blind Tobit’s and the afflicted Sarah’s — and he weaves their two sorrows into a single cure. He teaches Tobias to catch a great fish and to keep its heart, liver, and gall; with the burning of the heart and liver he drives off the demon Asmodeus, and with the gall he anoints Tobit’s eyes and restores his sight. Only then does he disclose himself: “I am Raphael, one of the seven holy angels, which present the prayers of the saints, and which go in and out before the glory of the Holy One.” The name of that fish-gall remedy passed into folk medicine, and Raphael became the patron of apothecaries, of the blind, of matchmakers, and of every traveller on a dangerous road. Later tradition also names him the angel who healed Abraham after his circumcision and who instructs Noah in the medicinal use of herbs.

In the Kabbalistic scheme Raphael is enthroned at [Tiphareth](/knowledge/tree-of-life/tiphareth), the sixth sphere at the very heart of the Tree of Life, the seat of harmony, beauty, and the redeeming sun. From this central station he mediates between the heights and the depths, which is fitting for the great reconciler and healer. His day is Wednesday, Mercury’s day, and his direction is the East, the place of sunrise and of the fresh morning air that is his element. His colours are yellow and green, and his emblems the staff, the fish, the pilgrim’s gourd, and the vial of medicine. To invoke Raphael is to call upon the faculty that makes whole — that bridges what is divided, that accompanies the sufferer on the road, and that knows the journey and the cure to be one and the same.
