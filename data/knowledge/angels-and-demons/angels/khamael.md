# Khamael

> *“He who sees God” — and, having seen, draws the sword in defence of what is holy.*

---

## The Archangel

Khamael — spelled also Camael or Chamuel — is the archangel of severity, the celestial warrior who stands guard at the gate of divine justice. The name is usually read “He who sees God,” though the harsher tradition renders it closer to “burner of God” or “the one who beholds God face to face,” and both meanings are true to his character, for to see the holy clearly is also to burn away everything unworthy of it. Where [Michael](/knowledge/angels-and-demons/angels/michael) wields solar fire in the service of the cosmic order, Khamael wields the martial fire of judgement — hotter, narrower, addressed not to the whole heaven but to the single act that must be punished, defended, or refused. He is the strength that says *no* and means it, the discipline that carves the true from the false with the edge of a blade. In iconography he is a warrior in armour of burnished red, a sword or spear in hand, sometimes crowned and sometimes surrounded by fire, the very figure of righteous severity.

His planet is Mars and his office is war — not the aimless violence of the battlefield but the sacred combat of the soul that must defend its boundaries and destroy what would corrupt it. He presides over Tuesday, the day of Mars, over iron, the metal of the blade and the plough, and over the whole province of strength turned to holy purpose: courage, fortitude, the will made adamantine. To draw near to Khamael is to be tested, for his grace is the grace of the crucible; he does not soothe, he tempers. Yet the severity is not cruelty. It is the necessary counter-weight to mercy, the pruning that lets the tree bear fruit, the fire that leaves gold and consumes the dross.

## Scripture and Tradition

Like [Haniel](/knowledge/angels-and-demons/angels/haniel), Khamael belongs chiefly to the mystical and Kabbalistic streams rather than to the canonical scriptures, where he is not named. The apocryphal and rabbinic literatures give him his offices: some traditions count him among the seven archangels who stand before the throne, and others make him the angel who strengthened Christ in the garden of Gethsemane, or the leader of the powers who wage war against the rebellious spirits. The medieval books of correspondence firmly established him as the archangel of Mars and its Tuesday, the martial complement to the Venusian Haniel and the solar Michael. In the tellings that soften him to “Chamuel,” he becomes an angel of consolation and just relationships, the one who seeks out and reconciles — for the sword that divides can also cut the knots that bind, and severity in the service of love is still severity.

In the Kabbalistic scheme Khamael presides over [Geburah](/knowledge/tree-of-life/geburah), the fifth sphere, the seat of severity, strength, and divine judgement, ruled by [Mars](/knowledge/astrology/planets/mars-astrology) and radiant with the disciplined fire that upholds the order of the Tree. Geburah is the arm of God that punishes and defends, the necessary rigour that keeps the boundless mercy of [Chesed](/knowledge/tree-of-life/chesed) from dissolving into formlessness, and Khamael is its captain. His planetary trump on the Tree is [The Tower](/knowledge/tarot/major-arcana/the-tower), the sixteenth key, the lightning-struck edifice whose sudden ruin is Mars breaking down what pride has built too high — the same severity, cast as catastrophe and liberation. His day is Tuesday, his metal iron, his colour the deep red of Mars, and his emblems the sword, the spear, and the flame. To invoke Khamael is to call upon the faculty that judges and defends — the strength that sets a boundary, the courage that fights the necessary battle, and the clear severe love that refuses to spare what must be burned away.
