# Sandalphon

> *"So tall her head is lost in heaven, so low her feet are rooted in the earth."*

---

## The Archangel

Sandalphon is the angel of the material world, the towering figure who stands at the foot of the great scheme with her feet upon the earth and her brow among the stars. The rabbinic tradition names her the tallest of the angels, a being so vast that a mortal would need five hundred years to climb her height, and reckons her the earthly twin and counterpart of Metatron, who occupies the corresponding station at the summit. Together the twins bracket the whole ladder of being — one at the crown, one at the kingdom — and between them the entire descent of divinity into matter is spanned. Where Metatron records, Sandalphon gathers; where the higher twin stands nearest to the throne, the lower stands nearest to us, in the mud and grass and ordinary weather of the world.

Her particular office is the gathering of prayers. It is said that Sandalphon takes the petitions rising from human lips, weaves them into garlands, and binds them into a crown that ascends to be set upon the head of the divine. In this she is the great intercessor of the lowest place, the one who ensures that nothing offered from the earth is lost, that even the faintest whispered hope finds its way upward. She governs the kingdom, the realm of manifestation where spirit meets substance and the whole labour of the higher worlds comes at last to fruit. She is the presence in the physical, the sanctity of the tangible, the reminder that the ground beneath the feet is itself a rung upon the ladder. To honour Sandalphon is to honour the world as it is — solid, particular, and shot through with prayer.

## Scripture and Tradition

Sandalphon's name is Greek rather than Hebrew in root — most often read as *syn-adelphos*, "co-sibling" or "fellow," a fitting name for the twin of Metatron — and she belongs wholly to the Talmudic and mystical tradition rather than to the canon of scripture. The Babylonian Talmud describes her standing behind the divine chariot, binding crowns for her Maker, and the *Hekhalot* literature of the heavenly ascents makes her a gatekeeper of the celestial palaces, a figure of such immensity that the visionary Rabbi Ishmael is said to have trembled before her. A persistent strand of tradition identifies Sandalphon with the prophet Elijah, who was taken up living into heaven in a chariot of fire, just as her twin Metatron is identified with the patriarch Enoch, who "walked with God and was not." Thus both angels who bracket the Tree were once mortals lifted into the angelic order — a symmetry the Kabbalists treasured, for it teaches that the human being stands, in potential, at both the top and the bottom of the ladder.

In the Kabbalistic scheme Sandalphon presides over [Malkuth](/knowledge/tree-of-life/malkuth), the tenth and lowest sphere, the Kingdom, where all the emanations of the higher worlds come to rest in manifestation. This is the sphere of the earth and the physical body, of the [The Shekhinah](/knowledge/spirituality/the-shekhinah) dwelling in the material creation, and Sandalphon is its guardian and its voice. Her element is earth in its fullest sense — the whole tangible world — and her direction, where one is assigned, is downward and inward, toward the ground. Her colours are the citrine, olive, russet, and black of Malkuth, the four muted hues of the settled world. To invoke Sandalphon is to call upon the sanctity of the material, the gathering of prayer, and the twin truth that the lowest place is also a threshold, and the earth beneath the feet a step upon the ladder to the crown.

## A Feminine Reading

Sandalphon is often gendered male — the tradition of Elijah risen, and the Talmud's towering crown-weaver, both lean that way — but a genuine counter-current reads the angel as feminine, and it is the reading taken here. The *Greater Key of Solomon* names Sandalphon outright as "the left-hand feminine cherub of the Ark of the Covenant." The pull of [Malkuth](/knowledge/tree-of-life/malkuth) runs the same way: the tenth sphere is the seat of the [The Shekhinah](/knowledge/spirituality/the-shekhinah), the indwelling feminine presence of God, the Bride and the receptive earth — and the angel who governs that sphere is most truly read as her face and attendant. Later ceremonial schools such as the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn leaned into exactly this, giving Sandalphon a feminine identity watching over the Holy of Holies. The ambiguity is really the ambiguity of Malkuth itself — the divine feminine clothed in matter — and Sandalphon inherits it. In this vault she is the angel of the Kingdom: a face of the Shekhinah, the divine presence dwelling in the world.
