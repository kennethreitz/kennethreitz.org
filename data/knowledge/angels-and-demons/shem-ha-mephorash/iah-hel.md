# Iah-Hel

> *“In the quiet room, the whole world becomes legible.”*

---

## The Angel

Iah-Hel doubles the divine name upon itself, joining *Yah* — the abbreviated Tetragrammaton — to *-el*, so that the whole name is little more than a repetition of God's presence. It is customarily translated *the Supreme Being*, and among the seventy-two it is the angel most concerned with the contemplative life. Its domain is wisdom sought in withdrawal: the philosopher's solitude, the desire to know for its own sake, the illumination that comes to those who quiet the noise of the world in order to hear the finer signal beneath it.

The virtue Iah-Hel bestows is the discipline of retreat. It favours those who seek tranquillity and enlightenment, who value the company of the wise over the crowd, and who understand that certain truths disclose themselves only to patience. Under its governance one learns to distinguish genuine solitude from mere isolation — the fertile silence from the barren one. It is invoked against the restlessness that scatters the mind, and for the steadiness that lets a single question be held long enough to open. Following Umabel's warmth of friendship, Iah-Hel turns inward, teaching that the soul must sometimes close the door in order to meet what stands, supreme and unhurried, on the other side.
