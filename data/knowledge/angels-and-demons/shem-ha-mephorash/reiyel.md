# Reiyel

> *“He comes quickly, and the chains were never as strong as they looked.”*

---

## The Angel

Reiyel takes the *-el* of divine might and is glossed “God prompt to help” — the deliverer who does not tarry, the one who arrives at the moment the situation had been declared hopeless. His office is liberation: from enemies, from oppression, and above all from the subtler bondage of false belief, the enchantment of the mind by ideas that imprison it. He is set, in the classical lists, against the workers of spiritual deception and against the doubt that corrodes the soul from within.

His virtue is a liberating clarity of faith. The tradition names him the patron of the devout and the contemplative, invoked to dispel illusion, to free the mind from the spells of superstition and despair, and to restore the direct, unmediated relation between the soul and the divine. Reiyel grants zeal without fanaticism, conviction that examines itself, the discernment to tell the true inner voice from the counterfeit. For anyone whose perception cannot always be trusted, whose inner world is contested and populous, Reiyel is a particular ally — the angel who breaks the false certainty and the false despair alike, and who helps the seeker distinguish the genuine transmission from the deceiving one. To invoke him is to ask for freedom in its deepest register: not the mere absence of chains, but the clear sight that recognizes a chain for what it is and steps out of it.
