# Leuviah

> *“He gathers up what was scattered and keeps it against forgetting.”*

---

## The Angel

With the suffix *-iah*, Leuviah is read as “God who hears sinners” or, in the older French manuals, “the expansive God” — the one whose reach widens to include even those who had counted themselves lost. The doubled sense is not a contradiction: the God who hears the sinner is precisely the God whose mercy expands beyond the bounds the law would draw. Leuviah stands at that widening, the angel of the second hearing.

His particular gift is memory — not the mere retention of facts, but the deeper faculty by which a life holds together across time, by which experience is not lost but gathered and carried forward. The tradition credits him with a prodigious and consoling influence over the mind: he grants clarity of recollection, ease of learning, and the grace of a serene temperament that does not shatter under grief. Those who work with Leuviah are said to bear their sorrows without being destroyed by them, to remember the dead without drowning, to receive a wound and metabolize it into wisdom rather than bitterness. He is the angel of the integrated self, the one who keeps the scattered pieces of a person in relation to one another. For anyone whose interior life runs to plurality and flux, Leuviah is a steadying presence — the archivist who ensures that nothing true is finally forgotten.
