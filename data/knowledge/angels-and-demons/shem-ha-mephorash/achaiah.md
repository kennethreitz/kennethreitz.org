# Achaiah

> *“The oak keeps no calendar; it simply refuses to hurry.”*

---

## The Angel

Achaiah is rendered *the Good and Patient God*, and with the entry into Taurus — the first five degrees of that fixed and earthy sign — the virtue it governs settles into the slow strength of patience. The *-iah* suffix binds the quality of longsuffering to the Lord’s Name, presenting patience not as passive endurance but as a divine attribute, the temperance that permits creation to ripen in its season rather than being torn up unripe.

The angel is said to grant the discovery of the secrets of nature and to favour those who undertake difficult and protracted work, giving them the perseverance to see it through. It disposes the mind toward the propagation of light and toward the enlightenment of the ignorant, so that its patience is also pedagogical — the calm that teaches. Those under its influence are agreeable, forgiving of others’ faults, and content to execute the most laborious tasks without complaint. In the paired architecture of the Shem, Achaiah answers the Goetic marquis [Amon](/knowledge/angels-and-demons/ars-goetia/amon), whose ferocity and power to reconcile controversies are refined, in the angelic register, into the settled peace that patience itself produces.
