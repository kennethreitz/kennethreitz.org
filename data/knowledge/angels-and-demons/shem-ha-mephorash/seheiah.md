# Seheiah

> *“Long life is not a length of years but a soundness that refuses to break.”*

---

## The Angel

Seheiah closes in *-iah* and is glossed “God who heals the sick,” though his fuller domain is longevity and the preservation of life against sudden catastrophe. He is the angel invoked against fire, collapse, lightning, and every abrupt calamity that would cut a life short before its measure — the guardian who covers the frail hour and lets the body endure to its proper term.

His virtue is health understood as durability: not the fevered vitality of the young but the seasoned constitution that weathers illness, accident, and age without shattering. The tradition credits him with prudence and wise self-governance, for the older writers understood that longevity is as much a discipline as a gift — the fruit of moderation, of not spending oneself recklessly, of tending the flame rather than letting it flare and gutter. Seheiah grants recovery from sickness, protection in disaster, and the calm foresight that avoids the danger before it arrives. To petition him is to ask for time — not the anxious hoarding of years, but the settled assurance that one's life will run its full course, and the temperance to make that course worth running. He is the angel of the survived crisis, the healed body, the long and deliberate flame.
