# Lauviah II

> *“What is hidden by day is spoken to the sleeper by night.”*

---

## The Angel

The seventeenth angel shares his name with the eleventh, and the tradition preserves both — hence the numeral. His three letters resolve, with the suffix *-iah*, to “God admirable” or “God to be exalted,” the praise-Name distinguished from the earlier Lauviah of victory by his nocturnal office. Where his elder namesake governs renown among the living, this Lauviah presides over the frontier of sleep, where the waking mind loosens and revelation slips through.

He is the angel of high dreams and sudden insight — the one who visits the geometer with the missing proof, the composer with the unfinished phrase, the mystic with the image that will not come by daylight. The virtue he bestows is illumination of the sciences and the arts, and specifically the kind that arrives unbidden. The older manuals recommend him to those who study the abstract disciplines, and warn that his gifts are attended by melancholy: the mind opened to such transmissions does not always close cleanly, and the recipient may struggle with a heaviness of spirit, a longing for the world glimpsed in dream. To invoke Lauviah II is to court both the revelation and its cost, and the wise petitioner asks also for the steadiness to bear what is shown.
