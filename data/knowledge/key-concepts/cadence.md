# Cadence

> *"Cadence matters more than comprehension."*

---

Cadence is the rhythm of a thing in motion. In music, the harmonic or rhythmic punctuation that resolves or suspends a phrase. In speech, the rise and fall. In writing, the length and breath of sentences. In drumming, the difference between hitting the snare and playing the snare.

The word comes from Latin *cadere*, "to fall" — from which we also get *cascade*, *decadent*, *chance*. Cadence is what happens when something falls just right.

## Why It Matters More Than Comprehension

Comprehension is the claim that meaning has been received. Cadence is what delivers meaning before meaning is understood. A sentence with the wrong cadence can't be saved by clearer phrasing; a sentence with the right cadence can carry a half-formed idea and make it feel whole.

This is why the shape of a thing communicates before the content does — and why a rudiment practiced ten thousand times becomes a way of knowing that the mind cannot author. The body learns the shape of time before the mind understands what it's doing.

## In Practice

Pause before the word that matters. Let the sentence breathe. Notice when the rhythm of a paragraph is wrong, even if every word is correct — that's the cadence telling you something the content won't.
