# Aquamarine

> *The sea, decided to hold still for a while.*

---

## The Stone

Aquamarine is beryl in its ocean mood — the same mineral family as emerald, but where emerald hoards its green, aquamarine runs clear and cool, from the palest glacier-blue to the deep blue-green of open water. The name is Latin, *aqua marina*, seawater, and the Romans meant it literally: this was the sailor's stone, carried on voyages as a charm against drowning and a token of Neptune's favor. Legend held that it was the treasure of mermaids, washed up from their caskets to the shore.

It is a hard, clean, trustworthy gem — often nearly flawless where its cousin emerald is famously included — and that clarity is the whole of its character. Nothing murky, nothing hidden. Water you can see to the bottom of.

## Properties

Aquamarine is the stone of calm courage and clear speech. Where the throat stones as a family govern expression, aquamarine's particular gift is saying the difficult thing without the storm — truth delivered at the temperature of cool water. It is turned to for soothing an overactive mind, releasing old anger held in the jaw and shoulders, and steadying fear of the unknown; the old sailors' charm generalizes to anyone setting out across water they cannot see the far side of. It is also considered a stone of letting go — of what the tide should be allowed to take.

## In Practice

Worn at the throat before hard conversations — the apology, the boundary, the ask — it is a companion for speaking clearly under pressure. Held during breath-focused [Meditation](/knowledge/spirituality/meditation), its watery calm pairs naturally with slow exhales. Some keep it near during travel, honoring the original use, and it is a traditional gift for 19th wedding anniversaries and March birthdays. Cleanse it in water; it is one of the few stones whose element and care instructions agree.
