# Rhodonite

> *The rose that grew through the wound and did not resent the thorn.*

---

## The Stone

Rhodonite is a warm rose-pink laced with veins and patches of black — manganese oxide threading through the pink like ink through blossom. The name comes from the Greek for rose, and the finest polished pieces look exactly like that: a soft dusty pink, sometimes deep and almost salmon, marbled with dark dendritic streaks that give each stone its own map. The contrast is the whole beauty of it, the tender pink and the hard black held together in one body.

That marriage of light and dark is fitting for a healer’s stone. Rhodonite does not pretend the black veins away; it carries them, and is more beautiful for it — a quality that gives the stone its gentle, forgiving character.

## Properties

Rhodonite is the stone of compassion and emotional healing — a balm for the wounded heart. It is turned to in the aftermath of grief, betrayal or old hurt, drawing the poison from the wound and encouraging forgiveness, both of others and of oneself. Sitting at the heart, it balances the emotions and steadies the reactive spikes of resentment and anger into something calmer and more merciful. Its earthy Venusian nature roots love in the practical and the enduring, making it a stone of reconciliation and of the patient work of repair.

## In Practice

Worn over the heart or held during emotional processing, it supports the slow labour of healing after loss or conflict — softening bitterness into acceptance. It is often kept close through the ending of a relationship or the mending of one, a quiet aid to forgiveness. In meditation it is placed at the chest to release stored grief and to open the heart again without leaving it defenceless.
