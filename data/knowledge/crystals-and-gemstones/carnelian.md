# Carnelian

> *The ember does not debate whether to burn.*

---

## The Stone

Carnelian is chalcedony flushed with iron into the warm colours of a low fire — orange, rust, and glowing red, sometimes clear and sometimes clouded, often shading from pale apricot to deep brick within a single stone. Held to the light it glows like stained glass with a candle behind it. The ancients loved it: Egyptians set it in jewellery and tomb amulets, Romans carved it into signet rings because hot wax would not stick to its surface, and it turns up in the sacred ornaments of many old cultures wherever vitality was the thing to be invoked.

There is heat in it, plainly. Carnelian looks like courage looks — bright, direct, unhesitating — and it feels warm to the intention even when cool to the touch.

## Properties

Carnelian fires the [sacral](/knowledge/chakras/svadhisthana) centre, the wellspring of creativity, passion, and vital force. It is the stone of courage and motivation — a restorer of drive to the depleted and a kindler of confidence in the timid. It stirs creative and sexual energy, banishes the apathy that keeps good work unstarted, and lends the boldness to act rather than merely intend. Where fear or self-doubt stalls momentum, Carnelian is reached for to get the blood and the will moving again.

## In Practice

Carnelian is carried or worn low on the body to feed vitality and creative drive, and placed on the sacral centre in meditation to unstick a stalled project or a stalled mood. Artists keep it at the workbench; the timid keep it in a pocket before something daunting. It cleanses easily in running water or sunlight, whose warmth suits its fiery nature well.
