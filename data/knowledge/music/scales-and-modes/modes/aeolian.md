# Aeolian

> *The plain truth of sadness, told without ornament.*

---

## The Mode

Aeolian is the sixth mode of the major scale, starting on its sixth degree — and like Ionian among the bright modes, it is so familiar it has its own everyday name: the natural minor scale. Built on C it borrows from E♭ major, flattening the third, sixth, and seventh against the major scale to produce the archetypal minor sound.

If Ionian is the sound of contentment, Aeolian is the sound of longing and loss. It carries no bright altered tone to soften it the way Dorian’s natural sixth does; it is minor through and through, the reference point against which the other minor modes reveal their particular colours.

## Structure

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Interval formula | W H W W H W W |
| Notes (from C) | C D E♭ F G A♭ B♭ |
| Characteristic tone | None — this is the natural minor scale |
| Parent major scale | E♭ major |

## Character

Aeolian’s mood is unguarded melancholy. The flattened third darkens the tonic chord, and the lowered sixth and seventh remove any pull toward brightness, so the whole scale settles into a plaintive, introspective gravity. Without a leading tone it lacks the sharp cadential drive of the harmonic minor, which leaves it feeling more resigned than dramatic — sorrow at rest rather than sorrow in crisis. It is the default emotional shorthand for “sad” in Western music.

## In Practice

Aeolian is the backbone of minor-key pop, rock, and folk, and of a great deal of film and game music reaching for grief, tension, or resolve. Ballads, laments, and brooding anthems tend to live here. As the natural minor it is one of the two modes — with Ionian — that most listeners internalise without ever naming.
