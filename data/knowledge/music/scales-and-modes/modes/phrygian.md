# Phrygian

> *A door opening onto something older and darker than you expected.*

---

## The Mode

Phrygian is the third mode of the major scale, beginning on its third degree. Built on C it draws from A♭ major, producing a minor scale whose defining feature is a lowered second — a half step sitting right above the root. That interval, arriving so early and so close, sets the entire mood before the melody has gone anywhere.

It is the darkest of the commonly used minor modes, tense and exotic, with an undertow of menace or ancient ceremony. Where Dorian keeps a window open, Phrygian pulls the shutters closed. The flattened second gives it a distinctly Iberian, Middle-Eastern colour that no other diatonic mode possesses.

## Structure

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Interval formula | H W W W H W W |
| Notes (from C) | C D♭ E♭ F G A♭ B♭ |
| Characteristic tone | The ♭2nd — dark, Spanish |
| Parent major scale | A♭ major |

## Character

The lowered second is the whole story. That half step pressing against the tonic creates a gravitational pull and an unresolved tension, as if the second degree is forever trying to fall into the root. To Western ears trained on a natural second, it reads as foreign, brooding, and a little dangerous. Stack a major chord on that flattened second and you get the “Phrygian cadence” snap that so much flamenco and metal live for.

## In Practice

Phrygian is the home mode of flamenco and Spanish guitar, and by extension of a huge swath of heavy metal, where its dark tension and low-register menace are prized. It surfaces in film scores reaching for the ancient or the ominous, and in any passage that wants to sound antique, sacred, or threatening.
