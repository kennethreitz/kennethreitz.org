# Scales

> *Everything that spills out past the seven diatonic notes.*

---

## Overview

The scales that live outside the seven diatonic [Modes](/knowledge/music/scales-and-modes/modes) — eight notes covering the altered minors, the pentatonics, and the symmetrical scales, each documented with its structure, its character, and its use in practice.

The altered minors first: the [Harmonic Minor](/knowledge/music/scales-and-modes/scales/harmonic-minor), with a knife hidden in its raised seventh, and the [Melodic Minor](/knowledge/music/scales-and-modes/scales/melodic-minor), a major scale that never got over its lowered third. Then the pentatonics, where subtraction does the work — the [Major Pentatonic](/knowledge/music/scales-and-modes/scales/major-pentatonic), five notes that cannot make a wrong sound, and the [Minor Pentatonic](/knowledge/music/scales-and-modes/scales/minor-pentatonic), five notes carrying a whole century of feeling. Add one bruise of a blue note to the latter and you have the [Blues Scale](/knowledge/music/scales-and-modes/scales/blues-scale).

The symmetrical scales close the set: the [Chromatic Scale](/knowledge/music/scales-and-modes/scales/chromatic-scale), every door in the building at once; the [Whole Tone](/knowledge/music/scales-and-modes/scales/whole-tone) scale, a staircase where every step is the same height; and the [Diminished](/knowledge/music/scales-and-modes/scales/diminished) scale, alternating patience and menace.
