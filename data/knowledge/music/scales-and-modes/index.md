# Scales & Modes

> *Seven notes, reordered seven ways — and everything that spills out past them.*

---

A **scale** is an ordered set of pitches, a ladder of intervals that defines the tonal world a piece lives in. A **mode** is what happens when you take one scale — classically the major scale — and start it on a different degree, keeping the same notes but shifting the centre of gravity. Play only the white keys from C and you get the major scale; start that same set of white keys on D and you get Dorian, on E Phrygian, and so on. The result is seven distinct colours drawn from a single source. Past the diatonic modes lie the other scales — the minors, the pentatonics, the symmetrical scales — each with its own architecture and mood. The same structures are what pytheory models in code.

---

## The Diatonic Modes

The seven rotations of the major scale, brightest to darkest:

[Lydian](/knowledge/music/scales-and-modes/modes/lydian) · [Ionian](/knowledge/music/scales-and-modes/modes/ionian) · [Mixolydian](/knowledge/music/scales-and-modes/modes/mixolydian) · [Dorian](/knowledge/music/scales-and-modes/modes/dorian) · [Aeolian](/knowledge/music/scales-and-modes/modes/aeolian) · [Phrygian](/knowledge/music/scales-and-modes/modes/phrygian) · [Locrian](/knowledge/music/scales-and-modes/modes/locrian)

[Ionian](/knowledge/music/scales-and-modes/modes/ionian) is the major scale; [Aeolian](/knowledge/music/scales-and-modes/modes/aeolian) is the natural minor. The rest sit between the fully bright ([Lydian](/knowledge/music/scales-and-modes/modes/lydian), with its raised 4th) and the fully dark ([Locrian](/knowledge/music/scales-and-modes/modes/locrian), with its flattened 2nd and 5th).

The same seven notes, walked from brightest to darkest:

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    Ly["Lydian — brightest"]
    Io["Ionian — major"]
    Mi["Mixolydian"]
    Do["Dorian"]
    Ae["Aeolian — natural minor"]
    Ph["Phrygian"]
    Lo["Locrian — darkest"]
    Ly --> Io --> Mi --> Do --> Ae --> Ph --> Lo
```
