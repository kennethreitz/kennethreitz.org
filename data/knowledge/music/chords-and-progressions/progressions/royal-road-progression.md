# Royal Road Progression

> *The bittersweet highway that runs straight through Tokyo.*

---

## The Progression

The Royal Road progression — IV–V–iii–vi — is a signature of Japanese pop, so ubiquitous there that it earned the nickname ōdō, the "royal road." It opens on the subdominant, rises to the dominant, but then sidesteps the expected tonic and slips into the mediant minor before landing on the relative minor. That deceptive turn, refusing the resolution the V sets up, is the source of its distinctive ache — hopeful and sorrowful at once.

The magic is in the V–iii move. The dominant primes the ear for a return home, and instead the iii arrives, a chord that shares two notes with the tonic but keeps the phrase suspended in shadow. The vi then completes the gentle collapse into the minor. It is a progression engineered for emotional swell, which is why it lives under so many soaring choruses.

## Structure

| Character | Bittersweet, soaring, wistful |
| Function | Subdominant → Dominant → deceptive turn to relative minor |

## In Practice

It is the bittersweet staple of J-pop and anime, threaded through countless theme songs and city-pop tunes; Western ears know it from songs that swap between hope and heartbreak within a single bar. Its deceptive V–iii motion makes it a cousin to the [Cadences](/knowledge/music/chords-and-progressions/progressions/cadences) family of interrupted resolutions, and its four chords rearrange the same diatonic set as the [Fifties Progression](/knowledge/music/chords-and-progressions/progressions/fifties-progression).
