# Andalusian Cadence

> *A staircase descending into fire and dust.*

---

## The Progression

The Andalusian cadence is a descending four-chord figure — i–♭VII–♭VI–V — whose bass walks stepwise down from the minor tonic to the dominant. Despite the name it is not truly a cadence in the textbook sense but a repeating progression, one whose falling motion evokes the Phrygian flavor of flamenco and the sun-baked tragedy of Spanish music. The final V, a major chord borrowed for its leading-tone bite, snaps the loop back to the minor tonic with a hint of scorched drama.

The descent is everything. Each chord lowers the bass by a whole or half step, and the major V at the bottom — with its raised third against the minor mode above — produces the distinctive Phrygian-dominant color that reads instantly as Andalusian, Middle Eastern, or simply fateful. It sounds ancient and inevitable, a slow fall that keeps beginning again.

## Structure

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|

| Chords used | [Minor Triad](/knowledge/music/chords-and-progressions/chords/minor-triad) (i), [Major Triad](/knowledge/music/chords-and-progressions/chords/major-triad) (♭VII, ♭VI, V) |
| Character | Dark, Spanish, descending, tragic |
| Function | Stepwise minor descent to a Phrygian dominant |

## In Practice

It is the harmonic signature of flamenco and passes freely into rock, classical, and film scoring — "Hit the Road Jack," "Sultans of Swing," the flamenco tradition writ large, and countless tango and surf tunes descend this same staircase. Its brooding character connects it to the minor-leaning [vi-IV-I-V](/knowledge/music/chords-and-progressions/progressions/vi-iv-i-v), and its link to the [Scales & Modes](/knowledge/music/scales-and-modes) of the Phrygian family gives it that unmistakable color.
