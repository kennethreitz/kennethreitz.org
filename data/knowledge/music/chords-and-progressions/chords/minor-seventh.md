# Minor Seventh

> *Cool air moving through an open window.*

---

## The Chord

The minor seventh adds a minor seventh to a minor triad — a minor third, a major third, then a minor third stacked on top. On C the notes are C, E♭, G, B♭. Both the third and the seventh are flattened, which gives the chord a soft, rounded quality with none of the sharp pull you find in a dominant seventh. The intervals are gentle, the tensions mild, and the whole thing settles into a mellow, slightly melancholy warmth.

Unlike its diminished and half-diminished cousins, the minor seventh keeps its perfect fifth, so it stays grounded. It is stable enough to sit on and colourful enough to stay interesting — a chord you can live inside without needing it to go anywhere in particular.

## Character

The minor seventh is understated and cool. It carries the introspection of a minor triad but softens it with the added seventh, trading sadness for something more like poise or quiet contemplation. It sounds urban and nocturnal, equally suited to a smoky ballad and a laid-back groove. Where the minor triad can ache, the minor seventh mostly sighs.

## In Practice

The minor seventh is the workhorse of jazz harmony, the ii and vi chords that make the classic ii–V–I progression turn. It anchors soul, R&B, funk, and neo-soul, where its smooth voicing sits perfectly under a vocal. As the ii it sets up the dominant; as the vi it offers a gentle detour from the tonic. It is the [Minor Triad](/knowledge/music/chords-and-progressions/chords/minor-triad) extended, and the natural partner to the [Dominant Seventh](/knowledge/music/chords-and-progressions/chords/dominant-seventh) in a turnaround.
