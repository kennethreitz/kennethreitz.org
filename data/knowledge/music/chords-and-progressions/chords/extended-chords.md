# Extended Chords

> *The overtones, invited up from the basement to sit at the table.*

---

## The Chord

Extended chords keep the stacking going. If a seventh chord is four thirds tall, the extensions add more — a ninth, an eleventh, a thirteenth — reaching past the octave into the upper structure of the harmony. Built most often on a dominant seventh, a ninth adds the note a whole step above the octave; on C, a C9 is C, E, G, B♭, D. Keep climbing and the eleventh and thirteenth follow, until a thirteenth chord in theory contains every note of the scale, though players voice only the ones that matter.

These upper notes are the same partials the ear already hears faintly ringing above any strong root. Naming them and playing them explicitly is what gives extended chords their density and shimmer. In practice the fifth, and sometimes the root, get dropped so the extension can breathe; the chord becomes less a stack than a curated selection of colours from high in the overtone series.

## Character

Extended chords sound lush, sophisticated, and full of air. Each added degree deepens the colour: the ninth is sweet and shimmering, the eleventh open and slightly tart, the thirteenth broad and luminous. Together they turn a functional chord into an atmosphere. Where a triad states a fact, an extended chord paints a whole room — the harmonic vocabulary of anyone who wants richness over bluntness.

## In Practice

Extended chords are the native language of jazz, soul, gospel, funk, and film scoring. A dominant thirteenth makes a V chord glow instead of merely pull; a ninth softens and modernises any progression. Pianists and arrangers voice them selectively, choosing which upper notes to sound and which to imply. They are the [Dominant Seventh](/knowledge/music/chords-and-progressions/chords/dominant-seventh) and its relatives grown tall, extending the logic that also produced the [Sixth Chords](/knowledge/music/chords-and-progressions/chords/sixth-chords). In pytheory they are generated by continuing the stack of thirds up the scale.
