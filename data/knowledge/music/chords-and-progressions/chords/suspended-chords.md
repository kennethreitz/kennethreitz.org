# Suspended Chords

> *A chord holding its breath, waiting to decide who it is.*

---

## The Chord

Suspended chords are triads with the third taken out and something put in its place. Since the third is the note that tells you whether a chord is major or minor, removing it leaves a harmony that has declined to say. In a sus2 the third is replaced by the second — on C that is C, D, G. In a sus4 it is replaced by the fourth — C, F, G. Either way the defining interval is gone, and what remains is open and expectant.

The suspended fourth is the older idea: a note held over from a previous chord, momentarily clashing before it slips down to the third it was always going to become. That downward resolution, from the fourth to the third, is one of the oldest gestures in Western music. The sus2 is its mirror, resolving upward or simply hanging there for its own bright, hollow sound.

## Structure

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Formula | sus2: 1 2 5 · sus4: 1 4 5 |
| Notes (on C) | Csus2: C D G · Csus4: C F G |
| Symbol | Csus2 / Csus4 |
| Quality | Open, unresolved, the third withheld |

## Character

Suspended chords feel spacious and unfixed. Without a third they escape the emotional binary of happy and sad, sounding instead like anticipation itself — a question posed, an ambiguity welcomed. The sus4 carries a gentle ache that begs to resolve; the sus2 is airier, cleaner, almost pastoral. Both give music a sense of hovering just above the ground.

## In Practice

Guitarists live in these shapes because they emerge naturally under the fingers — the ringing sus chords of folk and rock, the suspended jangle of countless intros. In a cadence the sus4 heightens the arrival by delaying the tonic’s third. Pop, worship music, and ambient writing all lean on suspensions for their unresolved glow. They resolve most naturally into the [Major Triad](/knowledge/music/chords-and-progressions/chords/major-triad) or [Minor Triad](/knowledge/music/chords-and-progressions/chords/minor-triad) whose third they withheld.
