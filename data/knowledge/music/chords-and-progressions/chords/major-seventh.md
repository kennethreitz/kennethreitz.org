# Major Seventh

> *Sunlight through a window, late in the afternoon.*

---

## The Chord

The major seventh takes a major triad and crowns it with one more note — a major seventh above the root, a half step below the octave. On C that adds a B to the familiar C, E, G, giving C, E, G, B. Structurally it is four thirds’ worth of stacking: a major third, a minor third, then another major third. The result is a chord with more colour and more tension than a plain triad, yet none of the urgency of a dominant.

The magic lies in that top note. A major seventh sits just a half step under the root’s octave, close enough to create a soft, glowing friction that never quite becomes a demand for resolution. The chord shimmers rather than pulls. It is the major triad grown wistful and sophisticated.

## Character

The major seventh is the sound of relaxed sophistication — warm, open, faintly dreamy. Where the plain major triad is bright and certain, the maj7 is bright and thoughtful, coloured by a gentle ache that reads as contentment rather than sorrow. It evokes ease, nostalgia, and a kind of soft-focus beauty, which is why it saturates jazz, bossa nova, and mellow soul.

## In Practice

The major seventh serves as a lush tonic in jazz and as a colour chord anywhere a songwriter wants warmth without weight. As the I chord of a jazz standard it establishes home with elegance; on the IV it deepens the pre-cadential glow. It is the natural extension of the [Major Triad](/knowledge/music/chords-and-progressions/chords/major-triad) and a cousin to the sweeter [Sixth Chords](/knowledge/music/chords-and-progressions/chords/sixth-chords). In pytheory it is a major triad with its seventh scale degree added.
