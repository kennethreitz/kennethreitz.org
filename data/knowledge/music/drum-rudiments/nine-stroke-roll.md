# Nine Stroke Roll

> *Four diddles and a period at the end of the sentence.*

---

## The Rudiment

The nine stroke roll is one of the most-used measured rolls, long enough to sit comfortably across a beat yet short enough to place with confidence. Four double strokes roll into a single accented tap, producing a substantial, singing roll that resolves cleanly on the pulse. It is the roll drummers reach for when a phrase needs weight without spilling into a full sustained roll, and it appears constantly across rudimental, orchestral, and kit repertoire.

## Sticking

The standard sticking is four doubles plus a terminating accent — nine strokes: **RRLL RRLL R**, accent on the final note, then reversed as **LLRR LLRR L**. The eight rolled notes are played as even doubles, closed or open, leading up to the accented ninth stroke that lands on the beat. It ends on the same hand it began, so the lead hand alternates naturally when the roll repeats.

## How to Play

Roll the four diddles low and even, keeping every second stroke of each double as full as the first, then raise and drive the ninth note for the accent. Whether played open with distinct diddles or closed into a buzz-like sustain, the target is a smooth, unbroken roll that snaps shut precisely on the accent. Slow, open practice builds the control that fast performance demands.

## In Practice

The nine stroke roll is a workhorse: orchestral swells, marching cadences, big-band figures, and drum set fills all lean on it. It extends the [Seven Stroke Roll](/knowledge/music/drum-rudiments/seven-stroke-roll) by one diddle and leads toward the [Ten Stroke Roll](/knowledge/music/drum-rudiments/ten-stroke-roll) and [Eleven Stroke Roll](/knowledge/music/drum-rudiments/eleven-stroke-roll) as the counts climb.
