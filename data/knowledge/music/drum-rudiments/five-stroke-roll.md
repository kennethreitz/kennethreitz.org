# Five Stroke Roll

> *Two diddles and a pop — the shortest complete roll.*

---

## The Rudiment

The five stroke roll is the most compact of the measured rolls and one of the most useful. It packs two double strokes into a quick roll that resolves onto a single accented tap, giving a crisp "brrr-DAT" that punctuates phrases everywhere from the concert stage to the drum set. Because it is short, it is endlessly repeatable and hand-to-hand reversible, making it the ideal entry point into the numbered-roll family and a favorite for locking rolls to the beat.

## Sticking

The standard sticking is two doubles plus a terminating accent — five strokes in all: **RRLL R**, accent on that final note, then reversed as **LLRR L**. Counted, the two diddles typically fall as the buzz and the accent lands on the beat, so the roll leads into the pulse rather than starting on it. Alternating the lead hand lets you play it continuously off either side.

## How to Play

Play the four rolled notes as clean, tight doubles — closed for a sustained sound, open for a rudimental bite — then drive the fifth stroke up for the accent. The two diddles stay low and even; only the final tap gets height and weight. Keeping the roll compact and the accent snapping cleanly on the beat is the whole exercise. Practice it leading with each hand.

## In Practice

The five stroke roll is everywhere: orchestral punctuation, marching cadences, jazz comping, and quick snare accents on the kit. It grows directly out of the [Double Stroke Open Roll](/knowledge/music/drum-rudiments/double-stroke-open-roll) and extends into the [Seven Stroke Roll](/knowledge/music/drum-rudiments/seven-stroke-roll) and [Nine Stroke Roll](/knowledge/music/drum-rudiments/nine-stroke-roll) as you add diddles.
