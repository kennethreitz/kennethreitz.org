# Single Stroke Seven

> *Seven strokes, one destination.*

---

## The Rudiment

The single stroke seven extends the idea of the single stroke four into a longer run: seven alternating single strokes that build across a beat or two and land on a final accent. It is a burst of the single stroke roll with a defined length and a clear resolution, giving the drummer a larger canvas to develop even, rapid singles that arrive precisely on the pulse. The odd number means the pattern ends on the same hand it began, then repeats from the opposite hand when strung together.

## Sticking

The standard sticking is seven alternating strokes leading to an accent: **RLRLRLR** with weight on the seventh note, or the mirror **LRLRLRL**. The seven strokes are commonly played as a septuplet across one beat, or as a group of six fast notes plus the accented arrival — either way the ear hears a smooth run gathering into a single strong landing. Describe it as seven even singles resolving onto the beat if the exact subdivision is in question.

## How to Play

Keep all six leading strokes low and matched between the hands so the run sounds seamless, then raise the stick and drive the final note for the accent. The motion lives mostly in the fingers and wrist at speed, staying loose throughout; any tension in the leading strokes will make the run uneven and rob the accent of its snap. Practice it starting from each hand.

## In Practice

The single stroke seven is a staple fill and solo figure, common in jazz, fusion, and rudimental drumming where a longer single-stroke burst is needed to reach a downbeat. It completes the single-stroke family alongside the [Single Stroke Roll](/knowledge/music/drum-rudiments/single-stroke-roll) and the [Single Stroke Four](/knowledge/music/drum-rudiments/single-stroke-four).
