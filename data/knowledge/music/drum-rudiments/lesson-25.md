# Lesson 25

> *A number for a name, and a drag for a signature.*

---

## The Rudiment

Lesson 25 carries the plainest name in the whole list — it is simply the twenty-fifth lesson from an old rudimental method, and the title stuck. Musically it is a drag-family figure: an accented, dragged note followed by two single strokes, giving a “drag-TAP, tap, tap” shape that alternates from hand to hand. The lone drag up front, the accent riding on it, and the clean singles that follow make it a cousin of the single drag tap with an extra stroke of flow.

## Sticking

Lesson 25 is conventionally written with a drag leading into an accented primary, then two singles: **llR L R** for the first pass and **rrL R L** for the second, alternating. The two lowercase strokes are the drag’s grace notes; the accent falls on the primary they lead into, and the following strokes are unaccented singles that hand off to the opposite drag. Notation and accent placement vary between sources, so hold onto the shape — accented drag, then two taps — rather than a single fixed count.

## How to Play

Play the drag as a low, crisp, controlled double, pop the accent on the primary, then let the two singles fall back to an even, quiet level. The trick is keeping those follow-up strokes relaxed and rhythmically square while the opposite hand preps the next drag. As with the rest of the family, the cleaner and more consistent the grace-note double, the better the whole figure sits.

## In Practice

Lesson 25 endures as a teaching rudiment — a tidy way to drill a single drag inside a flowing, alternating pattern — and it appears throughout traditional snare literature. On the kit it becomes a dragged, accented figure well suited to shuffles and jazz-flavored fills, and it sits comfortably alongside the single and double drag taps as part of the same lineage.
