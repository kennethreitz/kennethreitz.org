# Flam Paradiddle

> *The paradiddle, dressed for the occasion.*

---

## The Rudiment

The flam paradiddle — sometimes called the flamadiddle — is exactly what the name promises: a single paradiddle with a flam planted on its first note. The underlying RLRR / LRLL sticking of the paradiddle is untouched; the flam simply widens and accents the leading stroke of each group, so the familiar “para-diddle” lilt now opens with a fat, weighted downbeat.

It is the gateway from the flam family into the flammed hybrids, and it teaches you to launch a sticking pattern from a flam without disturbing the flow of what follows.

## Sticking

The standard PAS sticking is **lRLRR** then **rLRLL**, repeating. The lowercase stick is the grace note; the accented primary begins the paradiddle, and the remaining L-R-R (or R-L-L) proceeds exactly as an ordinary single paradiddle would, ending on the double that sets up the opposite hand’s flam. Counted in sixteenths, the flam falls on the beat.

## How to Play

Play the flam with a low soft grace and a strong accented primary, then let the paradiddle unwind at a lower, even dynamic — the accent belongs to the flam alone. The closing double should stay relaxed and rebound-driven so it flows straight into the next flam on the opposite hand. As always with flams, the grace-note height is what keeps the timing honest.

## In Practice

The flam paradiddle is a workhorse of both the rudimental pad and the drum set. On the kit it becomes an accented, orchestrated fill — flam on a tom or snare, the diddle voiced across drums — and in marching writing it drives accented sixteenth passages. It also sets up its denser relative, the flam paradiddle-diddle.
