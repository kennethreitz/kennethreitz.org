# Single Stroke Roll

> *One hand, then the other, forever — the first thing and the last thing every drummer learns.*

---

## The Rudiment

The single stroke roll is the foundation beneath everything else: a continuous stream of alternating strokes, one per hand, evenly spaced and evenly weighted. There are no doubles, no bounces, no ornaments — just a perfectly balanced pendulum swinging left and right. Simple to describe and endless to master, because the whole art lives in making the two hands indistinguishable in tone, timing, and volume across every tempo from a whisper to a blur.

## Sticking

The standard sticking is a pure alternation: **RLRL RLRL**. Every note carries equal weight in its basic form, though the roll is often practiced with accents migrating through the pattern to build control. It can be counted in any subdivision — eighths, triplets, sixteenths, sextuplets — because it is really a texture rather than a fixed figure.

## How to Play

Play it with a relaxed, controlled wrist stroke at slow to moderate speeds, keeping the stick heights matched between hands so neither side dominates. As tempo climbs, the motion transitions from wrist into fingers and a hint of forearm, and the challenge becomes evenness — most players have a stronger and a weaker hand, and the single stroke roll is where that imbalance gets exposed and corrected. Stay loose; tension is the enemy of speed.

## In Practice

The single stroke roll is everywhere: rolling fills around the toms, fast jazz and fusion phrasing, orchestral swells, drum corps runs, and the basic vocabulary of every solo. It underlies the [Single Stroke Four](/knowledge/music/drum-rudiments/single-stroke-four) and [Single Stroke Seven](/knowledge/music/drum-rudiments/single-stroke-seven) as their parent pattern, and building genuine speed here pays dividends across the entire kit.
