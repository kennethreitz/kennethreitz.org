# Double Stroke Open Roll

> *Two from the right, two from the left — the engine room of the roll.*

---

## The Rudiment

The double stroke open roll — often just "the long roll" — is the backbone of rudimental drumming. Each hand plays two strokes before passing to the other, and when those doubles are spaced evenly and accelerated, they close into the sustained roll that defines snare drum vocabulary. Played open and slow, it is a study in clean diddles; played fast and closed, it becomes a smooth, continuous sound. Nearly every numbered roll in the rudiment list is built from this pattern.

## Sticking

The standard sticking is a stream of doubles: **RRLL RRLL**. Each pair should sound as two distinct, matched notes when open, tightening as the tempo rises. Practiced open, it builds the second-stroke control that separates a clean roll from a messy one; the challenge is always making that second note of each double as strong and even as the first.

## How to Play

There are two schools: the primary-stroke approach, where each note of the double is played with a deliberate wrist or finger stroke for maximum control, and the rebound approach, where the first stroke is struck and the second is a controlled bounce. Most players use wrist-and-finger doubles when open and slow, letting rebound take over as speed increases. Keep the hands relaxed and the two strokes even in height and volume — a weak second stroke is the most common flaw.

## In Practice

The double stroke open roll underlies the entire family of measured rolls — the [Five Stroke Roll](/knowledge/music/drum-rudiments/five-stroke-roll), [Seven Stroke Roll](/knowledge/music/drum-rudiments/seven-stroke-roll), [Nine Stroke Roll](/knowledge/music/drum-rudiments/nine-stroke-roll) and beyond — and appears constantly in marching, orchestral, jazz, and kit playing wherever a sustained snare sound is called for. Mastering it unlocks the rest of the list.
