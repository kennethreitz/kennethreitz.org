# Bipolar Disorder

> *A mind tethered between two poles, swinging by the gravity of its own chemistry.*

---

## Overview

**Bipolar disorder** is a mood disorder defined by oscillation between two opposite extremes: the elevated, accelerated pole of **mania** and the flattened, weighted pole of **depression**. It is not ordinary moodiness but episodic illness, distinct stretches of days or weeks where the brain's regulation of energy, sleep, and affect comes unmoored. Between episodes, many people return to a stable baseline, which is part of what makes the condition so disorienting to live inside: the self that swings is also the self that watches.

## The Two Poles

The **manic pole** brings inflated self-esteem, reduced need for sleep, racing thoughts, pressured speech, distractibility, and impulsive pursuit of goals or pleasures, often with poor judgment about consequences. The **depressive pole** is its inverse: leaden fatigue, anhedonia, hopelessness, cognitive slowing, and sometimes suicidality. In **mixed states**, features of both arrive at once, agitated, sleepless despair, which is among the most dangerous configurations clinically.

## Bipolar I vs. Bipolar II

The two principal forms differ by the severity of the high pole. **Bipolar I** requires at least one full **manic** episode (lasting a week or causing hospitalization), with depressive episodes common but not mandatory. **Bipolar II** is defined by recurrent **depression** plus at least one **hypomanic** episode, a milder, shorter elevation that doesn't tip into full mania or psychosis. The **cycling** rhythm varies widely, from years between episodes to "rapid cycling" of four or more per year.

## Distinguishing Schizoaffective Disorder

The crucial boundary is psychosis. In bipolar disorder, psychotic features, when they appear, occur *only* during mood episodes. **Schizoaffective disorder** is diagnosed when delusions or hallucinations persist for two or more weeks *in the absence* of a mood episode, signaling a process not fully explained by mood alone.

## Treatment

The foundation is **mood stabilizers**, with **lithium** the oldest and best-evidenced, uniquely protective against suicide. **Lithium** remains Kenneth's mood-stabilizing foundation. Anticonvulsants, atypical antipsychotics, and careful antidepressant use round out the toolkit, paired with sleep regularity and psychoeducation.
