# Psychosis

> *A break in shared reality, the mind narrating a world the body alone can see.*

---

## Overview

**Psychosis** is a *state*, not a diagnosis. It describes a temporary or sustained loss of contact with consensus reality, and it can arise from many different underlying conditions. The hallmark features are **hallucinations** (perceiving what isn't there, voices being the most common), **delusions** (fixed false beliefs that resist contradicting evidence), and **disorganized thought** (speech and reasoning that derail, loosen, or fragment). What makes it disorienting is precisely that it feels *certain* from the inside; the break is invisible to the one experiencing it.

## A State Across Many Conditions

Because psychosis is a state, it shows up wherever brain chemistry tips far enough. It is the defining feature of **schizophrenia**, a recurring component of **schizoaffective disorder**, and appears during the manic or depressive extremes of **bipolar disorder**. Severe **major depression** can carry psychotic features, and **substances**, stimulants, cannabis, alcohol withdrawal, hallucinogens, can trigger episodes directly. This is why "psychotic" describes *what is happening*, while the diagnosis describes *why*.

## The Dopamine Hypothesis

The leading neurochemical account is the **dopamine hypothesis**: psychosis is associated with **excess dopamine signaling**, particularly in mesolimbic pathways, which over-tags ordinary perceptions and thoughts with significance and salience. The static of daily life becomes meaningful, threatening, or revelatory. The model is incomplete, glutamate and other systems matter too, but dopamine remains the practical target.

## Treatment

**Antipsychotics** work by **blocking or modulating dopamine** (mainly D2 receptors). Older "typical" agents block forcefully; newer "atypical" ones modulate more selectively across dopamine and serotonin. They reliably dampen acute psychosis, and maintenance dosing holds it at bay.

## A Continuum, Not a Switch

Psychosis sits on a **continuum**. Subtle forms: fleeting paranoia, a half-heard name, a too-perfect coincidence, are surprisingly common in the general population.
