# Secure Attachment

> *The child cries, the parent comes, the world makes sense. Repeat ten thousand times and you have a personality.*

---

## Overview

Secure attachment is the majority pattern — roughly sixty percent of infants in low-risk samples — and the baseline against which the insecure strategies are understood. In the [Strange Situation](/knowledge/psychology/attachment-theory/strange-situation), the secure infant (Ainsworth's group B) explores the unfamiliar room freely while the mother is present, may protest when she leaves, and at reunion does the thing that defines the category: goes to her, takes comfort, is actually comforted, and returns to play. Nothing about the pattern is stoic. The secure child feels the distress fully and expresses it plainly — what makes the child secure is not the absence of upset but the presence of trust that upset will be answered.

---

## The Secure Base

The pattern grows from caregiving that [Mary Ainsworth](/knowledge/psychology/attachment-theory/mary-ainsworth)'s sensitivity research described precisely: a caregiver who reads the infant's signals accurately and responds promptly to what the infant actually needs — not perfectly, but reliably enough. From this the child gets two things at once. A *safe haven*: somewhere to return when the world overwhelms. And a *secure base*: somewhere to leave from, which is the less obvious half and arguably the more important one. The confident toddler venturing across the playground and glancing back is running both halves of the system — exploration powered by the knowledge that retreat is available. Attachment and autonomy are not opposites in this pattern; the first funds the second.

Internally, the child is composing the [working model](/knowledge/psychology/attachment-theory/internal-working-models) this history dictates: *I am the kind of being whose signals matter, and others, on the whole, come when called.* Ruptures happen in secure dyads as in all dyads — the caregiver misreads, is tired, gets it wrong. What the secure child learns from ten thousand cycles of rupture and repair is that repair is what ruptures are *for*. Distress becomes survivable, communicable, temporary.

---

## The Adult Form

Carried forward, the pattern becomes what the Adult Attachment Interview calls secure-autonomous: an adult who can talk about their own history — good or bad — coherently, valuing attachment without being tangled in it. In relationships, secure adults are comfortable with both intimacy and independence, the same two-sided competence the toddler showed on the playground. They can ask for help without shame and give space without panic; conflict is a problem to solve rather than a threat to survival. None of this is a fixed inheritance: security is where the [anxious](/knowledge/psychology/attachment-theory/anxious-attachment), [avoidant](/knowledge/psychology/attachment-theory/avoidant-attachment), and [disorganized](/knowledge/psychology/attachment-theory/disorganized-attachment) patterns can arrive later, by another road — the road documented in [Earned Security](/knowledge/psychology/attachment-theory/earned-security).
