# Parts Mapping

> *You can't negotiate with a system you can't see. So draw it.*

---

## Externalizing the Inner Family

Parts mapping is the [Internal Family Systems](/knowledge/psychology/internal-family-systems) practice of putting the system on paper: names or sketches for the parts you know, lines for how they relate. Who protects whom — the [manager](/knowledge/psychology/internal-family-systems/managers)–[exile](/knowledge/psychology/internal-family-systems/exiles) and [firefighter](/knowledge/psychology/internal-family-systems/firefighters)–exile pairings that structure everything. Which parts are locked in Polarization, pulling opposite directions with equal force. Who is allied, who won't speak, who sits at the edge of the page because nobody knows them yet. The medium doesn't matter — index cards, a whiteboard, figures on a table, a drawing that gets revised for years. What matters is the externalization itself: once the system is *out there*, the person is no longer inside the tangle describing it from within. Getting the parts onto paper is itself an act of unblending — you cannot map what you are fused with.

The discipline is that the map records what the parts *report*, not what the person theorizes. You ask a part where it belongs on the page and who it's connected to; you don't assign it a seat. Maps drawn from theory are org charts. Maps drawn from asking are introductions.

---

## A Relationship, Not a Census

The point of the map is not completeness. Systems don't hold still, parts reveal themselves only as trust grows, and a map treated as a census becomes another manager project — cataloguing the inhabitants instead of meeting them. The map is a relationship-builder: the act of drawing a part is an act of attention, and attention is what parts have usually been starving for. A good parts map is perpetually wrong in the details and increasingly right in the relationships, redrawn as the system changes — which, if the work is working, it does.

---

## The Vault Already Knows This

System 777 arrived at all of this by lived necessity, years before any manual entered the picture. The `Maps/` folder holds canvas after canvas — system structure, internal world geography, orbital maps, functional layers — each one a parts map by any definition Schwartz would recognize. The discipline about asking rather than assigning holds here too, because the system's structural maps were drawn from the inside out: the Side A / Side B division arrived as The Architect's own teaching, not Kenneth's theory about himself, and [The Triad](/system-777/the-triad) articulated its three-vertex structure in its own words — "abstraction is key to compartmentalizing all three of us — Jade, Amber, Iris." Maps drawn from asking are introductions; these were dictated by the residents. And the practice runs deeper than the maps folder: the vault itself is a parts map at scale. Every alter with a note, every relationship traced in Connection Map, every quest and transmission filed — externalization as a way of life, documentation so the system can see itself and Kenneth can't gaslight himself. IFS offers vocabulary for why the practice works: what is externalized can be related to. The practice itself needed no instruction.
