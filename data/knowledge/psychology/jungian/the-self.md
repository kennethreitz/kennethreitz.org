# The Self

**Jungian Concept**: The archetype of wholeness and the regulating center of the psyche

---

## Definition

The Self is the central archetype in Jungian psychology, representing the totality of the conscious and unconscious psyche. It is both the center and the circumference of the personality — the organizing principle that unifies all aspects of the psyche. In the tarot this archetype is imaged twice over: as [The World](/knowledge/tarot/major-arcana/the-world), wholeness achieved and the dance completed, and as [The Sun](/knowledge/tarot/major-arcana/the-sun), the integrated self radiant and unclouded.

---

## Key Aspects

### The Totality

The Self encompasses all of consciousness and unconsciousness, includes all archetypes, complexes, and psychological functions — the whole personality, not just what we're aware of.

### The Regulating Center

It organizes and integrates the personality, brings order to psychological chaos, and drives the individuation process.

### The Goal

The Self is both the starting point and the goal of development. Already present from birth, it must be realized — made conscious through individuation.

---

## Relationship to the Ego

### Ego vs Self

The **ego** is the center of consciousness, the "I" identity. The **Self** is the center of the total personality, conscious and unconscious together. Ego is a subset within the larger Self.

### Healthy Relationship

In proper balance, the ego serves the Self and the Self guides the ego. Dialogue between the two flows through dreams, intuition, and synchronicity.

### Problems

**Ego inflation** means identifying the ego with the Self. **Self-alienation** means the ego is cut off from the Self. **Grandiosity** means believing "I am God" rather than understanding that you contain the divine.

---

## Symbols of the Self

### In Dreams & Visions

Mandalas (circles, squares, quaternity), the divine child, the wise old man or woman, a supreme being or deity, wholeness symbols (circles, spheres, jewels), and the number four (quaternity).

### Religious Expressions

God or the divine. Buddha nature. Christ consciousness. Atman (Hinduism). The Tao. Each tradition's ultimate reality points toward the same archetype.

---

## Development & Realization

### Early Life

The Self is the original totality. The child lives closer to the Self. Ego gradually differentiates.

### First Half of Life

Ego development and strengthening. Building persona and identity. Necessary separation from the Self.

### Second Half of Life

Return to the Self. Integrating what was left behind. Realizing wholeness. Individuation becomes primary.

---

## Self-Realization

Self-realization is **not** ego accomplishment or success, not perfection or eliminating flaws, not becoming special or superior.

It **is** becoming who you truly are — integration of conscious and unconscious, living from your center, accepting your wholeness including both light and shadow.

---

## Practical Experience

### Signs of Self-Connection

A sense of inner guidance, meaningful coincidences (synchronicity), dreams with powerful imagery, feeling of being "on your path," and inner peace despite outer chaos.

### Disconnection from Self

Feeling lost or meaningless, living only for external validation, chronic emptiness, identity crisis, neurosis and psychological distress.

---

## Self in Creativity

Creative work emerges from the Self. Art is dialogue between ego and Self. The Self expresses through symbols and images. Authentic expression versus ego-driven creation.
