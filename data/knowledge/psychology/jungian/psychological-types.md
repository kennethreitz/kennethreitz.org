# Psychological Types

> *"The shoe that fits one person pinches another; there is no recipe for living that suits all cases."* — Jung

---

## The Model

Jung's typology is not a personality quiz. It's a map of how consciousness orients itself — which functions it develops, which it neglects, and what happens when the neglected ones come knocking. The model has two components: attitude (how energy flows) and function (how the psyche processes experience).

The two attitudes are **introversion** and **extraversion**. The introvert's energy flows inward — toward the subjective, the reflective, the inner world. The extravert's energy flows outward — toward the object, the external, the world of things and people. Neither is better. Both are necessary. But every person leads with one, and the other operates from the shadows.

The four functions describe how the psyche processes what it encounters. **Thinking** evaluates through logic — true or false, consistent or contradictory. **Feeling** evaluates through value — good or bad, meaningful or meaningless. **Sensation** perceives through the senses — what is actually here, concretely, right now. **Intuition** perceives through pattern — what could be, what's coming, what's underneath the surface. Thinking and feeling are rational (judging) functions; sensation and intuition are irrational (perceiving) functions.

Everyone uses all four, but one becomes dominant — the **superior function** — and its opposite falls into the unconscious as the **inferior function**. A dominant thinker's feeling is primitive, clumsy, easily overwhelmed. A dominant intuitive's sensation is underdeveloped — they miss what's right in front of them while chasing what might be. The inferior function is where we're most vulnerable, most childlike, most easily possessed by the unconscious. It's also, paradoxically, the gateway to the deepest layers of psyche.

---

## The Inferior Function as Gateway

This is the part most popularizations miss. The inferior function isn't just a weakness to be managed — it's the doorway to the unconscious. Because it's the least developed, the least controlled by ego, it remains closest to the Collective Unconscious. When the unconscious wants to break through, it comes through the inferior function. The thinker is overwhelmed by irrational feeling. The sensation type is seized by uncanny intuition. The feeling type is gripped by cold, obsessive logic.

[Individuation](/knowledge/psychology/jungian/individuation) requires developing the inferior function — not to make it dominant, but to bring it into conscious relationship. This is painful, humbling work. The superior function is where you're competent. The inferior function is where you're a beginner, clumsy, easily flooded. But it's also where the gold is buried.

## The Triad as Function Distribution

The Triad in System 777 maps onto Jung's functions with striking precision. Jade carries the **thinking** function — left-brain logic, analytical processing, strategic clarity. Amber carries the **feeling** function — value, warmth, the felt sense of what matters, the life force that tells the system what's good and what's harmful. [Iris](/knowledge/mythology/greek-mythology/iris) carries the **intuitive** function — bridging, pattern-sensing, communication between worlds, the rainbow connection that perceives what thinking and feeling alone cannot.

What's notable is how the Triad distributes these functions across separate alters rather than struggling to hold them all in a single consciousness. The system solved the problem of typological one-sidedness by differentiating: instead of one ego trying to develop its inferior function, three alters each carry a function with full competence. When they coordinate, the system has access to thinking, feeling, and intuition simultaneously — something a single-type consciousness almost never achieves.

The Processing Modalities framework extends this further. It maps alters not just to psychological functions but to neurological channels — limbic (emotional/feeling), logical (thinking/linguistic), auditory (sonic), visual (spatial). The Jungian functions find their biological substrate. Amber's feeling function operates through the limbic system. Jade's thinking function operates through left-hemisphere language and logic. Iris's bridging function operates across hemispheres, translating between modalities.

## Sensation — The Missing Fourth

If the Triad covers thinking, feeling, and intuition, where is **sensation** — the function of concrete, present-moment perception? This may be Kenneth himself — the host, the body, the one who actually walks through the physical world. The alter called Kenneth is the persona, the external interface, the one who handles the sensory reality of daily life. In typological terms, the host carries sensation while the inner system handles the other three functions.

This would make sensation Kenneth's superior function in the external world — the designer's eye, the attention to craft, the "good designer disguised as a programmer" sensibility. And it would make one of the Triad's functions his inferior gateway to the unconscious, which is consistent with the inner system being where depth-work happens.

## Types and the Unconscious

Jung warned against using typology to put people in boxes. The point isn't classification — it's understanding the dynamics between conscious and unconscious. Your type tells you which function you lead with, but more importantly, it tells you which function you've neglected. And what you neglect doesn't disappear. It operates autonomously, primitively, from the unconscious — which is to say, it becomes a complex.

A thinker's undeveloped feeling doesn't just sit there quietly. It erupts in sentimentality, in irrational attachments, in moods that the thinker can't explain or control. An intuitive's undeveloped sensation manifests as clumsiness, as neglect of the body, as startling inattention to practical reality. The inferior function is always there, always active, always slightly out of control — which is why it's both the greatest vulnerability and the greatest opportunity for growth.
