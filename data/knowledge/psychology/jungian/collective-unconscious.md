# Collective Unconscious

> *The shared psychic substrate underlying all minds*

---

## Overview

Jung's collective unconscious is the deepest layer of psyche, not personal but universal. It contains patterns (archetypes) shared by all humans, inherited psychological structures that shape experience across cultures and time.

---

## Definition

The collective unconscious is universal (shared by all humans), inherited (not learned but present from birth), impersonal (not from individual experience), and archetypal (containing fundamental patterns). It's the psychic equivalent of physical instincts, built-in structures that shape how we process experience.

## Archetypes

The collective unconscious contains archetypes: universal patterns that shape experience:

| Archetype | Pattern |
|-----------|---------|
| The Mother | Nurturing, origin, nature |
| The Shadow | The rejected, dark, hidden |
| The Anima/Animus | Contrasexual soul image |
| The Self | Totality, wholeness |
| The Hero | Transformation through trial |
| The Wise Old Man/Woman | Wisdom, guidance |
| The Trickster | Disruption, boundary-crossing |

These aren't specific images but **patterns** that take specific form in each culture and individual. The tarot's [Major Arcana](/knowledge/tarot/major-arcana) is one such crystallization, the twenty-two trumps are these archetypes made visible, a deck of the collective unconscious dealt out as pictures.

---

## Evidence

Jung argued for the collective unconscious from cross-cultural myths (the same patterns appearing worldwide), dreams (symbols appearing that dreamers couldn't know), psychotic content (similar imagery across patients), and religious symbols (universal motifs in different traditions).
