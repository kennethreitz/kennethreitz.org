# Muladhara  ·  Root

> *Before you can climb, you must consent to have been born.*

---

## Essence

Muladhara sits at the base of the spine, at the perineal floor where the body meets the ground it stands upon. Its name means “root support,” and everything the subtler centres later reach for depends on the sureness of this footing. This is the chakra of incarnation itself — of having arrived, of occupying a body, of belonging to the earth and to the tribe that received you. It governs the primal questions that precede all others: Am I safe? Do I have a right to be here? Will there be enough? Where survival is not yet settled, the higher aspirations of the heart and mind cannot properly bloom; the animal keeps one ear turned toward the exit.

Here the coiled serpent Kundalini sleeps, wound three and a half times around the base, awaiting the readiness of the whole system before she begins her ascent. To tend Muladhara is to tend the foundation — food, shelter, money, ancestry, the plain fact of the flesh — with reverence rather than contempt.

## Balanced & Blocked

A rooted Muladhara feels like unremarkable well-being — the quiet confidence of someone who trusts the floor will hold. Such a person is grounded without being heavy, patient, physically present, at ease with money and matter and mortality. When the centre is blocked, the world turns precarious: chronic anxiety, a scarcity that no amount of accumulation soothes, restlessness, a sense of not quite belonging in one’s own body. When it runs overactive, the grip tightens the other way — hoarding, rigidity, an obsession with security that mistakes fear for prudence. The medicine is the same in both cases: slow down, come back into the body, feel the weight of yourself received by the ground, and let the earth do what it has always done, which is hold.
