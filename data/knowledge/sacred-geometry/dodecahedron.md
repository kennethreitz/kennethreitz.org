# Dodecahedron

> *Twelve pentagons for the twelve signs — the shape God kept back for the whole.*

---

## The Form

The dodecahedron is built from twelve regular pentagons, three meeting at each of its twenty vertices. It is the odd one among the Platonic solids, the only one whose faces are pentagons rather than triangles or squares, and with the pentagon it smuggles the golden ratio into its very construction, for phi is written into the proportions of every five-sided face. This gives the dodecahedron a subtle, irrational elegance the other solids lack, a beauty that cannot be reduced to whole numbers.

It is the dual of the icosahedron, its twelve face-centers marking that solid's twelve vertices. Plato treated the dodecahedron apart from the others, writing that the god used it for the whole, arranging the constellations upon it. Later tradition read its twelve faces as the twelve signs of the zodiac, making the solid a model of the heavens themselves, the container that holds the four elemental bodies as the cosmos holds the world.

## Meaning

Where the other four solids are given to the elements of the sublunary world, the dodecahedron is reserved for Aether, the fifth essence, the quintessence that pervades and transcends the material. In [Alchemy](/knowledge/alchemy) the quintessence is the refined spirit distilled out of the four elements, the subtle unity behind their multiplicity, the aim of the great work. The dodecahedron thus becomes the geometry of the whole and the heavenly, the vessel of the zodiac and the emblem of that which contains all else. Its pentagonal faces, laced with the golden proportion, tie it to the [Golden Ratio](/knowledge/sacred-geometry/golden-ratio) and to the sense that the cosmos is ordered by a beauty just beyond the reach of counting.
