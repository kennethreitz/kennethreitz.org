# Norepinephrine

> *The alarm chemical — the surge that widens the eyes and readies the body to run before the mind has finished deciding.*

---

## Overview

**Norepinephrine** (noradrenaline) is a catecholamine that acts as both neurotransmitter and stress hormone, the brain's master dial for **arousal and vigilance**. Where Dopamine governs wanting and Serotonin modulates mood, norepinephrine sets the level of alertness itself — how awake, how attentive, how braced for action the whole system is. It sharpens focus onto what matters, raises heart rate and blood pressure, and mobilizes the body for effort. In small measure it is the crispness of a good morning; in flood it is the jolt of a near-miss on the highway. It is the chemistry of paying attention because something might be at stake.

Its kinship with dopamine is literal: norepinephrine is synthesized *from* dopamine by a single enzyme, so the two catecholamines share the same precursor chain and often the same fate. The related molecule **epinephrine** (adrenaline), poured out by the adrenal glands under the [HPA Axis](/knowledge/neuroscience/hpa-axis) and sympathetic drive, is the bloodstream hormone that carries the same alarm to the body's periphery — heart, lungs, muscle — while norepinephrine carries it through the brain.

## What It Does

Most of the brain's norepinephrine originates in a tiny nucleus in the brainstem called the **locus coeruleus** — "the blue spot" — whose few thousand neurons project almost everywhere, a small switchboard with vast reach. It acts through **alpha** and **beta adrenergic receptors** distributed across cortex, [Amygdala](/knowledge/neuroscience/amygdala), and body alike, which is why a single arousal signal can simultaneously sharpen cortical attention and quicken the heart. Its firing tracks arousal: quiet in sleep, moderate and phasic during focused attention, and high under stress or threat. This is the central arm of the **fight-or-flight** response, working alongside the sympathetic nervous system and adrenal output. There is an inverted-U to it — the Yerkes-Dodson relationship: too little and you are drowsy and unfocused, too much and attention fractures into jittery hypervigilance, and the [Prefrontal Cortex](/knowledge/neuroscience/prefrontal-cortex)'s deliberate control gives way to faster reflexive circuits. Optimal arousal sits in the middle, and norepinephrine is what tunes the position.

## Anxiety, PTSD, and Hypervigilance

When the norepinephrine system is chronically overactive, the body lives in a state of alarm it cannot switch off — the hallmark of both Anxiety and PTSD. In PTSD especially, an over-responsive locus coeruleus underlies the **hypervigilance**, exaggerated startle, and intrusive re-experiencing: the alarm keeps sounding for a threat that has already passed, and the noradrenergic surge stamps traumatic memories in with unusual vividness, which is part of why they intrude so forcefully. This is why the blood-pressure drug **prazosin**, which blocks noradrenergic alpha-1 receptors, is used off-label to quiet trauma nightmares — it dials down the surge that keeps the sleeping brain braced for danger. **Beta-blockers** like propranolol act on the peripheral side, blunting the racing heart and tremor of acute anxiety, and drugs that raise norepinephrine, the SNRIs and atomoxetine, are used deliberately in depression and attention disorders — the same dial turned the other way. Understanding norepinephrine is understanding why a nervous system, once taught to expect threat, has such trouble standing down.
