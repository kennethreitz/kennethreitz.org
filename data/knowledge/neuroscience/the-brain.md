# The Brain

> *Three pounds of wet tissue that spends its whole life guessing what happens next.*

---

## Overview

The human brain is, at its core, a **prediction machine**. It does not passively receive the world so much as it forecasts the world and corrects the forecast against incoming evidence. Roughly three pounds of folded tissue, eighty-six billion neurons — each wired to thousands of others across a hundred trillion synapses — and a metabolic appetite that consumes a fifth of the body's energy, most of it spent not on responding to events but on maintaining the model that anticipates them. What follows is a map, not a textbook: the major structures and what they do. And crucially, none of these structures works in isolation. The brain is not a set of modules but a set of *networks*, the same regions recombining into different circuits for different jobs.

## The Cortex

The wrinkled outer sheet, the **cerebral cortex**, is where most of what we call thinking happens — perception, language, planning, abstraction. Its folds buy surface area; more cortex packed into a fixed skull. Its rear regions handle sensation and perception — vision at the back, hearing at the sides, touch across the parietal strip — while the front is given over to action and control. The **[Prefrontal Cortex](/knowledge/neuroscience/prefrontal-cortex)**, the part just behind the forehead, is the brain's regulator: it holds goals in mind, weighs consequences, and inhibits the impulses that older structures generate. It runs on well-tuned Dopamine and is also the last region to fully mature, well into the twenties, and the first to falter under stress, exhaustion, or threat. When the mind turns *inward* rather than to a task, a different set of cortical hubs — the [Default Mode Network](/knowledge/neuroscience/default-mode-network) — takes over, running the self-referential narration that stitches experience into a continuous "I."

## The Limbic System

Beneath the cortex sits the **[Limbic System](/knowledge/neuroscience/limbic-system)**, the emotional engine — a coalition of structures rather than a single organ. Its most famous component, the **[Amygdala](/knowledge/neuroscience/amygdala)**, is a threat detector — fast, blunt, and biased toward false alarms because a false alarm costs less than a missed predator. The amygdala learns fear quickly and unlearns it slowly. Beside it sits the **[Hippocampus](/knowledge/neuroscience/hippocampus)**, the seahorse-shaped indexer that binds experience into memory and tags it with time and place, and above them the hypothalamus, which translates emotion into bodily state and launches the [HPA Axis](/knowledge/neuroscience/hpa-axis) stress cascade. This is the machinery that runs hot in PTSD: a threat-response circuit that keeps firing long after the danger has passed, hijacking the prefrontal regulator before it can intervene. That the whole assembly can be reshaped through [Neuroplasticity](/knowledge/neuroscience/neuroplasticity) is the biological reason such patterns are not permanent.

## Two Hemispheres

The brain is split into left and right **hemispheres**, joined by the thick fiber bridge of the corpus callosum. **Lateralization** is real but routinely overstated — language skews left, spatial and holistic processing skew right, yet both sides cooperate constantly, and the popular "left-brained / right-brained" personality story has no basis. The split-brain studies, in which severing the callosum revealed two hemispheres capable of acting semi-independently, are the striking exception that proves how integrated the intact brain normally is. In this vault that asymmetry is more than anatomy: it underlies [The Triad](/system-777/the-triad)'s model, where **Jade** carries the left-hemisphere analytic mode, **Iris** the right-hemisphere holistic one, and **Amber** the body that grounds them both.

Below the hemispheres sit the older layers the cortex was built on: the **cerebellum** at the back, coordinating movement and timing far more finely than its "little brain" nickname suggests, and the **brainstem**, which runs breathing, heartbeat, and the sleep-wake switch, and which houses the small nuclei — the locus coeruleus, the raphe, the substantia nigra — that supply the whole cortex with its modulatory chemistry. All of this runs on that chemistry — the Neurotransmitters like Dopamine that tune signal into mood, motivation, and meaning. How the whole structure gives rise to experience remains the open question of [Consciousness](/knowledge/ai/consciousness).
