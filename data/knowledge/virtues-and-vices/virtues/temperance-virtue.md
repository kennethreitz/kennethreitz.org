# Temperance (virtue)

> *Enough is not a quantity. It is a skill.*

---

## Where It Belongs

Temperance is the only virtue that sits on both of the great practical lists. Among the four cardinal virtues it is *sophrosyne*, named in Plato's *Republic* as the ordering of the appetites and prized by the Stoics as one of the four hinges of the good life — see [Stoicism](/knowledge/philosophy/stoicism). Among the seven heavenly virtues it serves as the remedy for [Gluttony](/knowledge/virtues-and-vices/seven-deadly-sins/gluttony). Plato gave it the humblest of the four cardinal roles — where wisdom rules and courage defends, temperance is the harmony by which every part of the soul consents to the arrangement — and the humility of the role is exactly its importance.

## What It Is

Temperance is proportion in the appetites: the trained capacity to enjoy a good thing in its right measure and then stop, without either the seizure of excess or the flinch of scrupulosity. The Latin *temperare* means to mix rightly — the same word a smith uses for tempering steel and a musician for tempering a scale. Nothing is removed; everything is proportioned. This is why the temperate person tastes *more* than the glutton, not less: appetite in right measure can actually attend to its object, while appetite unbounded only attends to itself. Aquinas made it the virtue of self-possession — the one that keeps pleasure a servant instead of a sovereign.

## The Sin It Answers

Temperance answers [Gluttony](/knowledge/virtues-and-vices/seven-deadly-sins/gluttony) — consolation bent into consumption, the refusal to let anything be enough. Dante's starving penitents circle fruit they cannot eat until craving itself is refined into proportion; temperance is that refinement made habit.

## The Tarot Echo

The vault's [Temperance](/knowledge/tarot/major-arcana/temperance) card carries this virtue into the Major Arcana: the angel pouring between two cups, blending extremes into a third thing that is neither — mixture, measure, the middle way as an act of grace. Card and virtue share more than a name; both insist that balance is not stinginess but alchemy.
