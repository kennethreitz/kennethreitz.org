# Justice (virtue)

> *Justice is love working at scale — the shape charity takes between strangers.*

---

## Where It Belongs

Justice is the second of the four cardinal virtues, alongside [Prudence](/knowledge/virtues-and-vices/virtues/prudence), [Fortitude](/knowledge/virtues-and-vices/virtues/fortitude), and [Temperance](/knowledge/virtues-and-vices/virtues/temperance-virtue) — the list that begins in Plato's *Republic* and runs through the Stoics into Aquinas. See [Stoicism](/knowledge/philosophy/stoicism), where justice governs everything owed to others. For Plato it was the crowning virtue, and his definition is worth keeping: justice is each part doing its own work — in the city, each class; in the soul, reason, spirit, and appetite each in right relation. Injustice, on this reading, is not first a crime but a disorder: something in the whole usurping a place that is not its own.

## What It Is

The Roman jurists compressed it into a formula the Middle Ages never improved: *justice is the constant and perpetual will to render to each what is due* — *suum cuique*, to each their own. Two words in that formula carry the weight. It is a *will*, a settled disposition rather than an occasional mood of fairness; and it is *constant*, extending to people one dislikes, cases where one loses, and days when no one is watching. Among the cardinal virtues it is the only one aimed wholly outward — prudence perfects one's seeing, fortitude and temperance one's passions, but justice exists entirely in relation, which is why the tradition ranked it highest of the moral virtues.

## Its Opposite

Justice has no single deadly sin as its opposite — injustice is rather the *social form of every sin*: [Greed](/knowledge/virtues-and-vices/seven-deadly-sins/greed) takes what is another's due, [Envy](/knowledge/virtues-and-vices/seven-deadly-sins/envy) resents it, [Wrath](/knowledge/virtues-and-vices/seven-deadly-sins/wrath) seizes the judge's bench, [Pride](/knowledge/virtues-and-vices/seven-deadly-sins/pride) exempts the self from the law it applies to others. That breadth is the point. Where the remedial virtues each answer one passion, justice answers the sum of their damage.

## The Tarot Echo

The vault's [Justice](/knowledge/tarot/major-arcana/justice) card carries the virtue into the Major Arcana — the crowned figure with sword and scales, weighing exactly, cutting cleanly. Card and virtue agree on the essentials: measure before the blade, and no thumb on the scale.
