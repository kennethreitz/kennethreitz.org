# Envy

> *Envy is the only sin that never once feels good.*

---

## The Tradition

Envy is the one sin on the list that Evagrius did not name. His eight thoughts included *lype* — sadness, a grieving over what one lacks — and Gregory the Great, condensing the eight to seven around 590, sharpened that sadness into *invidia*: sorrow at another's good. The Latin root is telling — *invidere*, to look against, to see with hostility. Envy is a way of seeing before it is a way of acting, which is why Gregory ranked it just below pride, its natural child: once I must be the source of my own worth, your flourishing becomes my injury.

## The Disordered Desire

Beneath envy is a starved love of the good — the sufferer genuinely perceives what is excellent, in the neighbor's talent or luck or peace, and cannot bear that it lives somewhere else. The desire to *have* curdles into the desire that the other *lose*. It is misdiagnosed as wanting things; really it is a wound about being someone, the conviction that another's fullness proves my emptiness. Of all the sins it is the most self-punishing, joyless even in imagination, which the tradition read as its signature: the sin that torments only its host.

## In Purgatorio

On the second terrace, Dante's envious sit in haircloth against the cliff, leaning on one another like blind beggars, and their eyes — the offending organ — are sewn shut with iron wire. Having looked against their neighbors in life, they now cannot look at all, and must learn to be held up by the very people they once resented. Voices fly past overhead crying exempla of generous love, and the whips and bridles of the terrace all concern the eyes.

## The Remedy

The counter-practice is [Kindness](/knowledge/virtues-and-vices/virtues/kindness) — willing and working the neighbor's good until the neighbor's good stops feeling like theft. The tradition's insight is behavioral: envy is not argued away but practiced away, one act of unforced goodwill at a time.

## The Enneagram Passion

In the [Enneagram](/knowledge/enneagram), envy is the passion of [Type 4](/knowledge/enneagram/type-4-the-individualist) — the persistent sense that others received a wholeness the Four was born without, the eye forever drawn to the fullness of elsewhere. The Four's longing is envy rendered romantic, and its healing is the same as Dante's terrace: learning to see without the wire of comparison.
