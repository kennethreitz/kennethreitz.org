# Gluttony

> *Gluttony is not hunger. It is the refusal to let anything be enough.*

---

## The Tradition

Gluttony (*gastrimargia*, madness of the belly) came first on Evagrius's list — not because it was gravest but because it was the beginner's battle, the appetite a monk met before any subtler thought could take hold. Cassian kept it first for the same reason; Gregory the Great carried it into the seven as *gula* and catalogued its five modes with scholastic relish: eating too soon, too expensively, too much, too eagerly, too daintily. The last one matters — the tradition thought fussiness was as gluttonous as excess, because the sin was never about quantity.

## The Disordered Desire

Beneath gluttony is the desire for consolation, bent into consumption. It is the reflex that treats every discomfort — boredom, loneliness, anxiety, the ache of an ordinary afternoon — as a hunger, because hunger is the one ache that can be fed on demand. The object barely matters: food, drink, novelty, content, stimulation of any kind. What defines the sin is the *eagerness*, the using of pleasure as anesthetic, the inability to be present to a lack. Which is why the glutton is never satisfied: the meal keeps answering a question the emptiness never asked.

## In Purgatorio

Dante's sixth terrace starves its penitents into wisdom. The gluttonous circle two strange trees hung with fragrant fruit and washed by clear spray, and may not eat or drink; their bodies waste until the sockets of their eyes spell, to Dante's reading, the word *omo* — the human face reduced to its signature. The poet's friend Forese Donati, unrecognizable in his gauntness, explains the paradox: the craving itself, redirected, has become their purification, and they circle the tree in something like joy.

## The Remedy

The counter-practice is [Temperance](/knowledge/virtues-and-vices/virtues/temperance-virtue) — not deprivation but proportion, the trained ability to enjoy a good thing in its right measure and then stop. The temperate person tastes more than the glutton, not less; enough, it turns out, is a skill.

## The Enneagram Passion

In the [Enneagram](/knowledge/enneagram), gluttony is the passion of [Type 7](/knowledge/enneagram/type-7-the-enthusiast) — consumption widened from the table to the calendar. The Seven devours experiences, plans, and possibilities, keeping every option open and every moment stimulating so that pain never catches up. It is the desert's diagnosis at modern scale: appetite as flight from the ache of the present.
