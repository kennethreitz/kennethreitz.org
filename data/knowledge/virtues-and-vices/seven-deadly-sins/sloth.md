# Sloth

> *The noonday demon does not tempt you to do evil. It tempts you to stop caring.*

---

## The Tradition

Sloth has the strangest pedigree of the seven. Evagrius knew it as *acedia*, the "noonday demon" — the restless listlessness that strikes the monk mid-day, making the cell unbearable, the hours endless, the whole vocation seem pointless. He counted it and *lype*, sadness, as separate thoughts; Gregory the Great merged them into *tristitia*, and the later Middle Ages renamed the result *acedia* again, gradually flattening it into the "laziness" the word sloth now suggests. The flattening lost the point. The desert never thought the problem was inactivity — acedia is perfectly compatible with frantic busyness, as long as the busyness avoids the one thing that matters.

## The Disordered Desire

Sloth is not the absence of desire but its evasion — a sorrow in the face of the good, the shrinking from what would demand the whole self. Aquinas defined it as sadness about spiritual good: the good is seen, acknowledged, and quietly declined because the cost of caring feels unbearable. Beneath it is usually a self-forgetting — it is easier to go numb, to drift, to let the days administrate themselves, than to want something enough to be changed by it. Of all the sins it is the most invisible, because it produces no dramatic wreckage. It just lets the life go unlived.

## In Purgatorio

Dante places sloth on the fourth terrace, the mountain's exact middle — the sin of deficient love, the hinge between love perverted below and love excessive above. The penance is pure motion: the slothful run, ceaselessly, day and night, shouting exempla of zeal as they pass — Mary hastening to the hill country, Caesar striking at Ilerda. Having loved too little and too slowly, they now cannot stop.

## The Remedy

The counter-practice is [Diligence](/knowledge/virtues-and-vices/virtues/diligence) — not busyness but *zeal*, the sustained fidelity of attention and effort to what one actually loves. The runner's cure works because sloth is momentum-shaped: caring, like running, is rebuilt one committed stride at a time.

## The Enneagram Passion

In the [Enneagram](/knowledge/enneagram), sloth is the passion of [Type 9](/knowledge/enneagram/type-9-the-peacemaker) — a self-forgetting rather than a laziness. The Nine can be endlessly industrious about everything except its own life, merging with others' agendas to avoid the conflict of having one. It is acedia in its original sense: the quiet refusal to show up for oneself.
