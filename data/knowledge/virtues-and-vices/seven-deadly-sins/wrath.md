# Wrath

> *Anger is a fire that borrows its heat from something it will not name.*

---

## The Tradition

Anger (*orge*) sat in the middle of Evagrius's eight thoughts, and he described it with a clinician's precision: a boiling of the spirit against one who has injured us — or seems to have — that persists after the moment, distorts prayer, and returns at night as the face of the offender. Gregory the Great carried it into the seven as *ira*. The tradition was careful to distinguish the sin from the emotion: anger itself can be just, and Aquinas held that the failure to be angry at real evil is its own defect. Wrath is what anger becomes when it stops serving justice and starts serving the self.

## The Disordered Desire

Underneath wrath is a love of justice bent into a love of retribution — the legitimate perception that something is *wrong* fused to the illegitimate demand that I be the one to make it pay. It runs on a hidden sweetness: rehearsing the grievance, perfecting the comeback, savoring the imagined reckoning. What it protects, usually, is a wound too tender to look at directly; rage is easier than grief. This is why wrath so often wears the costume of principle — the angriest people are frequently the ones most certain they are right, which is what makes the fire so hard to put down.

## In Purgatorio

Dante's third terrace purges wrath in acrid black smoke, so dense the penitents cannot see — the outer blindness matching the inner one, since anger, as Dante has it, blinds the eye of judgment. The souls walk through the dark singing the Agnus Dei in perfect unison, learning the gentleness they refused, while visions of patience — Mary in the temple, Stephen forgiving his killers — arrive directly in the mind, bypassing the smoked-out eyes.

## The Remedy

The counter-practice is [Patience](/knowledge/virtues-and-vices/virtues/patience) — not the suppression of anger but the willingness to suffer a wrong without demanding immediate repayment, keeping the eye of judgment clear long enough to tell justice from revenge.

## The Enneagram Passion

In the [Enneagram](/knowledge/enneagram), anger is the passion of [Type 1](/knowledge/enneagram/type-1-the-reformer) — and characteristically it goes underground, emerging not as rage but as resentment, criticism, and the relentless internal auditor. The One's anger is wrath moralized, boiling at a world that refuses to be correct, and its healing mirrors Dante's terrace: serenity, the smoke clearing into sight.
