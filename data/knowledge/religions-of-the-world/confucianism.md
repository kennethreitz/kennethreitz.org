# Confucianism

> *Is it not a joy to learn and to practice what one has learned? Is it not a joy to have friends come from afar?*

---

## Overview

Confucianism is the tradition of ethical, social, and spiritual cultivation that grew from the teaching of **Confucius** (Kong Fuzi, "Master Kong," 551–479 BCE) and shaped East Asian civilization more deeply than any other single body of thought. Whether it is a religion at all is a genuinely open question — it has no creator god, no clergy in the usual sense, and little to say about the afterlife — yet it carries a full religious weight of ritual, reverence, sacred texts, and a vision of the cosmos as a moral order in which humans participate. Its wager is that the way to transcendence runs directly through the ordinary: family, work, courtesy, and the slow craft of becoming a decent human being.

---

## Origins and History

Confucius lived in the same era of political disintegration that produced [Taoism](/knowledge/religions-of-the-world/taoism) — the late Zhou dynasty, when the old feudal order was collapsing into the Warring States. He was a minor official from the state of Lu who spent years wandering between courts, trying and failing to find a ruler who would govern by virtue rather than force. He considered himself a transmitter, not an inventor, restoring the ritual culture of the early Zhou. His students recorded his conversations in the *Analects* after his death, and his teaching survived where his career had not.

Two great successors developed the tradition in opposite directions: **Mencius** (4th century BCE) argued that human nature is fundamentally good, a sprout needing only cultivation, while **Xunzi** held it crooked timber requiring the discipline of ritual to straighten. Under the Han dynasty (2nd century BCE), Confucianism became state orthodoxy, and its texts the basis of the imperial examination system — for roughly two thousand years, the road to government office in China ran through the Confucian classics. In the Song dynasty, **Neo-Confucianism** (Zhu Xi, and later Wang Yangming) rebuilt the tradition into a full metaphysics, absorbing challenges from [Buddhism](/knowledge/religions-of-the-world/buddhism) and Taoism while insisting on the reality and goodness of the everyday world. The 20th century battered it — the New Culture Movement and later the Communist state blamed Confucianism for China's stagnation — but it never left the culture's bones, and both academic "New Confucianism" and official rehabilitation have brought it back into the open.

## Core Beliefs

At the center is **ren** — humaneness, benevolence, co-humanity — the quality of being fully and generously human toward others. Confucius refused to define it cleanly, but he gave its negative formulation plainly: *do not impose on others what you would not wish for yourself*, a version of the golden rule predating its Western counterpart by half a millennium. Ren is expressed through **li** — ritual propriety, which spans everything from solemn ancestral rites to the way you greet a colleague. Ritual, in this vision, is not empty formalism but the choreography of respect: outward forms that train inward dispositions, virtue ethics practiced through the body.

Around these orbit the other constant virtues: **yi** (righteousness — doing what is right because it is right), **zhi** (wisdom), **xin** (trustworthiness), and above all **xiao** (filial piety), the gratitude and care owed to parents and ancestors, which Confucians take to be the root from which all wider morality grows. Society is understood through the **five relationships** — ruler and minister, father and son, husband and wife, elder and younger brother, friend and friend — each carrying mutual obligations, not mere obedience: the son owes reverence, but the father owes kindness; the minister owes loyalty, but the ruler owes justice. The ideal product of all this cultivation is the **junzi**, the "exemplary person," whose nobility comes from character rather than birth. Heaven (**tian**) presides over it all — not a personal god so much as the moral order of the cosmos, whose mandate legitimates rulers and withdraws from tyrants.

## Practices

Confucian practice is life itself, done attentively. Its most visibly religious form is **ancestor veneration** — offerings, tablets, and rites of remembrance that bind the generations — along with ceremonies honoring Confucius in temples across East Asia. But the deeper practice is self-cultivation: study, reflection, the rectification of one's conduct, and the disciplined performance of li in every social interaction. Education holds a place close to sacrament; the tradition essentially invented the idea of the scholar as moral exemplar and the examination as a ladder of merit. Where [Taoism](/knowledge/religions-of-the-world/taoism) cultivates spontaneity and retreat, Confucianism cultivates responsibility and presence — the two have functioned for two millennia as complementary poles of the Chinese soul, and many people have simply been Confucian at the office and Taoist in the mountains.

---

## Sacred Texts

The canon settled into the **Four Books** — the *Analects*, the *Mencius*, the *Great Learning*, and the *Doctrine of the Mean* — and the **Five Classics**: the *Book of Songs*, *Book of Documents*, *Book of Rites*, *I Ching*, and the *Spring and Autumn Annals*. Zhu Xi's arrangement of the Four Books became the core curriculum of the examination system, making these among the most-memorized texts in human history.

## Branches and Schools

| School | Character |
|--------|-----------|
| **Mencian tradition** | Human nature is good; cultivation as nurturing innate sprouts of virtue |
| **Xunzi's tradition** | Human nature is unruly; ritual and teaching as corrective discipline |
| **Neo-Confucianism (Cheng-Zhu)** | Zhu Xi's rationalist synthesis; principle (*li*) investigated through study |
| **School of Mind (Lu-Wang)** | Wang Yangming's idealism; principle found within, knowledge and action as one |
| **New Confucianism** | Modern revival engaging democracy, science, and global philosophy |

The tradition also traveled: Korea built the most thoroughly Confucian society in history under the Joseon dynasty, Japan wove Confucian ethics into samurai and civic culture alongside [Shinto](/knowledge/religions-of-the-world/shinto) and Buddhism, and Vietnam ran its own examination system for centuries.
