# Walking Each Other Home

> *"We're all just walking each other home."*

---

## The Phrase

Of everything Ram Dass said, this is the line people keep. *We're all just walking each other home.* It reframes the whole human situation in a single gentle image. We are not strangers passing on the way to separate fates; we are companions on one road, and the road leads somewhere, and the kindest thing any of us does is keep each other company while we walk it. Home is where we're headed and where we came from. The phrase became the title of the book he wrote late in life with Mirabai Bush — *Walking Each Other Home* (2018) — a set of conversations on aging, dying, and being present for both, in ourselves and in the people we love.

---

## Being With Dying

Long before that book, Ram Dass had made the deathbed part of his classroom. Through the Living/Dying Project and years of sitting with people in their final months, he learned to be present at the one door everyone eventually walks through, and to help others be present too — not fixing, not fleeing, not pretending, just staying. His counsel was consolation without denial. Death, in his teaching, is not an ending but a transition, the way a room is not destroyed when you step out of it. The fear we carry is mostly the ego's fear of its own dissolution, and the ego is not the whole of us. So he taught people to loosen their grip early, in life, so that the final letting-go might feel less like being torn from something and more like setting down a bag you'd been carrying a long way.

---

## Somebody, Nobody, Everybody

He mapped the journey in three words. We spend our lives becoming *somebody* — a name, a résumé, a story we defend. The spiritual work, and then aging, and then death, ask us to become *nobody* — to let the story thin out, the roles fall away, the somebody we worked so hard to build quietly relax its grip. And on the far side of nobody, he said, is *everybody* — the small separate self dissolving not into nothing but into everything, into the loving awareness that was never only yours. Ego dissolution as expansion rather than loss. This is the same map his own [Loving Awareness](/knowledge/spirituality/ram-dass/loving-awareness) draws, now turned to face the last threshold.

---

## Conscious Aging

Growing old, he insisted, need not be only decline. It can be a curriculum. The body's losses, met with awareness, become teachers of non-attachment; the narrowing of the outer life can open the inner one. Conscious aging means meeting each subtraction — of strength, of speed, of role — as an invitation to rest deeper in what cannot be subtracted. He modeled it himself in the long, quiet years after his stroke, aging in public, unafraid, showing that a life can grow more luminous as it grows more frail. Walking each other home is not a grim duty. It is, in his telling, the tenderest thing we get to do for one another.
