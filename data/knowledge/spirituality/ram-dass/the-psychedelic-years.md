# The Psychedelic Years

> *"The trip was a glimpse, not a home."*

---

## Richard Alpert at Harvard

Before he was Ram Dass, he was Dr. Richard Alpert, and he had arrived. A psychology professor at Harvard with appointments across departments, an apartment full of nice things, a Mercedes, an airplane, and the particular hollow feeling of a man who has everything the culture told him to want and still, at the center, feels the ache untouched. He was, by every outward measure, a success. He also suspected that the maps of the mind he was teaching left out the most important territory — that the psychology of his day had gone quiet exactly where the real questions began.

---

## The Harvard Psilocybin Project

Then came Timothy Leary, and psilocybin. Together they launched the Harvard Psilocybin Project in 1960, running experiments with the newly available compound — prisoners, divinity students, willing volunteers, and, increasingly, themselves. For Alpert the experience did what a lifetime of achievement hadn't. It cracked the walls. For a few hours the separate, defended self loosened and something vast and quiet showed through, and he knew, the way you know a thing in the body, that the maps had indeed left out the territory. The research grew less careful and more evangelical, and Harvard grew alarmed. In 1963 Alpert was dismissed — as the account goes, for giving psilocybin to an undergraduate, crossing a line the university would not overlook. Leary was pushed out the same season. They decamped to a rambling estate at Millbrook, New York, and kept going, turning it into a laboratory and commune of the psychedelic frontier.

---

## The Honest Limit

And here is the turn that makes this a prologue and not a legend. Alpert was honest enough to notice what the drug could not do. The psychedelic opened a door onto a room of extraordinary beauty and depth — but he could not *stay* in the room. The state always faded. He would touch the vast, luminous, egoless place and then, hours later, come down, still restless, still Richard Alpert, still ashore on the same small self with a memory of the ocean. He tried to live there and couldn't. The doorway was real; the residence was not for sale at that price. That limit — clearly seen, not denied — is precisely what sent him to India in 1967, looking for someone who lived *permanently* in the place he could only visit. He found [Neem Karoli Baba](/knowledge/spirituality/ram-dass/neem-karoli-baba), a man the medicine couldn't touch because he was already past it, and the psychedelic chapter closed. It had opened the question. It could not answer it. What came after — [Be Here Now](/knowledge/spirituality/ram-dass/be-here-now), and the long teaching life — was the answer.
