# Neem Karoli Baba

> *"Feed everyone, serve everyone, love everyone, remember God."*

---

## The Meeting

In 1967 a restless ex-Harvard psychologist named Richard Alpert wandered into the Himalayan foothills and met an old man wrapped in a plaid blanket. What happened next became one of the founding stories of Western spirituality. As the tale is told, Maharaj-ji asked Alpert about the medicine he carried — the psychedelics that had opened so many doors and closed none of them — and asked to try it. Alpert, uneasy, handed over a large dose. Accounts vary on the exact amount, and it is best kept general, but by every telling it was enough to flatten several people, and Maharaj-ji swallowed all of it. Nothing happened. He sat there, twinkling, entirely unmoved. Whatever chemistry could reach, this man was already past. For Alpert it was the end of one road and the beginning of another — the moment recounted at the start of [Be Here Now](/knowledge/spirituality/ram-dass/be-here-now) — and out of it came a new name, Ram Dass, servant of God.

---

## The Mystery and the Blanket

Almost nothing is reliably known of his early life. Neem Karoli Baba left few facts and no autobiography — even the name is one of several, taken from a village where he was said to have sat. He seemed to arrive already old, already free. He wore a simple blanket, ate what was put in front of him, and gave away whatever came his way. Around him grew temples, most famously Kainchi Dham in the hills near Nainital, where devotees still gather. He performed no grand public role; people simply found themselves changed in his presence, loved in a way that asked nothing back and saw straight through them.

---

## The Simplest Teaching

He gave no elaborate philosophy. Pressed for instruction, he would say some version of the same handful of words: *feed everyone, serve everyone, love everyone, remember God.* That was the whole path — bhakti, the [yoga of devotion](/knowledge/spirituality/yoga/four-yogas/bhakti-yoga), love as the road home. Not a doctrine to master but a heart to open. Serve the person in front of you as if they were God, because they are; remember God in everything, because there is nothing else. In the [Ram Dass](/knowledge/spirituality/ram-dass) framing, the guru is finally a mirror for unconditional love — and "the guru is within you." Maharaj-ji pointed people not toward himself but through himself, toward the love he was so plainly made of.

---

## The Western Devotees

He drew an unlikely constellation of Westerners who would carry the transmission home. [Ram Dass](/knowledge/spirituality/ram-dass) became the storyteller and teacher. Krishna Das and Jai Uttal turned the devotion into kirtan, chanting his name into concert halls. Larry Brilliant, sent by Maharaj-ji into public health, went on to help lead the campaign that eradicated smallpox from the earth — service as spiritual practice made literal. Lama Surya Das carried it toward Buddhist teaching. Different tongues, different vocations, one root. They had each come looking for something and found instead someone who simply loved them, and spent the rest of their lives passing that on.
