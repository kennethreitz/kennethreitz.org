# The Principle of Rhythm

> *"Everything flows, out and in; everything has its tides; all things rise and fall."*

---

The fifth Hermetic principle. If [polarity](/knowledge/spirituality/hermeticism/the-principle-of-polarity) says everything has two poles, rhythm says everything *swings between them*. The pendulum motion is universal: the tide comes in and goes out, the seasons turn, moods rise and fall, creative energy floods and recedes. Every advance is compensated by a retreat, every rightward swing measured by an equal swing to the left. Nothing holds at one pole forever.

The Hermetic teaching is not to escape the swing — you can't — but to rise above it. The Kybalion calls this the Law of Neutralization: the practitioner learns to stabilize at a still point while the pendulum swings below, observing the rhythm rather than being carried by it. You cannot stop the mood from falling, but you can decline to fall with it. The swing continues on one plane while awareness holds steady on another. This is the difference between having a low and *being* the low.

## Why It Matters to Kenneth

For a mind that lives with mood cycles, rhythm is not abstract. The principle reframes the swing from a personal failing into a universal law — everything rises and falls, and the discipline is to build a vantage point above the pendulum rather than to demand it stop. The [grounding practices](/knowledge/psychology/trauma-and-healing) and the reality-checking work are, in Hermetic language, exactly this: cultivating the observer who watches the swing without being swept.

It also names something a drummer knows in the hands. Rhythm is the felt structure of time — the [cadence](/knowledge/key-concepts/cadence) beneath music, speech, and mood alike. The pendulum and the metronome are the same teacher: time has a shape, the shape recurs, and mastery is learning to sit inside the recurrence rather than being tossed by it.
