# Dhyana

> *When holding becomes flowing, concentration has become meditation.*

---

## The Seventh Limb

Dhyana is what [Dharana](/knowledge/spirituality/yoga/eight-limbs-of-yoga/dharana) becomes when the effort falls away. Patanjali's definition (III.2): meditation is the unbroken flow of awareness toward the object — *tatra pratyaya ekatanata dhyanam*, one-pointedness extended in time, a single cognition stretched continuous. The classical image is oil poured from one vessel to another: not drops, however rapid, but an unbroken stream. In dharana, attention is placed and replaced; the practitioner is still doing something. In dhyana, the doing has dissolved into a current. Attention no longer returns to the object because it no longer leaves.

This is the limb that gave the contemplative world its most travelled word. Sanskrit *dhyana* became Pali *jhana*, the graded absorptions of early [Buddhism](/knowledge/religions-of-the-world/buddhism) — the Buddha's own map of deepening stillness runs directly through this territory. Carried to China it became *chan*, and carried on to Japan it became [Zen](/knowledge/spirituality/mysticism/zen): an entire tradition named, at four removes, after the seventh limb. That one word could seed so many lineages says something about what it names — not a technique belonging to any school, but a state the mind is capable of, discovered independently wherever the experiment is run.

---

## Practice

Dhyana cannot be performed, only permitted. There is no instruction for it beyond the instructions for dharana, sustained until sustaining becomes unnecessary — which is why [Meditation](/knowledge/spirituality/meditation) teachers say the state is not achieved but arrived at, the way sleep is. What can be described is its flavor: effort resolves into ease, the sense of a watcher managing the watching grows thin, time behaves strangely, and the object stands in awareness with a vividness that ordinary distracted perception never grants. The duality of observer and observed persists — that distinction is precisely what remains for the eighth limb to dissolve — but it has worn transparent.

---

## Place in the Sequence

Dhyana is the middle term of *samyama*, the inner triad. Behind it lies the effortful binding of [Dharana](/knowledge/spirituality/yoga/eight-limbs-of-yoga/dharana); ahead lies [Samadhi](/knowledge/spirituality/yoga/eight-limbs-of-yoga/samadhi), where even the flowing awareness ceases to be a separate thing and the object alone shines. The three are one movement at three depths — attention placed, attention flowing, attention dissolved.
