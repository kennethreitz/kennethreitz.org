# Niyama

> *Having stopped harming the world, begin keeping your own house.*

---

## The Second Limb

If [Yama](/knowledge/spirituality/yoga/eight-limbs-of-yoga/yama) governs the practitioner's conduct toward others, niyama governs conduct toward the self. The Yoga Sutras (II.32) name five observances — positive disciplines rather than restraints, the daily housekeeping of a life aimed at stillness. Where the yamas subtract harm, the niyamas add order. Together they form the ethical base of the eight limbs, and Patanjali spends more of the second chapter on them than on any of the limbs Westerners actually associate with yoga.

The last three niyamas are important enough that Patanjali gives them a collective name before the eight limbs are even introduced: *kriya yoga*, the yoga of action (II.1) — tapas, svadhyaya, and ishvara-pranidhana as a complete preliminary practice in themselves.

---

## The Five Observances

| Sanskrit | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| Shaucha | Purity, cleanliness |
| Santosha | Contentment |
| Tapas | Discipline, austerity, heat |
| Svadhyaya | Self-study, study of sacred texts |
| Ishvara-pranidhana | Surrender to the divine |

**Shaucha** is purity of body and environment, but Patanjali pushes it inward: purity of mind, the gradual clarification of the instrument. **Santosha** is contentment — and the sutra attached to it (II.42) is one of the most quietly radical in the text: from contentment, unsurpassed happiness. Not from attainment. From the decision to stop demanding that this moment be a different one. **Tapas** literally means heat — the friction of discipline, the willingness to endure difficulty on purpose, the same fire that drives any practice worth keeping. **Svadhyaya** is self-study in both senses: study of scripture, and study of the one doing the studying. A vault full of self-documentation is svadhyaya by another name. **Ishvara-pranidhana** is surrender to the divine — the devotional thread running through an otherwise technical text, and Patanjali's stated shortcut: samadhi is attained through surrender alone (II.45). It is the bhakti path folded inside the raja path.

---

## Place in the Sequence

With the world quieted by [Yama](/knowledge/spirituality/yoga/eight-limbs-of-yoga/yama) and the self ordered by niyama, the practitioner has built a life that will not sabotage the sitting. Only now does Patanjali turn to the body itself — [Asana](/knowledge/spirituality/yoga/eight-limbs-of-yoga/asana), the third limb, the seat from which the inward journey is made. The ordering repeats a pattern found across contemplative traditions, from monastic rules to the [Vedanta](/knowledge/spirituality/vedanta) prerequisites for inquiry: ethics before technique, character before power.
