# Karma

> *Action and consequence -- not punishment, not reward, just the way things work*

---

## Overview

Karma is one of the most misunderstood concepts in Western spiritual culture. It is not a cosmic scoreboard. It is not divine retribution. It is not "what goes around comes around" in the simplistic sense of a universe that punishes bad people and rewards good ones. Karma, in its original Sanskrit meaning, is simply action -- and the recognition that every action has consequences that ripple outward, shaping the conditions of future experience.

The concept appears across Hindu, Buddhist, and Jain traditions with different emphases, but the core insight is consistent: you are not separate from the web of cause and effect. What you do matters, not because a deity is keeping score, but because action shapes reality. Every choice creates conditions. Every condition shapes future choices. This is not mysticism. It is, if anything, the most grounded observation in all of spirituality.

---

## Karma Yoga

[Ram Dass](/knowledge/spirituality/ram-dass) taught karma yoga as one of the central paths -- action performed without attachment to results. The Bhagavad Gita puts it plainly: "You have the right to work, but never to the fruit of work." This does not mean indifference. It means caring about the quality of the action itself rather than obsessing over what it will get you. It means doing the work because the work needs doing, not because of the reward.

This is harder than it sounds. The ego wants credit, wants outcomes, wants to know that effort will be repaid. Karma yoga asks you to release that need -- not to stop working, but to stop keeping score. The action is the practice. The result belongs to the universe.

---

## Open Source as Karma Yoga

If karma yoga has a modern expression, Open Source software is one of its purest forms. Kenneth built `requests` -- a library that sixteen million machines use daily -- and gave it away. Not as a business strategy with deferred monetization. Not as resume padding. As a gift. The code is there because people need it, and someone who could write it did.

This is karma yoga in its most literal form: selfless action in service of the collective. The work is offered freely. What people do with it is their business. The fruits -- the downloads, the GitHub stars, the conference invitations -- came, but they were never the point. The point was that HTTP should be simple for humans, and someone had the skill to make it so.

The broader open source community operates on this principle whether it knows it or not. Thousands of developers contributing millions of hours to tools they will never be paid for, because the tools should exist and they can build them. This is not naive altruism. It is the recognition that work done without attachment to personal gain has a different quality -- a different karma -- than work done purely for profit.

---

## Karma and Healing

In the context of Kenneth's life, karma is not about deserving what happened. Trauma is not karmic punishment. Schizoaffective disorder is not the universe balancing a ledger. That interpretation is spiritual abuse dressed in Sanskrit.

What karma does offer is a framework for what comes after. The actions taken in response to suffering -- the documentation, the creative work, the sharing of experience, the building of tools that help others -- these are karmic choices. They shape what happens next. Not in a magical sense, but in the straightforward sense that how you respond to difficulty determines the conditions you create going forward.
