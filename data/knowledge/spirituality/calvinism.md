# Calvinism

> *Reformed theology — The sovereignty of God and the doctrines of grace*

---

## Overview

Calvinism is the theological system developed by John Calvin (1509-1564) and later systematized by his followers. It emphasizes God's absolute sovereignty, human depravity, and salvation by grace alone through faith alone. The system profoundly shaped Protestant Christianity and Western thought.

---

## Core Doctrines: TULIP

The five points of Calvinism, crystallized at the Synod of Dort (1618-1619):

### Total Depravity

Humanity is completely affected by sin. Not that humans are as evil as possible, but that sin touches every aspect of human nature — mind, will, emotions, body. Apart from grace, humans cannot choose God.

### Unconditional Election

God chooses who will be saved based on His will alone, not on foreseen faith or merit. Election is unconditional — not based on anything in the person, only on God's sovereign choice.

### Limited Atonement

Christ's atonement is sufficient for all but efficient only for the elect. Jesus died to actually save His people, not to make salvation merely possible. Also called "particular redemption."

### Irresistible Grace

When God calls the elect to salvation, they cannot ultimately resist. The Holy Spirit overcomes resistance and brings the person to faith. Grace is effectual, not just offered.

### Perseverance of the Saints

Those truly saved will persevere to the end. True believers cannot lose their salvation. Often called "eternal security" or "once saved, always saved" — though Calvinists emphasize that perseverance is evidence of genuine faith.

## Historical Development

### John Calvin (1509-1564)

French theologian and reformer who published *Institutes of the Christian Religion* (1536, expanded through 1559), the systematic theology that became the foundation for the Reformed tradition. Geneva, Switzerland was where Calvin established reformed church governance; Strasbourg was his period of exile and pastoral work.

### The Reformation Context

Calvin's theology emerged alongside Luther's but developed distinctive emphases. Where Luther focused on justification by faith alone, Calvin centered the sovereignty of God in salvation.

### Post-Calvin Development

The **Synod of Dort (1618-1619)** responded to Arminianism, crystallized the five points (TULIP), and defined orthodox Reformed theology. The **Puritans** — English Calvinists — brought the theology to America and profoundly influenced American culture.

## Key Concepts

### Sovereignty of God

God is absolutely sovereign over all creation, including salvation. Nothing happens outside His will and decree. This is the central axis around which Calvinism turns.

### Predestination

God has eternally decreed who will be saved and who will be lost. Double predestination covers both election to salvation and reprobation to damnation, though not all Calvinists emphasize the latter equally.

### Sola Scriptura

Scripture alone is the ultimate authority. The Bible is sufficient, clear, necessary, and authoritative for faith and practice.

### Covenant Theology

God relates to humanity through covenants: the Covenant of Works (Adam) and the Covenant of Grace (Christ). History is the unfolding of God's covenant plan.

### The Glory of God

The ultimate purpose of everything is God's glory. Salvation glorifies God's grace and mercy; even damnation glorifies His justice.

---

## Theological Framework

### God

Sovereign, omnipotent, omniscient, omnipresent. Immutable (unchanging), simple (not composed of parts), Trinitarian (Father, Son, Holy Spirit). Utterly transcendent yet intimately involved.

### Humanity

Created in God's image, fallen through Adam's sin, under God's wrath apart from grace, totally dependent on God for salvation.

### Salvation (Ordo Salutis)

The order of salvation proceeds through election (God's eternal choice), effectual calling (irresistible summons), regeneration (born again by the Spirit), conversion (faith and repentance), justification (declared righteous), sanctification (growing in holiness), and glorification (final perfection).

### Church and Sacraments

The church as covenant community with two sacraments (Baptism and Lord's Supper), governed through Presbyterian or Reformed polity with elders and deacons.

---

## Variations and Schools

### Reformed vs. Presbyterian

Reformed is the broader tradition including continental European churches. Presbyterian is the specific form with elder governance, primarily Scottish and American.

### Supralapsarianism vs. Infralapsarianism

These represent different views of the logical order of God's decrees. Supralapsarianism places election before the Fall in God's eternal decree; infralapsarianism (the more common view) places election after the Fall.

### Neo-Calvinism

A 19th-20th century movement led by Abraham Kuyper and Herman Bavinck, emphasizing cultural engagement, "every square inch" under Christ's lordship, and sphere sovereignty.

### New Calvinism

A contemporary resurgence through John Piper, Tim Keller, and others, emphasizing God's glory, passion for missions, cultural engagement, and complementarian gender roles.

---

## Impact on Culture

### Work Ethic

Max Weber's "Protestant work ethic" traces how Calvinist emphasis on calling and vocation shaped capitalism and modern work culture.

### Democracy and Rights

Calvinist resistance to tyranny influenced Dutch independence, the English Civil War, the American Revolution, and the development of federal systems and checks on power.

### Education

The emphasis on literacy (for reading Scripture) drove universal education and college founding (Harvard, Yale, Princeton) with a spirit of intellectual rigor.

### Art and Aesthetics

Iconoclasm removed images from worship, favoring simple, austere worship spaces and emphasizing Word over image — yet producing a rich literary tradition.

## Common Critiques

### Determinism

Critics argue Calvinism makes humans puppets, eliminates free will, and makes God the author of evil. Calvinists respond with compatibilism (divine sovereignty and human responsibility coexist), appeal to mystery (how they relate isn't fully comprehensible), and distinction (God decrees but isn't morally responsible for sin).

### Harshness

The doctrine of reprobation seems cruel — God condemning people to hell before they're born. Calvinists respond that all deserve hell (total depravity), grace is mercy not obligation, God's justice is perfect, and we cannot judge God's ways.

### Pastoral Problems

The system can lead to presumption (false assurance), despair (wondering if you're elect), or fatalism (why evangelize or obey?). Calvinists respond that election encourages assurance, obedience is evidence of election, and evangelism is the means God uses.

---

## Calvinism and Other Traditions

### vs. Arminianism

Arminianism emphasizes prevenient grace enabling choice, conditional election (God foreknows who will believe), universal atonement (Christ died for all), resistible grace, and possible loss of salvation. The central difference is the role of human will in salvation.

### vs. Catholicism

Catholic teaching holds to faith plus works for justification, church authority alongside Scripture, seven sacraments, and purgatory. Calvinism insists on faith alone (sola fide), Scripture alone (sola scriptura), two sacraments only, and no purgatory.

### vs. Lutheranism

They share justification by faith and Scripture authority but differ on predestination emphasis (Calvin is stronger), sacramental theology (Luther holds to real presence, Calvin to spiritual presence), and church-state relations.
