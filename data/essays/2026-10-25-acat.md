# acat

*October 2026*

`cat` prints files. `acat` plays them.

That's the whole idea. I built it today, and I've been smiling about it all afternoon.

## Why

I had a recording of an X Space I'd hosted, sitting in my home directory as an mp4. I wanted to listen to it. Not open an app, not drag it into a window, not wait for something with a library and an account to wake up. I just wanted to hear it, from the place I already was: my terminal.

So I typed what felt natural:

```
acat song.mp3
```

And then I made it work.<label for="sn-space" class="margin-toggle sidenote-number"></label><input type="checkbox" id="sn-space" class="margin-toggle"/><span class="sidenote">The first thing it ever played was that recorded Space. It's a good test. It's long, it's a video file, and I actually wanted to hear it.</span>

## What It Does

It behaves the way you'd expect something named after `cat` to behave. Give it a file, and it plays. Give it several, and it plays them one after another, the same way `cat` concatenates. Pipe something into it, and stdin works too.

Videos play their audio track: mp4, mov, mkv, webm, avi, and friends. That was the case I cared about most, and it's the one most players make awkward.

While it plays, you get a progress bar with elapsed and total time and the volume, and a single line of controls underneath:

```
[←/→] ∓5s  [a/d] ∓30s  [space] pause  [↑/↓] volume  [n] next  [p] prev  [q] quit
```

If you don't want any of that, `-q` gives you plain playback.

This is the [for humans](/knowledge/philosophy/for-humans) idea again: sensible defaults, obvious behavior, nothing to configure. The command you'd guess is the command that works.

## How It Works

The player is modeled on the one I wrote for [Interpretations](/essays/2026-04-01-interpretations_an_album_written_in_python), my album written in Python. It uses sounddevice and numpy for playback, with ffmpeg streaming the decoded audio in.

The part I'm happiest with is seeking. Rather than loading a whole file into memory, a seek just restarts ffmpeg at the new position. Memory stays flat, even for a 47-minute recording.<label for="sn-seek" class="margin-toggle sidenote-number"></label><input type="checkbox" id="sn-seek" class="margin-toggle"/><span class="sidenote">It's one of those choices that sounds lazy and turns out to be the right one. The simplest thing that could possibly work, working.</span>

When the fancy path isn't available, acat gets out of the way. If it's piped, running quiet, or there's no ffmpeg, it falls back to whatever your system already has: afplay, ffplay, mpv, paplay, or aplay. And if you need ffmpeg, it tells you how to install it, kindly, for the platform you're actually on.

## Getting It

The code is at [github.com/kennethreitz/acat](https://github.com/kennethreitz/acat), ISC licensed.

The name `acat` on PyPI was already taken by an unrelated chemistry toolkit, so the package is `acat-player`. The command is still `acat`:

```
uv tool install acat-player
```

It's at v0.1.1.

## That's It

It's a small tool. It does one thing. I like small tools that do one thing, and I like it when the thing they do is exactly what you'd guess from the name.

Audio and video in your terminal, for humans.
